

# 🎤 The Paradox of Choice: Does More Freedom Make Us Less Happy?

*As delivered by a 16-year-old student from Uttar Pradesh*

---

**[COLD OPEN]**

You get your Class 10 result.
You passed. Good marks.
And before you can even feel happy —
*someone* asks you:

**"Toh ab kya loge? Science? Commerce? Arts?"**

And suddenly, the happiness is gone.
Because now you have to *choose.*

I have been in that moment.
And I don't think I'm the only one in this room who has.

---

**[CONTRARIAN DECLARATION]**

Teachers, students — I want to say something today that sounds wrong when you first hear it:

**More choices do not make us more free.
They make us more afraid.**

---

**[THREE PILLARS]**

**First — every option you don't pick becomes a ghost.**

An American psychologist named Barry Schwartz did a study.
One group could choose from 6 options. Another from 24.
The people with *fewer* choices were happier with what they picked.
Why? Because when you have 24 options, you spend the rest of your life wondering about the 23 you didn't choose.

Sound familiar?
When we are choosing between Science and Commerce — we don't just choose a stream.
We carry the weight of every stream we *didn't* take.
Forever.

**Second — more options raise expectations. And expectations are happiness's enemy.**

Thirty years ago, a student in a small town in UP had maybe two career options.
He chose one. He worked hard. He felt proud.

Today — we have engineering, medicine, law, design, finance, content creation, civil services...
And somehow, with *all* of that choice — the anxiety has not gone down.
It has gone up.

*The more we can have, the more we feel we should have.*
And "should" is the most exhausting word in the English language.

**Third — the cost of too much choice is invisible.**

It shows up as a student who has every possible resource — YouTube, Unacademy, NCERT, coaching, tuition —
and sits staring at the ceiling, paralyzed.
Not lazy. *Overwhelmed.*

Studies show depression is rising fastest in the most "option-rich" environments.
We gave young people everything to choose from.
And forgot to give them the confidence to commit.

---

**[FULL CIRCLE — CALLBACK + CLOSING]**

I come back to that moment after Class 10.

What if someone had said — *"Not every path needs to be considered. You just need to walk one — fully."*

That is not limitation.
That is freedom of a deeper kind.

Because the students I most admire —
and the teachers I most respect —
are not the ones with the most options.

They are the ones who chose something,
and meant it completely.

---

**Choose less. Mean it more.**

---

*[Pause. Look at the room. Walk back.]*
