# Organizational Behavior: Sensemaking and Dynamic Capabilities

## Summary
In complex, ambiguous, and rapidly changing business environments, traditional rational decision-making models fail. Organizational behavior scholars like Karl Weick introduced **Sensemaking** to explain how individuals and teams collectively give meaning to uncertainty. When combined with strategic management's concept of **Dynamic Capabilities** (the ability to sense, seize, and reconfigure assets), sensemaking becomes the cognitive foundation that allows organizations to adapt, survive, and thrive in volatile markets.

## Key Academic Concepts

### 1. Sensemaking (Karl Weick)
Sensemaking is the ongoing, retrospective process by which people give meaning to their collective experiences. It is how order is created from chaos. It shifts the focus from "decision-making" (which assumes clear options) to "meaning-making" (which is required before options can even be formulated).

**The Seven Properties of Sensemaking:**
1.  **Identity Construction:** Sensemaking is shaped by who we are. How an organization interprets a crisis depends on its core identity.
2.  **Retrospective:** Meaning is made by looking backward at actions already taken. "How can I know what I think until I see what I say?"
3.  **Enactment:** Organizations create their own environments through their actions. They don't just react to the market; their actions shape the market.
4.  **Social:** It is a collaborative process involving interaction, negotiation, and shared narratives.
5.  **Ongoing:** Sensemaking never stops. It is a continuous flow of interpretation.
6.  **Extracted Cues:** We focus on specific, familiar details ("cues") to interpret the broader, chaotic environment.
7.  **Plausibility over Accuracy:** The goal of sensemaking in a crisis is not to be 100% accurate, but to create a "plausible" story that allows action to continue.

### 2. Dynamic Capabilities
Originating in strategic management (Teece, Pisano, Shuen), dynamic capabilities refer to a firm's ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competencies to address rapidly changing environments. 
*   **Sensing:** Identifying and assessing opportunities outside the company.
*   **Seizing:** Mobilizing resources to address opportunities and capture value.
*   **Transforming/Reconfiguring:** Continuous renewal of the organization.

## Deep Insights & Mental Models

*   **Action Precedes Understanding:** Weick argues that in highly ambiguous situations, you cannot think your way into a new way of acting; you must act your way into a new way of thinking. Action generates the data required for sensemaking.
*   **The "Map" is Just an Excuse to Walk:** In Weick's famous story of the Hungarian detachment lost in the Alps, the soldiers survived because they found a map. It gave them the confidence to act and keep moving. They later discovered the map was for the Pyrenees, not the Alps. The mental model is: In extreme uncertainty, any plausible plan is useful primarily because it sparks action, which generates new cues and updates the sensemaking process.
*   **Sensemaking as the Precursor to Dynamic Capabilities:** If dynamic capabilities are the "muscles" that allow a business to pivot, sensemaking is the "nervous system." A company cannot "sense and seize" an opportunity if its internal narrative cannot make sense of the market signals.

## Actionable Takeaways for the Book / Startup
1.  **Value Plausibility over Perfection:** In early-stage startups, do not wait for perfect data. Create a plausible hypothesis that justifies taking action. The action will generate the real data you need.
2.  **Foster Collective Sensemaking:** When facing a pivot or market disruption, leaders must facilitate conversations that allow the team to collectively interpret what is happening. A fragmented narrative destroys execution speed.
3.  **Design for "Enactment":** Understand that your startup's actions shape the competitive landscape. You are not just analyzing a static market; you are enacting a dynamic one through your product releases and marketing.
