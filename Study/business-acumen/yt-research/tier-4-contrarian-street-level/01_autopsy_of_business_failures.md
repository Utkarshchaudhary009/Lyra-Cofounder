# Autopsy of Business Failures: Contrarian Insights from the Graveyard

## Overview
This document synthesizes deep-dive startup post-mortems and VC breakdown videos. The core premise is that startups rarely die from starvation (lack of ideas or capital); they usually die from indigestion (focusing on the wrong things) or founder suicide (giving up).

## Key Videos Analyzed
1. **The Biggest Mistakes First-Time Founders Make - Michael Seibel (Y Combinator)**
2. **Why Investors Can’t Fix Your Company – Dalton Caldwell and Michael Seibel**
3. **The Decline of Dunzo! | Startup Case Study**

## Core Mental Models & Heuristics

### 1. Fake Progress vs. Real Progress
Many dead startups spent their final months optimizing "fake progress." Fake progress includes winning pitch competitions, optimizing the company logo, appearing in Forbes 30 under 30, and attending networking events.
> *"If you are writing code or talking to users, you are working on your startup. If you are doing anything else, you are playing house." — Michael Seibel*

**Heuristic:** Track only two metrics: Lines of code shipped that solve a problem, and number of users talked to (or dollars collected).

### 2. The "Investor Rescue" Fallacy
Founders often believe that raising the next round will solve their operational chaos. 
> *"Investors buy stock. They don't buy your problems. If you have fundamentally broken unit economics, a VC check is just a highly explosive accelerant to your own funeral." — Dalton Caldwell*

**Contrarian Insight:** Capital is a commodity. It magnifies the underlying physics of your business. If the unit economics (CAC to LTV) are negative, more money just means you lose money faster and more spectacularly (as seen in the Dunzo case study, where hyper-expansion broke the fragile delivery economics).

### 3. Premature Scaling (The Silent Killer)
Scaling before achieving strict Product-Market Fit (PMF).
*   **The Trap:** You get early traction from early adopters (who are forgiving) and you assume the mainstream market will behave the same. You hire 50 people. The mainstream market hates your buggy product, churn skyrockets, and your burn rate kills you in 4 months.
*   **The Street-Level Reality:** Do things that don't scale until it is physically painful to continue doing them manually.

### 4. Founder Divergence
Most startups don't actually run out of money; the founders just stop wanting to work on them. 
> *"A startup is not dead until the founders stop typing."*
Co-founder disputes over equity, direction, or effort account for a massive percentage of premature deaths. When the vision diverges, execution paralyzes.

## Actionable Takeaways for the Book
*   **Embrace the Cockroach Mode:** Before you can be a unicorn, you must be a cockroach—indestructible and capable of surviving nuclear winter.
*   **Kill your darlings early:** If a feature isn't moving the needle on retention, kill it. Focus on the core wedge.
*   **Profitability as the ultimate leverage:** The moment you don't need VC money to survive is the moment VCs will beg to give you money.
