# Haloalkanes & Haloarenes — Foundation Notes

## Why these notes exist

These notes are written from **first principles** for a student whose Class 11 foundation is weak. The goal is not to memorize a list of reactions. The goal is to build a chain of ideas so that the reactions become logical.

The first question we will answer is:

> **How do we convert an alcohol (R–OH) into an alkyl halide (R–X)?**

where X = Cl, Br or I.

## The master picture

```text
Alcohol
  R–OH
    │
    │ replace –OH by X
    ↓
Alkyl halide
  R–X

X = Cl, Br, I
```

### Core principle

The hard part is **not** bringing in the halogen. The hard part is getting the –OH group to leave in a useful way.

So keep this sentence in your head throughout the topic:

> **OH⁻ is a poor leaving group, so the reaction first activates or changes the –OH group into something that can leave more easily.**

---

## Important notation

- **R** = an alkyl group such as CH₃–, C₂H₅–, etc.
- **–OH** = hydroxyl group.
- **X** = halogen; in this chapter usually Cl, Br or I.
- **R–X** = alkyl halide / haloalkane.
- **1°, 2°, 3°** = primary, secondary, tertiary.

## Study rule

Do not memorize the reagent table before understanding the mechanism idea in Notes 01–04. Once the mechanism is clear, the reagents have a reason to exist.
