# 02 — Why –OH Is a Poor Leaving Group

## 1. First: what is a leaving group?

Imagine a general molecule:

\[
R-LG
\]

If LG can leave the carbon while taking the bonding electron pair with it, LG is called a **leaving group**.

For example, bromide can leave:

\[
R-Br \rightarrow R^+ + Br^-
\]

Bromine takes the bonding electrons and becomes Br⁻.

A good leaving group is one that can exist reasonably comfortably after leaving.

---

## 2. Why cannot alcohol simply lose OH?

An alcohol has:

\[
R-OH
\]

A tempting idea is:

\[
R-OH \rightarrow R^+ + OH^-
\]

But this is unfavorable because OH⁻ is a **strong base** and is not a good leaving group.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{OH^-\text{ is a poor leaving group}}
\]

This is the central obstacle in converting alcohols into haloalkanes.

---

## 3. The chemistry trick

Instead of forcing OH⁻ to leave directly, we **activate the –OH group first**.

A common way is to add a proton, H⁺.

The oxygen atom in the alcohol has lone-pair electrons and can accept H⁺:

\[
R-OH + H^+ \rightarrow R-OH_2^+
\]

Now the group attached to carbon can leave as:

\[
H_2O
\]

rather than as OH⁻.

And water is a much better leaving group.

---

## 4. Compare the two possibilities

### Bad route

\[
R-OH \rightarrow R^+ + OH^-
\]

Problem: OH⁻ is a poor leaving group.

### Better route

\[
R-OH + H^+ \rightarrow R-OH_2^+
\]

then:

\[
R-OH_2^+ \rightarrow R^+ + H_2O
\]

Now the departing species is neutral H₂O.

So the reaction has converted a **bad leaving group (OH⁻)** into a **good leaving group (H₂O)**.

---

## 5. The mental model

Do not memorize “OH is bad” as an isolated fact. Ask **why**:

> A leaving group should be able to survive after leaving. OH⁻ is relatively unstable as a leaving group because it is a strong base. Protonating the oxygen turns –OH into a group that can leave as neutral H₂O.

This idea will keep returning in organic chemistry.

---

## Quick check

**Q:** Why is –OH difficult to remove directly from an alcohol?

**A:** Because direct departure would produce OH⁻, which is a poor leaving group.

**Q:** How does H⁺ help?

**A:** It converts R–OH into R–OH₂⁺ so that water, H₂O, can leave.
