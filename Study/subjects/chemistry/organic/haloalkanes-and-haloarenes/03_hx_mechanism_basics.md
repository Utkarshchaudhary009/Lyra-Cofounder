# 03 — How HCl, HBr and HI Replace –OH

## 1. The general reaction

Hydrogen halides are:

\[
HCl,\ HBr,\ HI
\]

All have the general form:

\[
HX
\]

where X = Cl, Br or I.

The overall conversion is:

\[
\boxed{ROH + HX \rightarrow RX + H_2O}
\]

For example:

\[
CH_3CH_2OH + HCl \rightarrow CH_3CH_2Cl + H_2O
\]

---

## 2. What is HCl actually doing?

Think of HCl as giving two useful pieces:

\[
HCl \rightarrow H^+ + Cl^-
\]

This is a simplified ionic picture used to understand the reaction.

### H⁺ has one job

H⁺ protonates the alcohol oxygen:

\[
ROH + H^+ \rightarrow ROH_2^+
\]

This changes the leaving group situation.

Instead of trying to lose:

\[
OH^-
\]

we can lose:

\[
H_2O
\]

---

## 3. Water leaves

Under conditions where the mechanism permits carbocation formation:

\[
ROH_2^+ \rightarrow R^+ + H_2O
\]

The important point is not to memorize the equation alone. Understand the transformation:

\[
\boxed{R-OH \xrightarrow{H^+} R-OH_2^+ \rightarrow R^+ + H_2O}
\]

The OH group has effectively been converted into water, which is able to leave much more easily.

---

## 4. The halide ion finishes the job

The same HX provided the halide ion:

\[
Cl^-,\ Br^-,\ I^-
\]

If a carbocation is formed, the halide ion can bond to it:

\[
R^+ + X^- \rightarrow R-X
\]

For HCl:

\[
R^+ + Cl^- \rightarrow R-Cl
\]

For HBr:

\[
R^+ + Br^- \rightarrow R-Br
\]

For HI:

\[
R^+ + I^- \rightarrow R-I
\]

---

## 5. The whole story in one chain

```text
ROH + HX
   │
   ├── H⁺ activates the –OH
   ↓
ROH₂⁺
   │
   ├── H₂O can leave
   ↓
R⁺
   │
   ├── X⁻ attacks
   ↓
RX
```

So do not say:

> “HCl just swaps OH for Cl.”

A better mental model is:

> **H⁺ makes the –OH group leave as water, and Cl⁻/Br⁻/I⁻ supplies the new halogen.**

---

## 6. Important warning: not every alcohol + HX reaction has a free carbocation

This mechanism is a conceptual foundation, especially useful for reactions that proceed by an SN1-type pathway.

For primary alcohols, the reaction can instead proceed by an **SN2-type pathway**, where the halide ion attacks as the leaving group departs rather than waiting for a stable free carbocation.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{Do not memorize “alcohol + HX always makes a free carbocation.”}}
\]

The exact pathway depends on the alcohol structure and reaction conditions.

---

## Quick check

**Q:** What does H⁺ do to an alcohol in this reaction?

**A:** It protonates the oxygen and changes –OH into a form that can leave as H₂O.

**Q:** What does X⁻ ultimately become?

**A:** The halogen attached to carbon in the alkyl halide, RX.
