# 04 — Why the Reactivity Order Is 3° > 2° > 1°

## 1. First understand 1°, 2° and 3° alcohols

Look at the **carbon directly attached to –OH**.

### Primary (1°)

That carbon is attached to one other carbon:

\[
R-CH_2-OH
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3CH_2OH
\]

### Secondary (2°)

That carbon is attached to two other carbons:

\[
R_2CH-OH
\]

Example:

\[
CH_3-CH(OH)-CH_3
\]

### Tertiary (3°)

That carbon is attached to three other carbons:

\[
R_3C-OH
\]

Example:

\[
(CH_3)_3C-OH
\]

---

## 2. Why does the reaction care about this carbon?

In an SN1-type pathway, after the alcohol is protonated and water leaves, a carbocation can form:

\[
ROH_2^+ \rightarrow R^+ + H_2O
\]

Now compare the possible carbocations.

### Primary carbocation

\[
R-CH_2^+
\]

Very unstable.

### Secondary carbocation

\[
R_2CH^+
\]

More stable.

### Tertiary carbocation

\[
R_3C^+
\]

Most stable of these three.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ}
\]

for carbocation stability.

---

## 3. Why is a stable carbocation useful?

Formation of a carbocation is one of the important steps in an SN1 mechanism.

If a carbocation is relatively stable, it forms more easily.

So, under conditions where this pathway operates:

\[
3^\circ\text{ alcohol} > 2^\circ\text{ alcohol} > 1^\circ\text{ alcohol}
\]

in reactivity toward a given hydrogen halide.

This is why the NCERT statement says:

> **The order of reactivity of alcohols with a given haloacid is 3° > 2° > 1°.**

---

## 4. The cause-and-effect chain

Do not memorize the order alone. Remember:

\[
\boxed{
\text{3° alcohol}
\rightarrow
\text{more stable 3° carbocation}
\rightarrow
\text{easier ionization in SN1-type conditions}
\rightarrow
\text{faster reaction}
}
\]

The same logic explains why tertiary alcohols can react readily with concentrated HCl at room temperature.

---

## 5. Important limitation

The order is a useful statement for the conditions and reaction type being discussed. It should not be turned into the false rule that **every mechanism makes every primary alcohol slower than every secondary reaction under every condition**.

Primary alcohols can often react by a different mechanism, especially **SN2**, where a free carbocation is not formed.

---

## Quick check

**Q:** Which is more stable: a tertiary or primary carbocation?

**A:** Tertiary.

**Q:** What is the common reactivity order of alcohols with a given HX in this context?

**A:**

\[
\boxed{3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ}
\]
