# 06 — Thionyl Chloride (SOCl₂): Why It Is Preferred

## 1. The reagent

Thionyl chloride is:

\[
\boxed{SOCl_2}
\]

It is a very useful reagent for converting an alcohol into an alkyl chloride.

\[
\boxed{ROH + SOCl_2 \rightarrow RCl + SO_2 + HCl}
\]

The exact conditions may vary, but the important products for this basic concept are:

- **RCl** — desired alkyl chloride
- **SO₂** — sulfur dioxide gas
- **HCl** — hydrogen chloride gas

---

## 2. Why does NCERT prefer SOCl₂?

The key observation is that both by-products are gases under the reaction conditions:

\[
SO_2\uparrow
\]

\[
HCl\uparrow
\]

The gases can escape from the reaction mixture.

So the unwanted products are physically removed instead of remaining mixed with the alkyl chloride.

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{SOCl₂ gives a convenient route to relatively pure alkyl chloride.}}
\]

---

## 3. Think about the reaction like a separation problem

Suppose a reaction produces:

\[
\text{desired product} + \text{unwanted gas} + \text{unwanted gas}
\]

If the gases leave, the desired product is easier to isolate.

That is exactly the practical advantage highlighted by NCERT.

---

## 4. Compare with a reaction that gives a liquid by-product

If a reaction gives unwanted liquid products mixed with your desired liquid product, extra purification may be needed.

With SOCl₂:

\[
ROH + SOCl_2 \rightarrow RCl + SO_2\uparrow + HCl\uparrow
\]

Two unwanted products escape as gases.

That makes purification easier and is why SOCl₂ is preferred for this purpose.

---

## 5. Important correction to the common typo

The gaseous sulfur-containing product is:

\[
\boxed{SO_2}
\]

**not SO**.

So remember the reaction as:

\[
\boxed{ROH + SOCl_2 \rightarrow RCl + SO_2 + HCl}
\]

---

## Quick check

**Q:** What are the gaseous by-products?

**A:** SO₂ and HCl.

**Q:** Why is SOCl₂ preferred?

**A:** Because the unwanted products SO₂ and HCl escape as gases, making the alkyl chloride easier to obtain in pure form.
