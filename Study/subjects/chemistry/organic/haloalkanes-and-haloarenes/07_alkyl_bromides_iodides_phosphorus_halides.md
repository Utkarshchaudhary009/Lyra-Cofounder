# 07 — Preparing Alkyl Bromides, Alkyl Iodides and Phosphorus Halides

## 1. Preparing alkyl bromides

To convert an alcohol into an alkyl bromide:

\[
ROH \rightarrow RBr
\]

A common reagent is concentrated HBr.

\[
\boxed{ROH + HBr \rightarrow RBr + H_2O}
\]

NCERT specifically notes the use of **48% HBr** as a constant-boiling mixture for preparing alkyl bromides.

The important idea for now:

> **HBr supplies the H⁺ that helps deal with the –OH group and Br⁻ that becomes the bromine in R–Br.**

---

## 2. Preparing alkyl iodides

To convert an alcohol into an alkyl iodide:

\[
ROH \rightarrow RI
\]

Good yields can be obtained by heating alcohols with:

\[
\boxed{NaI\text{ or }KI}
\]

in:

\[
\boxed{95\%\ H_3PO_4}
\]

So a useful memory line is:

\[
\boxed{ROH + NaI/KI \xrightarrow[\text{heat}]{95\%\ H_3PO_4} RI}
\]

Here **H₃PO₄ is orthophosphoric acid**.

---

## 3. Why use iodide salts?

NaI and KI are sources of iodide ion:

\[
NaI \rightarrow Na^+ + I^-
\]

\[
KI \rightarrow K^+ + I^-
\]

The important reactive species for the substitution is the iodide ion:

\[
\boxed{I^-}
\]

which ultimately provides the iodine atom in R–I.

---

## 4. Phosphorus tribromide and triiodide

Another way to convert alcohols is with phosphorus halides such as:

\[
PBr_3
\]

and

\[
PI_3
\]

They are usually **generated in situ**.

---

## 5. What does “in situ” mean?

This phrase simply means:

> **Produced in the same reaction mixture where it is going to be used.**

You do not necessarily prepare a separate bottle of the reagent first.

Instead:

\[
\text{starting materials} \rightarrow \text{reagent formed in the mixture} \rightarrow \text{used there}
\]

---

## 6. How PBr₃ is generated

Red phosphorus reacts with bromine:

\[
\boxed{\text{red phosphorus} + Br_2 \rightarrow PBr_3}
\]

The PBr₃ formed in the mixture can react with alcohol to make alkyl bromide.

A simplified representation is:

\[
ROH \xrightarrow{PBr_3} RBr
\]

---

## 7. How PI₃ is generated

Red phosphorus reacts with iodine:

\[
\boxed{\text{red phosphorus} + I_2 \rightarrow PI_3}
\]

The PI₃ formed in the reaction mixture can then convert alcohol into alkyl iodide:

\[
ROH \xrightarrow{PI_3} RI
\]

The exam-level takeaway is the preparation method:

\[
\boxed{\text{red P + Br}_2 \rightarrow PBr_3}
\]

\[
\boxed{\text{red P + I}_2 \rightarrow PI_3}
\]

---

## 8. Reagent map

| Desired product | Useful reagent/method |
|---|---|
| R–Cl | HCl, PCl₃/PCl₅, SOCl₂ |
| R–Br | HBr, PBr₃ |
| R–I | NaI/KI + 95% H₃PO₄ (heat), PI₃ |

---

## Quick check

**Q:** What does “in situ” mean?

**A:** The reagent is generated inside the reaction mixture where it is used.

**Q:** How is PBr₃ generated for this purpose?

**A:** From red phosphorus and Br₂.

**Q:** How can alcohols give good yields of R–I according to the NCERT statement?

**A:** Heat the alcohol with NaI or KI in 95% orthophosphoric acid.
