**Section A (MCQs):**
1. (c) Milk — it's a colloid, not a true solution.
2. (b) m = (5.85 × 1000)/(58.5 × 500) = 0.2 m
3. (c) Sparingly soluble — high K_H means low solubility.

7. (d) AlCl₃ gives 4 ions, highest i value.

9. (b) Minimum boiling azeotrope.
10. (c) Al₂(SO₄)₃ → 2Al³⁺ + 3SO₄²⁻, total 5 ions, i = 5.

**Section B:**
**Q11:** For a non-volatile solute, P_s = χ_solvent × P°. RLVP = (P° − P_s)/P° = χ_solute.

**Q12:** Assume 100 g solution: 30 g CCl₄, 70 g C₆H₆.
n_CCl₄ = 30/154 = 0.195, n_C₆H₆ = 70/78 = 0.897
χ_benzene = 0.897/(0.897 + 0.195) = 0.821

**Q13:** Molarity = moles/L of solution (temp dependent). Molality = moles/kg solvent (temp independent). Molality is preferred for colligative properties since it doesn't change with temperature.

**Q14:** A mixture that boils at constant composition with identical composition in liquid and vapour phases. Example: ethanol-water (95.6%, BP 78.1°C) — minimum boiling azeotrope.

**Q15:** The excess pressure required to prevent solvent flow across a semipermeable membrane. π = CRT = (n/V)RT.

**Q16:** m = (5 × 1000)/(342 × 100) = 5000/34200 = 0.1462 m
K_f = ΔT_f/m = 0.28/0.1462 = 1.915 ≈ 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹ (close to standard value)

**Q17:** i = Observed colligative property / Expected colligative property.
K₄[Fe(CN)₆] → 4K⁺ + [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ gives 5 ions, i = 5.

**Q18:** Expected π = CRT = 0.1 × 0.0821 × 300 = 2.463 atm
i = π_obs/π_expected = 2.52/2.463 = 1.023

**Section C:**
**Q19:** (a) Solid solubility in liquid generally increases with temperature (endothermic dissolution). (b) Gas solubility decreases with increasing temperature (Henry's law — dissolution of gas is exothermic).

**Q20:** P_s = χ_solvent × P° = (1 − χ_solute)P°. Therefore (P° − P_s)/P° = χ_solute. [Standard derivation with diagram]

**Q21:** n_glucose = 5/180 = 0.0278, n_water = 100/18 = 5.556
χ_solute = 0.0278/5.5838 = 0.00498
RLVP = χ_solute = 0.00498
P_s = P°(1 − χ_solute) = 23.8 × 0.995 = 23.68 mm Hg

**Q22:** Non-ideal solutions deviate from Raoult's law.
Positive deviation: A-A and B-B interactions > A-B interactions. ΔH > 0, ΔV > 0. e.g., ethanol + water.
Negative deviation: A-B interactions > A-A and B-B interactions. ΔH < 0, ΔV < 0. e.g., acetone + chloroform.

**Q23:** m = (0.5 × 1000)/(100 × 50) = 500/5000 = 0.1 m
ΔT_b = K_b × m = 0.52 × 0.1 = 0.052°C
BP = 100 + 0.052 = 100.052°C

**Q24:** i = ΔT_f/(K_f × m) = 0.432/(1.86 × 0.1) = 2.323
K₂SO₄ → n = 3: α = (2.323 − 1)/(3 − 1) = 1.323/2 = 0.662 = 66.2%

**Q25:** Modified formulas:
RLVP: (P° − P_s)/P° = i × χ_solute
ΔT_b = i × K_b × m
ΔT_f = i × K_f × m
π = i × C × R × T

**Q26:** π_needed = 7.4 atm at T = 310 K
For NaCl: π_NaCl = iCRT = 2 × 0.1 × 0.0821 × 310 = 5.09 atm
Remaining π needed = 7.4 − 5.09 = 2.31 atm
C_glucose = π/(RT) = 2.31/(0.0821 × 310) = 2.31/25.45 = 0.0908 M
Need ~0.091 M glucose.

**Section D:**
**Q27:** (i) NaCl has higher π (i=2) vs glucose (i=1). (ii) NaCl has higher BP (more particles). (iii) i = 1 + 0.8 = 1.8. (iv) NaCl has lower FP (more depression).

**Q28:** (a) <br>
(A) Both true, R explains A. (b) <br>
(A) Both true, R explains A.

**Section E:**
**Q29:** (a) Standard derivation of vapour phase composition using p_A = χ_A P°_A, p_B = χ_B P°_B, y_A = p_A/(p_A + p_B).
(b) P_total = χ_A × 400 + (1 − χ_A) × 200 = 300

**Q30:** (a) Properties depending only on number of particles: RLVP, ΔT_b, ΔT_f, π.
(b) Beckmann's method or Rast method — standard experimental procedure with Beckmann thermometer, freezing point apparatus.
(c) m = 0.40/5.12 = 0.0781 m, MM = (1 × 1000)/(0.0781 × 50) = 1000/3.905 = 256 g/mol

(b) m = (2×1000)/(74.5×100) = 0.2685 m. Expected ΔT_f = 1.86×0.2685 = 0.5°C. i = 0.96/0.5 = 1.92. α = (1.92−1)/1 = 0.92 = 92%.
