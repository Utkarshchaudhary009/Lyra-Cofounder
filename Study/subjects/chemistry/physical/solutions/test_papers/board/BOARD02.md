**Section B:**
**Q11:** True solution: particle size < 1 nm, no Tyndall effect. Colloid: 1–1000 nm, shows Tyndall effect.

**Q12:** M = (4/40)/0.25 = 0.1/0.25 = 0.4 M

**Q13:** Gas solubility decreases with increasing temperature. Governed by Henry's law: p = K_H × χ. The dissolution is exothermic, so higher T shifts equilibrium toward gas phase.

**Q14:** Vapour pressure decreases. The non-volatile solute occupies surface area, reducing the number of solvent molecules that can escape into vapour phase. P_s = χ_solvent × P°.

**Q15:** Cryoscopic constant K_f is the depression in freezing point when 1 mole of non-volatile solute is dissolved in 1 kg of solvent. K_f (water) = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹.

**Q16:** Expected ΔT_f = 5.12 × 0.01 = 0.0512°C. Observed ΔT_f = 5.50 − 5.474 = 0.026°C. i = 0.026/0.0512 = 0.508 ≈ 0.5.

**Q17:** Isotonic solutions have the same osmotic pressure at the same temperature. Example: 0.9% NaCl (saline) and blood plasma.

**Q18:** Expected π = CRT = 0.1 × 0.0821 × 300 = 2.463 atm. i = 5.1/2.463 = 2.07.

**Section C:**
**Q19:** Standard 3×3 table of gas/liquid/solid solute and solvent combinations with examples.

**Q20:** n_ethanol = 46/46 = 1; n_water = 36/18 = 2. χ_ethanol = 1/3 = 0.333. χ_water = 2/3 = 0.667.

**Q21:** m = 0.40/5.12 = 0.0781. MM = (1 × 1000)/(0.0781 × 50) = 1000/3.905 = 256 g/mol.

**Q22:** Standard distinction with composition, BP behaviour, and examples.

**Q23:** RLVP = (640 − 600)/640 = 40/640 = 0.0625.
n_benzene = 78/78 = 1 mol. RLVP = n_solute/(n_solute + n_benzene) ≈ n_solute/n_benzene (dilute).
0.0625 = n_solute/1 → n_solute = 0.0625.
MM = 2/0.0625 = 32 g/mol.

Alternatively: (P° − P_s)/P_s = n_solute/n_solvent → 40/600 = n_solute/1 → n_solute = 0.0667. MM = 2/0.0667 = 30 g/mol.

**Q24:** Henry's law: p = K_H × χ at constant T.
Applications: (i) Carbonated beverages (CO₂ dissolved under pressure), (ii) Deep-sea diving (N₂ solubility at high pressure → decompression sickness).

**Q25:** π = (W/MM)RT/V → MM = WRT/(πV).
Preferred for polymers because even at low concentrations, π gives measurable values (unlike ΔT_f or ΔT_b which are too small to measure accurately for high MM compounds).

**Q26:** (i) HA → H⁺ + A⁻, n = 2; i = 1 + (2−1)×0.05 = 1.05.
(ii) ΔT_f = 1.05 × 1.86 × 0.2 = 0.391°C.
(iii) π = 1.05 × 0.2 × 0.0821 × 300 = 5.17 atm.
