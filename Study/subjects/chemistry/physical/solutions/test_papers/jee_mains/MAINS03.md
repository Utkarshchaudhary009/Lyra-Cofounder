## Section A: Multiple Choice Questions (20 × 4 = 80 marks)

**Q1.** The mole fraction of water in 20% (w/w) aqueous NaOH solution is approximately:
(1) 0.90   (2) 0.80   (3) 0.95   (4) 0.85

**Q2.** The vapour pressure of pure liquid A at a certain temperature is 300 mm Hg. When 1 mole of a non-volatile solute is dissolved in 4 moles of A, the vapour pressure of the solution will be:
(1) 240 mm   (2) 300 mm   (3) 200 mm   (4) 60 mm

**Q3.** The osmotic pressure of 0.2 M solution of a non-electrolyte at 27°C is:
(1) 4.926 atm   (2) 2.463 atm   (3) 0.4926 atm   (4) 0.2463 atm

**Q4.** Which of the following solutions has the highest boiling point?<br>
(1) 0.1 M glucose   (2) 0.1 M NaCl   (3) 0.1 M CaCl₂   (4) 0.1 M AlCl₃

**Q5.** The molality of a solution containing 5.85 g of NaCl in 2000 g of water is:
(1) 0.05 m   (2) 0.5 m   (3) 0.005 m   (4) 5 m

**Q6.** For a 1:1 electrolyte like NaCl, which of the following correctly represents i?<br>
(1) i = 1 − α   (2) i = 1 + α   (3) i = 1 + 2α   (4) i = α

**Q7.** The ratio of ΔT_f for a 0.1 m solution of K₂SO₄ to that of a 0.1 m solution of glucose (both aqueous) is:
(1) 1:1   (2) 3:1   (3) 1:3   (4) 2:1

**Q8.** A 0.1 m solution of acetic acid in benzene shows ΔT_f = 0.0256°C. K_f (benzene) = 5.12. The van't Hoff factor is:
(1) 0.5   (2) 1.0   (3) 1.5   (4) 2.0

**Q9.** Which of the following will have the highest vapour pressure at a given temperature?<br>
(1) 0.1 M glucose   (2) 0.1 M NaCl   (3) 0.1 M CaCl₂   (4) Pure water

**Q10.** The total vapour pressure of a binary solution of two volatile liquids A and B is given by P = χ_A P°_A + χ_B P°_B. This implies the solution is:
(1) Ideal   (2) Non-ideal with positive deviation
(3) Non-ideal with negative deviation   (4) Azeotropic

**Q11.** The Henry's law constant for a gas is 2.0 × 10⁵ atm at 25°C. What pressure is needed to achieve a mole fraction of 5 × 10⁻⁵?<br>
(1) 5 atm   (2) 10 atm   (3) 1 atm   (4) 0.5 atm

**Q12.** The degree of dissociation of AlCl₃ in 0.1 m solution (ΔT_f = 0.651°C, K_f = 1.86) is:
(1) 0.5   (2) 0.6   (3) 0.7   (4) 0.8

**Q13.** Two solutions of the same solute have concentrations in the ratio 1:2. The ratio of their osmotic pressures at the same temperature is:
(1) 1:1   (2) 2:1   (3) 1:2   (4) 4:1

**Q14.** A solution of 6 g of urea (MM = 60) in 90 g of water has a vapour pressure of 23.33 mm Hg at 25°C. The vapour pressure of pure water at this temperature is:
(1) 23.8 mm   (2) 24.0 mm   (3) 22.0 mm   (4) 25.0 mm

**Q15.** Assertion <br>
(A): When the solute undergoes association, the observed molar mass is greater than the actual molar mass.
Reason (R): For association, the van't Hoff factor i < 1.
(1) Both true, R explains A   (2) Both true, R doesn't explain A
(3) A true, R false   (4) A false, R true

**Q16.** The molarity of 0.1 m aqueous urea solution (d = 1 g/mL) will be:
(1) 0.1 M   (2) < 0.1 M   (3) > 0.1 M   (4) Cannot be determined

**Q17.** 0.1 m NaCl and 0.1 m glucose solutions are separated by a semipermeable membrane. Which direction will solvent flow?<br>
(1) Glucose → NaCl   (2) NaCl → Glucose   (3) No flow   (4) Both directions

**Q18.** The ebullioscopic constant depends on:
(1) Nature of solute   (2) Nature of solvent
(3) Concentration of solution   (4) Temperature only

**Q19.** The vapour pressure of pure A (P°_A = 200 mm) and pure B (P°_B = 100 mm) form an ideal solution. An equimolar mixture is taken in a closed container. The mole fraction of A in the vapour phase at equilibrium is:
(1) 0.33   (2) 0.50   (3) 0.67   (4) 0.75

**Q20.** A 0.1 M solution of a weak acid has pH = 3. Its van't Hoff factor i at this concentration is approximately:
(1) 1.0   (2) 1.001   (3) 1.01   (4) 1.1
