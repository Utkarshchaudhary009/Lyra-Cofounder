### Section D: Long Answer (4 marks × 1 = 4 marks)

**Q10.** Derive the relation for elevation of boiling point: ΔT_b = K_b × m. A solution of 0.5 g of a substance in 50 g of water gives a boiling point elevation of 0.052°C. Calculate the molar mass of the substance. (K_b = 0.52 K kg mol⁻¹)

---

<summary>📝 Answer Key</summary>

**Q1:** < 1 nm (or < 10⁻⁹ m)

**Q2:** At constant temperature, the solubility of a gas in a liquid is directly proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid. (p = K_H × x)

**Q3:** M = (1000 × m × d) / (1000 + m × MM_solute)

**Q4:** K_b is the elevation in boiling point when 1 mole of non-volatile solute is dissolved in 1 kg of solvent.

**Q5:**
- n_water = 36/18 = 2 mol
- n_ethanol = 46/46 = 1 mol
- n_water > n_ethanol, both liquids → **Water = solvent, Ethanol = solute**

**Q6:**
- m = (W_solute × 1000) / (MM × W_solvent)
- m = (10 × 1000) / (58.5 × 250) = 10000/14625 = **0.684 m**

**Q7:**
- RLVP = (P° − P_s)/P° = (23.8 − 23.2)/23.8 = 0.6/23.8 = **0.0252**

**Q8:**
- 5% w/w means 5 g glucose in 100 g solution
- Volume = mass/density = 100/1.02 = 98.04 mL = 0.09804 L
- Moles glucose = 5/180 = 0.0278 mol
- M = 0.0278/0.09804 = **0.283 M**

**Q9:**
- ΔT_b = K_b × m → m = 0.15/0.52 = 0.2885 m
- m = (W_solute × 1000) / (MM × W_solvent)
- 0.2885 = (3 × 1000) / (MM × 100)
- MM = 3000/(0.2885 × 100) = 3000/28.85 = **104 g/mol**

**Q10:**
Derivation: ΔT_b ∝ m → ΔT_b = K_b × m (standard derivation with PV = nRT analogy or VP-T graph).

Given: ΔT_b = 0.052°C, K_b = 0.52, W_solute = 0.5 g, W_solvent = 50 g

- m = ΔT_b/K_b = 0.052/0.52 = 0.1 m
- MM = (W_solute × 1000)/(m × W_solvent) = (0.5 × 1000)/(0.1 × 50) = 500/5 = **100 g/mol**
