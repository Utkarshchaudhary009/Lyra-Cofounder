### Section D: Long Answer (4 marks × 1 = 4 marks)

**Q10.** Derive the relation ΔT_f = K_f × m. 1.5 g of a substance dissolved in 75 g of water gave a freezing point depression of 0.372°C. Calculate the molar mass of the substance. (K_f for water = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹)

---

<details>
<summary>📝 Answer Key</summary>

**Q1:** Mole fraction (χ) is the ratio of number of moles of a component to the total number of moles in the solution.

**Q2:** mol/kg (or mol kg⁻¹)

**Q3:** K_H is inversely proportional to solubility. Higher K_H means lower solubility.

**Q4:** K_f for water = 1.86 K kg mol⁻¹

**Q5:**
- n_NaCl = 5.85/58.5 = 0.1 mol
- n_water = 90/18 = 5 mol
- χ_NaCl = 0.1/(0.1 + 5) = 0.1/5.1 = **0.0196**

**Q6:**
- Henry's law: p = K_H × χ → χ = p/K_H
- χ = 0.21/(4.34 × 10⁴) = **4.84 × 10⁻⁶**

**Q7:**
- ΔT_f = K_f × m → K_f = ΔT_f/m = 0.186/0.1 = **1.86 K kg mol⁻¹** ✓

**Q8:**
- Assume 1 L of solution: n_glucose = 2 mol; mass_glucose = 2×180 = 360 g
- Mass of solution = V × d = 1000 × 1.15 = 1150 g
- Mass of solvent = 1150 − 360 = 790 g = 0.79 kg
- m = 2/0.79 = **2.53 m**

**Q9:**
- ΔT_f = 5.5 − 4.9 = 0.6°C
- m = ΔT_f/K_f = 0.6/5.12 = 0.1172 m
- MM = (W_solute × 1000)/(m × W_solvent) = (2 × 1000)/(0.1172 × 100) = 2000/11.72 = **170.6 g/mol**

**Q10:**
Derivation: ΔT_f ∝ m → ΔT_f = K_f × m (standard derivation).

Given: ΔT_f = 0.372°C, K_f = 1.86, W_solute = 1.5 g, W_solvent = 75 g

- m = ΔT_f/K_f = 0.372/1.86 = 0.2 m
- MM = (W_solute × 1000)/(m × W_solvent) = (1.5 × 1000)/(0.2 × 75) = 1500/15 = **100 g/mol**

</details>
