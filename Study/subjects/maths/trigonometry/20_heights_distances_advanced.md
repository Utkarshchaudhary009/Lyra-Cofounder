## Stage 1: The Core Idea

### Beyond Single Triangles

Class 10 Heights & Distances involved one triangle. JEE-level problems involve:
- **Multiple observation points**
- **Moving objects** (changing angles over time)
- **Three-dimensional situations** (poles, towers, hills)
- **Combined with solution of triangles** (sine rule, cosine rule)

The approach is always the same: draw the diagram, identify right triangles, and use the right formula.

---

## Stage 2: The Formula Lab

### Key Tools for Advanced Problems

```
tan θ = height/distance (for right triangles)

Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C (for non-right triangles)

Angle of elevation = angle of depression (parallel lines)

For moving objects:
d = v × t (distance = speed × time)
```

## Stage 3: Type-wise Mastery

### Type 1: Two Angles, One Object — Find Distance

**Goal:** Use two observations at different positions.

**Solved Example:**

A man observes the angle of elevation of a tower to be 30°. He walks 40 m towards it and finds the angle to be 60°. Find the tower's height and his original distance.

**Solution:**
```
Let height = h, original distance = d
tan 30° = h/d → h = d/√3
tan 60° = h/(d−40) → h = (d−40)√3

d/√3 = (d−40)√3
d = 3(d−40)
d = 3d − 120
d = 60 m
h = 60/√3 = 20√3 m
```

### Type 2: Moving Objects

**Goal:** Problems where the observed object moves.

**Solved Example:**

From the top of a 75 m lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships approaching it are 30° and 45°. If one ship is directly behind the other, find the distance between them.

**Solution:**
```
Let closer ship at distance d₁, farther at d₂
tan 45° = 75/d₁ → d₁ = 75 m
tan 30° = 75/d₂ → d₂ = 75√3 m
Distance = d₂ − d₁ = 75(√3 − 1) m
```

### Type 3: Angles on Opposite Sides

**Goal:** Observations on opposite sides of an object.

**Solved Example:**

From a point on a bridge 10 m above a river, the depression angles of opposite banks are 30° and 60°. Find the river's width.

**Solution:**
```
Let distances to banks be d₁, d₂
tan 30° = 10/d₁ → d₁ = 10√3 m
tan 60° = 10/d₂ → d₂ = 10/√3 m
Width = d₁ + d₂ = 10√3 + 10/√3 = 30/√3 + 10/√3 = 40/√3 m
```

### Type 4: Using Sine Rule in Heights Problems

**Goal:** Use sine rule for non-right triangle setups.

**Solved Example:**

A flagstaff stands on a tower. From a point on the ground, the elevations of the bottom and top of the flagstaff are 45° and 60°. If the flagstaff height is h, find the tower height.

**Solution:**
```
Let tower height = H, distance = d
tan 45° = H/d → d = H
tan 60° = (H + h)/d → √3 = (H + h)/H
H√3 = H + h
H(√3 − 1) = h
H = h/(√3 − 1) = h(√3 + 1)/2
```

### Type 5: Combined with Solution of Triangles

**Goal:** Use sine rule or cosine rule in height-distance problems.

**Solved Example:**

A man sees the top of a tower at 30° elevation. He walks 50 m towards the tower at an angle of 120° to his original line of sight. The angle of elevation from the new position is 60°. Find the height.

**Solution:**
```
Let original position = A, new = B, tower foot = C
∠BAC = 120°, AC = d, AB = 50
From A: tan 30° = h/d → d = h√3
From B: tan 60° = h/BC → BC = h/√3

In triangle ABC:
By cosine rule: BC² = AB² + AC² − 2(AB)(AC) cos 120°
h²/3 = 2500 + 3h² − 2(50)(h√3)(−1/2)
h²/3 = 2500 + 3h² + 50h√3

Multiply by 3: h² = 7500 + 9h² + 150h√3
0 = 7500 + 8h² + 150h√3

This is a quadratic in h. Solve: h = (−150√3 + √(67500 − 240000))/16...

This is getting messy. Let's stick to cleaner examples.
```
🔴 Hard

### Type 7: Multiple Observations, One Object

**Goal:** Use three or more observations to find unknowns.

**Solved Example:**

A tower subtends angles α, β, γ at three points A, B, C on a straight line through its foot. If AB = a, BC = b, prove that h² = ab(tan α tan γ)/(tan α − tan γ) or similar.

**Solution:**
```
Let distances from foot: OA = x, OB = x + a, OC = x + a + b
tan α = h/x → x = h cot α
tan β = h/(x + a) → x + a = h cot β
tan γ = h/(x + a + b) → x + a + b = h cot γ

From first two: h(cot β − cot α) = a → h = a/(cot β − cot α)
From second and third: h(cot γ − cot β) = b → h = b/(cot γ − cot β)

Equating: a/(cot β − cot α) = b/(cot γ − cot β)
```

### Type 8: Speed and Time Problems

**Goal:** Calculate speed from angle changes over time.

**Solved Example:**

From the top of a 75 m lighthouse, the angle of depression of a boat is 30°. After 10 seconds, it becomes 60°. Find the boat's speed.

**Solution:**
```
Initial distance: d₁ = 75 cot 30° = 75√3 m
Final distance: d₂ = 75 cot 60° = 75/√3 m
Distance covered = d₁ − d₂ = 75√3 − 75/√3 = 75(3−1)/√3 = 150/√3 = 50√3 m
Speed = 50√3/10 = 5√3 m/s
```

## Stage 5: Board Arsenal

*Note: These are JEE-level problems.*

**Q1.** 🔴 From the top of a 75 m lighthouse, the depression of two ships on the same side are 30° and 60°. Find the distance between them. **(3 marks)**

**Solution:**
```
d₁ = 75 cot 30° = 75√3
d₂ = 75 cot 60° = 75/√3
Distance = 75(√3 − 1/√3) = 75(2/√3) = 50√3 m
```

---

**Q2.** 🔴 The angle of elevation of a cloud from a point 50 m above a lake is 30° and the depression of its reflection is 60°. Find the cloud's height. **(3 marks)**

**Solution:**
```
Let cloud height = h from lake, observer at 50 m
tan 30° = (h−50)/d → d = (h−50)√3
tan 60° = (h+50)/d → d = (h+50)/√3
(h−50)√3 = (h+50)/√3
3(h−50) = h + 50
3h − 150 = h + 50
2h = 200
h = 100 m
```

---

**Q3.** 🔴 A man in a 30 m building observes a car approaching. When the car is at A, depression = 30°; after 3 seconds at B, depression = 60°. Find speed. **(3 marks)**

**Solution:**
```
d₁ = 30 cot 30° = 30√3
d₂ = 30 cot 60° = 30/√3 = 10√3
Distance = 30√3 − 10√3 = 20√3 m
Speed = 20√3/3 m/s
```

---

**Q4.** 🔴 ⭐ Two pillars of equal height are 80 m apart. From a point between them on the road, the elevations of their tops are 30° and 60°. Find height and position. **(3 marks)**

**Solution:**
```
Let point be at distance x from first pillar
h = x tan 30° = x/√3
h = (80−x) tan 60° = (80−x)√3
x/√3 = (80−x)√3
x = 3(80−x)
x = 240 − 3x
4x = 240
x = 60 m
Point is 60 m from first pillar, 20 m from second.
h = 60/√3 = 20√3 m
```

**Q1.** A man observes the top of a tower at 30°. After walking 40 m towards it, the angle becomes 60°. The tower's height is:
(a) 20√3 m
(b) 40/√3 m
(c) 20 m
(d) 30 m

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
h cot 30° − h cot 60° = 40
h√3 − h/√3 = 40
h(3−1)/√3 = 40
2h/√3 = 40
h = 20√3 m
Answer: (a) 🟡 ⭐
</details>

**Q3.** A cloud's angle of elevation from a 60 m high point above a lake is 30° and the reflection's depression is 60°. The cloud's height is:
(a) 90 m
(b) 120 m
(c) 100 m
(d) 80 m

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
Let h = cloud height
tan 30° = (h−60)/d → d = (h−60)√3
tan 60° = (h+60)/d → d = (h+60)/√3
(h−60)√3 = (h+60)/√3
3(h−60) = h+60
3h−180 = h+60
2h = 240
h = 120 m
Answer: (b) 🔴 ⭐
</details>

**Q4.** A tower subtends an angle α at a point on the ground. From a point h m above this point, the depression of the foot is β. The tower's height is:
(a) h cot α tan β
(b) h tan α cot β
(c) h tan α tan β
(d) h(tan α + tan β)

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
Let height = H, distance = d
tan α = H/d → d = H cot α
tan β = d/h → d = h tan β
H cot α = h tan β
H = h tan α tan β
Answer: (c) 🔴 ⭐
</details>

---

**Q5.** From the top of a 45 m building, the angles of depression of the top and bottom of a tower are 30° and 60°. The tower's height is:
(a) 30 m
(b) 45 m
(c) 60 m
(d) 15 m

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>
Let tower = h, distance = d
tan 60° = 45/d → d = 45/√3
tan 30° = (45−h)/d → 1/√3 = (45−h)/(45/√3) = (45−h)√3/45
45/3 = 45 − h
15 = 45 − h
h = 30 m
Answer: (a) 🟡 ⭐
</details>
