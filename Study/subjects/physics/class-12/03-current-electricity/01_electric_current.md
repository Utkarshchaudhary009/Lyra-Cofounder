## 🎯 Stage 1: The Core Idea

### What Is Electric Current?<br>

Imagine a crowded corridor during lunch break. Students are walking randomly in every direction — no net movement anywhere. Now imagine the fire alarm goes off: suddenly, everyone rushes toward the exit. There's now a *net flow* of students in one direction.

**Electric current is exactly like this.**

Inside a metal conductor, free electrons are always jiggling around randomly at enormous speeds (~10⁶ m/s). But there's no net flow — just chaos. Now connect a battery: it creates an electric field inside the wire, and all those electrons start drifting slowly (very slowly — ~10⁻⁴ m/s) in one direction.

> **Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a cross-section of a conductor.**

$$I = \frac{Q}{t} \quad \text{(steady current)}$$
$$I = \frac{dQ}{dt} \quad \text{(instantaneous current)}$$

### Key Insights

1. **Current is a scalar**, not a vector. It doesn't obey vector addition laws. When we say "current flows from A to B," we mean the direction of flow, not a vector direction.

2. **Conventional current** flows from positive (+) to negative (−) terminal *outside* the cell. This is opposite to the actual electron flow! Benjamin Franklin's mistake strikes again.

3. **Current requires a closed circuit.** No complete loop → no sustained current.

### Types of Current

| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Steady (DC) | Constant magnitude and direction | Battery-powered torch |
| Varying | Magnitude changes with time | Charging capacitor |
| Alternating (AC) | Periodically reverses direction | Household supply |

## 🔬 Stage 2: The Formula Lab

### The Core Formula

$$I = \frac{Q}{t} = \frac{ne}{t}$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| I | Electric current | Ampere <br>
(A) |
| Q | Total charge flowing | Coulomb <br>
(C)
 |
| t | Time | Second (s) |
| n | Number of electrons | Dimensionless |
| e | Elementary charge | 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C |

### Definition of 1 Ampere

> **1 Ampere** is the current flowing through a conductor when **1 Coulomb** of charge passes through any cross-section in **1 second**.

$$1 \text{ A} = \frac{1 \text{ C}}{1 \text{ s}}$$

### Smaller Units

| Unit | Symbol | Value |
|------|--------|-------|
| Milliampere | mA | 10⁻³ A |
| Microampere | μA | 10⁻⁶ A |
| Nanoampere | nA | 10⁻⁹ A |

### Charge as Integral of Current

For non-steady current:

$$Q = \int_0^t I \, dt$$

> 💡 **Graphical interpretation:** The charge flowing in time t equals the **area under the I-t curve**.

7. 🟡 A copper wire has a current of $0.16\text{ A}$ flowing through it. Find the number of electrons passing through any cross-section of this wire in $5\text{ seconds}$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formulas:
$$Q = I \times t \quad \text{and} \quad n = \frac{Q}{e}$$
Given:
- $I = 0.16\text{ A}$
- $t = 5\text{ s}$
- $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$

Step-by-step substitution:
1. Calculate charge:
   $$Q = 0.16\text{ A} \times 5\text{ s} = 0.8\text{ C}$$
2. Calculate number of electrons:
   $$n = \frac{0.8\text{ C}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}} = \mathbf{5 \times 10^{18}\text{ electrons}}$$
</details>

8. 🟡 An alpha particle consists of 2 protons. If a beam of alpha particles delivers $3.2 \times 10^{15}$ particles per second to a target, find the current carried by the beam.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formula:
$$I = \frac{Q}{t} = \frac{n \cdot q}{t}$$
Given:
- $q = 2e = 2 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C} = 3.2 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$
- $\frac{n}{t} = 3.2 \times 10^{15}\text{ particles/s}$

Substitution:
$$I = 3.2 \times 10^{15}\text{ s}^{-1} \times 3.2 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C} = 1.024 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A} = \mathbf{1.024\text{ mA}}$$
</details>

9. 🔴 ⭐ In a hydrogen atom, the electron revolves around the nucleus in a circular orbit of radius $5.3 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m}$ at a speed of $2.2 \times 10^{6}\text{ m/s}$. Find the equivalent current due to the orbital motion of the electron.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formulas:
$$T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} \quad \text{and} \quad I = \frac{e}{T} = \frac{ev}{2\pi r}$$
Given:
- $r = 5.3 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m}$
- $v = 2.2 \times 10^{6}\text{ m/s}$
- $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$

Substitution:
$$I = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C} \times 2.2 \times 10^{6}\text{ m/s}}{2 \times \pi \times 5.3 \times 10^{-11}\text{ m}}$$
$$I = \frac{3.52 \times 10^{-13}}{3.33 \times 10^{-10}}\text{ A} \approx 1.057 \times 10^{-3}\text{ A} = \mathbf{1.06\text{ mA}}$$
</details>

10. 🟡 A device is powered by a $12\text{ V}$ battery, and a current of $1.5\text{ A}$ flows through it. Calculate the number of electrons passing through the device in $1\text{ hour}$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formulas:
$$Q = I \times t \quad \text{and} \quad n = \frac{Q}{e}$$
Given:
- $I = 1.5\text{ A}$
- $t = 1\text{ hour} = 3600\text{ s}$
- $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$

Substitution:
$$Q = 1.5\text{ A} \times 3600\text{ s} = 5400\text{ C}$$
$$n = \frac{5400\text{ C}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}} = \mathbf{3.375 \times 10^{22}\text{ electrons}}$$
</details>

11. 🔴 A beam of protons is accelerated through a potential difference. If the proton current is $2.0\text{ mA}$, how many protons strike a target in each millisecond?

### Type 3: Non-uniform current — charge from I(t) ⭐

**Pattern:** "Current varies as I = f(t). Find charge in time interval."

**Solved Example** 🟡

> The current through a conductor is given by I = (3t² + 2t) A. Find the charge flowing in the interval t = 2s to t = 4s.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution</b></summary>

$$Q = \int_2^4 (3t^2 + 2t) \, dt = \left[t^3 + t^2\right]_2^4$$
$$= (64 + 16) - (8 + 4) = 80 - 12 = \textbf{68 C}$$
</details>

**Practice:**

1. 🟡 I = 5t A. Find Q from t = 0 to t = 4 s.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Q = ∫₀⁴ 5t dt = 5[t²/2]₀⁴ = 5 × 8 = **40 C**
</details>

2. 🟡 I = (2 + 3t²) A. Find Q from t = 1 to t = 3 s.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Q = ∫₁³ (2 + 3t²) dt = [2t + t³]₁³ = (6 + 27) − (2 + 1) = **30 C**
</details>

3. 🔴 ⭐ I = 4e⁻²ᵗ A. Find the total charge from t = 0 to t = ∞.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Q = ∫₀^∞ 4e⁻²ᵗ dt = 4 × [−1/2 × e⁻²ᵗ]₀^∞ = 4 × (0 − (−1/2)) = **2 C**
</details>

4. 🔴 I = I₀ sin(ωt). Find the charge flowing in one complete cycle (0 to 2π/ω).

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Q = ∫₀^(2π/ω) I₀ sin(ωt) dt = I₀ × [−cos(ωt)/ω]₀^(2π/ω)
= I₀/ω × [−cos(2π) + cos(0)] = I₀/ω × [−1 + 1] = **0**

Net charge in one full cycle of AC is zero!
</details>

5. 🟡 The current in a wire changes with time according to the relation $I = (2t + 4t^3)\text{ A}$. Calculate the total charge flowing through the cross-section between $t = 1\text{ s}$ and $t = 2\text{ s}$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the integration formula:
$$Q = \int_{t_1}^{t_2} I \, dt$$
Substitution:
$$Q = \int_{1}^{2} (2t + 4t^3) \, dt = \left[ t^2 + t^4 \right]_1^2$$
$$Q = (2^2 + 2^4) - (1^2 + 1^4) = (4 + 16) - (1 + 1) = 20 - 2 = \mathbf{18\text{ C}}$$
</details>

6. 🟡 ⭐ The current in a conductor varies as $I = I_0 e^{-t/\tau}$, where $I_0 = 5\text{ A}$ and $\tau = 2\text{ s}$. Find the charge that flows through the conductor from $t = 0$ to $t = \tau$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the integration formula:
$$Q = \int_{0}^{\tau} I_0 e^{-t/\tau} \, dt$$
Substitution:
$$Q = I_0 \left[ -\tau e^{-t/\tau} \right]_0^\tau = -I_0 \tau \left( e^{-1} - 1 \right) = I_0 \tau \left( 1 - e^{-1} \right)$$
Given $I_0 = 5\text{ A}$ and $\tau = 2\text{ s}$:
$$Q = 5 \times 2 \times \left( 1 - e^{-1} \right) = 10 \left( 1 - 0.368 \right) = 10 \times 0.632 = \mathbf{6.32\text{ C}}$$
</details>

7. 🔴 A time-varying current is described by $I = (3t^2 - 2t + 1)\text{ mA}$ (where $t$ is in seconds). Determine the number of electrons passing through a cross-section of the wire between $t = 2\text{ s}$ and $t = 4\text{ s}$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the integration formula to find charge first:
$$Q = \int_{2}^{4} I(t) \, dt$$
$$Q = \int_{2}^{4} (3t^2 - 2t + 1) \times 10^{-3} \, dt$$
$$Q = 10^{-3} \left[ t^3 - t^2 + t \right]_2^4$$
$$Q = 10^{-3} \left[ (4^3 - 4^2 + 4) - (2^3 - 2^2 + 2) \right]$$
$$Q = 10^{-3} \left[ (64 - 16 + 4) - (8 - 4 + 2) \right] = 10^{-3} \left[ 52 - 6 \right] = 46 \times 10^{-3}\text{ C} = 0.046\text{ C}$$
Number of electrons:
$$n = \frac{Q}{e} = \frac{0.046\text{ C}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}} = \mathbf{2.875 \times 10^{17}\text{ electrons}}$$
</details>

8. 🟡 The current flowing in a circuit is $I = I_0 \sin(\omega t)$. Find the average current over the half-cycle from $t = 0$ to $t = \pi/\omega$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the average value definition:
$$I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{\Delta t} \int_{0}^{\pi/\omega} I \, dt$$
Where $\Delta t = \frac{\pi}{\omega} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{\omega}$.
$$Q = \int_{0}^{\pi/\omega} I_0 \sin(\omega t) \, dt = I_0 \left[ -\frac{\cos(\omega t)}{\omega} \right]_0^{\pi/\omega} = \frac{I_0}{\omega} \left[ -\cos(\pi) + \cos(0) \right] = \frac{I_0}{\omega} [1 + 1] = \frac{2I_0}{\omega}$$
Thus:
$$I_{\text{avg}} = \frac{2I_0/\omega}{\pi/\omega} = \mathbf{\frac{2I_0}{\pi}}$$
</details>

9. 🔴 ⭐ The charge passing through a point in a circuit is given by $Q(t) = Q_0 (1 - e^{-t/t_0})$, where $Q_0 = 10\text{ C}$ and $t_0 = 5\text{ s}$. Find the instantaneous current at $t = 5\text{ s}$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the differentiation formula:
$$I(t) = \frac{dQ}{dt}$$
$$I(t) = \frac{d}{dt} \left[ Q_0 \left( 1 - e^{-t/t_0} \right) \right] = \frac{Q_0}{t_0} e^{-t/t_0}$$
Given $Q_0 = 10\text{ C}$ and $t_0 = 5\text{ s}$:
At $t = 5\text{ s}$:
$$I(5) = \frac{10}{5} e^{-5/5} = 2 e^{-1} = \frac{2}{e} \approx \frac{2}{2.718} = \mathbf{0.736\text{ A}}$$
</details>

10. 🔴 If the current through a conductor is given by $I = I_0 + \alpha t^2$, where $I_0 = 3\text{ A}$ and $\alpha = 0.5\text{ A/s}^2$, calculate the charge flowing through a cross-section of the conductor during the first $10\text{ seconds}$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the integration formula:
$$Q = \int_{0}^{10} \left( I_0 + \alpha t^2 \right) \, dt$$
$$Q = \int_{0}^{10} \left( 3 + 0.5t^2 \right) \, dt = \left[ 3t + \frac{0.5t^3}{3} \right]_0^{10}$$
$$Q = 3(10) + \frac{0.5(1000)}{3} = 30 + \frac{500}{3} \approx 30 + 166.67 = \mathbf{196.67\text{ C}}$$
</details>

### Type 4: Charge from I-t graph ⭐

**Pattern:** "Given an I-t graph, find the charge."

**Solved Example** 🟡

> A current varies linearly from 0 to 10 A over 5 seconds, then stays constant at 10 A for 3 more seconds. Find the total charge.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution</b></summary>

- Phase 1 (triangle): Q₁ = ½ × base × height = ½ × 5 × 10 = 25 C
- Phase 2 (rectangle): Q₂ = 10 × 3 = 30 C
- **Total Q = 25 + 30 = 55 C**
</details>

**Practice:**

<details>

Q = area of rectangle = 3 × 10 = **30 C**
</details>

2. 🟡 Current increases linearly from 2 A to 8 A over 6 seconds. Find Q.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Q = area of trapezium = ½ × (2 + 8) × 6 = **30 C**
</details>

3. 🟡 An I-t graph shows a triangular pulse: I rises from 0 to 5 A in 4 s, then drops back to 0 in 4 s. Find Q.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Q = area of triangle = ½ × 8 × 5 = **20 C**
</details>

4. 🟢 ⭐ An $I-t$ graph shows a constant current of $5\text{ A}$ for the first $4\text{ seconds}$ and then it decreases linearly to $0\text{ A}$ at $t = 8\text{ seconds}$. Calculate the total charge.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

The total charge is the area under the $I-t$ graph:
$$Q = \text{Area of rectangle } (0 \text{ to } 4\text{ s}) + \text{Area of triangle } (4 \text{ to } 8\text{ s})$$
$$Q = (5\text{ A} \times 4\text{ s}) + \frac{1}{2} \times (8\text{ s} - 4\text{ s}) \times 5\text{ A}$$
$$Q = 20 + \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 5 = 20 + 10 = \mathbf{30\text{ C}}$$
</details>

5. 🟡 A current graph contains a semi-circular pulse of radius $2\text{ A}$ starting at $t = 0$ and ending at $t = 4\text{ s}$ (with time on the x-axis in seconds and current on the y-axis in amperes). What is the total charge?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

The vertical radius of the semi-circle represents $I_{\text{max}} = 2\text{ A}$ and the horizontal base represents a time duration of $\Delta t = 4\text{ s}$. The horizontal radius is therefore $\tau = 2\text{ s}$.
The area of this semi-circular region in the current-time space is:
$$Q = \frac{1}{2} \pi \times r_{\text{time}} \times r_{\text{current}}$$
Substitution:
$$Q = \frac{1}{2} \pi \times 2\text{ s} \times 2\text{ A} = \mathbf{2\pi\text{ C}} \approx \mathbf{6.28\text{ C}}$$
</details>

6. 🟡 An $I-t$ graph consists of a trapezoid. The current starts at $0$, increases to $4\text{ A}$ in $2\text{ s}$, remains at $4\text{ A}$ for $4\text{ s}$, and then drops to $0$ in $1\text{ s}$. Find the total charge.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formula for the area of a trapezoid:
$$Q = \frac{1}{2} \times (a + b) \times h$$
Where:
- $a = 4\text{ s}$ (top parallel side, from $t=2$ to $t=6$)
- $b = 7\text{ s}$ (bottom parallel side, from $t=0$ to $t=7$)
- $h = 4\text{ A}$ (height)

Substitution:
$$Q = \frac{1}{2} \times (4 + 7)\text{ s} \times 4\text{ A} = 11 \times 2 = \mathbf{22\text{ C}}$$
</details>

7. 🔴 ⭐ In an AC circuit, the current is positive and has a triangular shape during the first half-cycle. It rises from $0$ to $I_0 = 10\text{ A}$ in $t = 0.01\text{ s}$ and falls back to $0$ at $t = 0.02\text{ s}$. Find the charge flown.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formula for the area of a triangle:
$$Q = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$$
Given:
- $\text{base} = 0.02\text{ s}$
- $\text{height} = 10\text{ A}$

Substitution:
$$Q = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.02\text{ s} \times 10\text{ A} = \mathbf{0.1\text{ C}}$$
</details>

8. 🟡 Find the charge flowing through a circuit during $t = 0$ to $t = 5\text{ s}$ if the $I-t$ graph shows a linear increase of current from $I = 2\text{ A}$ at $t = 0$ to $I = 12\text{ A}$ at $t = 5\text{ s}$.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formula for the area of a trapezoid:
$$Q = \frac{1}{2} \times (I_{\text{initial}} + I_{\text{final}}) \times \Delta t$$
Given:
- $I_{\text{initial}} = 2\text{ A}$
- $I_{\text{final}} = 12\text{ A}$
- $\Delta t = 5\text{ s}$

Substitution:
$$Q = \frac{1}{2} \times (2 + 12)\text{ A} \times 5\text{ s} = 7 \times 5 = \mathbf{35\text{ C}}$$
</details>

9. 🔴 A current pulse is given by a parabolic curve $I(t) = 4t - t^2$ from $t = 0$ to $t = 4\text{ s}$. Find the total charge.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the integration formula:
$$Q = \int_{0}^{4} I(t) \, dt$$
Substitution:
$$Q = \int_{0}^{4} \left( 4t - t^2 \right) \, dt = \left[ 2t^2 - \frac{t^3}{3} \right]_0^4$$
$$Q = \left( 2(16) - \frac{64}{3} \right) - 0 = 32 - 21.33 = \mathbf{10.67\text{ C}}$$
</details>

### Type 5: Direction of conventional vs electron current

**Pattern:** "In which direction does current flow?<br> / Identify electron flow direction."

<details>
<summary><b>Solution</b></summary>

Conventional current is opposite to electron flow.
∴ Conventional current flows from **B to A** in the external circuit.
This means B is the positive terminal and A is the negative terminal.
</details>

**Practice:**

1. 🟢 Electrons drift from right to left through a wire. What is the direction of conventional current?<br>

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Conventional current flows **left to right** (opposite to electron flow).
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Electrons move from **N (negative terminal) → through external circuit → to P (positive terminal)** — opposite to conventional current.
</details>

3. 🟡 Current flows clockwise in a circular loop when viewed from above. What is the direction of electron flow?<br>

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Electrons flow **anticlockwise** when viewed from above.
</details>

4. 🟢 In a semiconductor device, holes (which act as positive charge carriers) move from right to left, while electrons move from left to right. What is the direction of conventional current?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Conventional current direction is:
- The same as the direction of motion of positive charge carriers (holes): **right to left**.
- Opposite to the direction of motion of negative charge carriers (electrons): **right to left**.

Thus, both carriers contribute to current flowing in the same direction: **right to left**.
</details>

5. 🟡 ⭐ In a particle accelerator, a beam of positively charged protons moves horizontally northward. What is the direction of the electric current associated with this beam? What if the beam consisted of electrons moving northward?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

1. For protons (positive charge carriers), the current direction is the same as their motion: **Northward**.
2. For electrons (negative charge carriers), the current direction is opposite to their motion: **Southward**.
</details>

6. 🟡 An electrolytic cell contains positive copper ions ($Cu^{2+}$) moving towards the cathode (negative electrode) and negative sulfate ions ($SO_4^{2-}$) moving towards the anode (positive electrode). If the cathode is on the right and the anode is on the left, what is the direction of conventional current?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

- Positive $Cu^{2+}$ ions move right (towards cathode), so their current contribution is to the **right**.
- Negative $SO_4^{2-}$ ions move left (towards anode), so their current contribution is opposite to their motion, which is to the **right**.

Thus, the net conventional current flows from **left to right** (from anode to cathode).
</details>

7. 🟡 ⭐ A negatively charged plastic ruler is rotated clockwise in a plane. What is the direction of the equivalent conventional current?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Since the ruler has negative charges, their physical motion is clockwise. The direction of conventional current is opposite to the direction of motion of negative charges:
$$\text{Direction of current} = \text{Opposite to Clockwise} = \mathbf{\text{Anticlockwise}}$$
</details>

8. 🟢 During the charging of a secondary battery, the conventional current inside the battery flows from the positive terminal to the negative terminal. What is the direction of electron flow inside the battery during charging?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Because electrons are negatively charged, their drift direction is always opposite to the conventional current direction.
Since conventional current inside the battery flows positive-to-negative, the electron flow must be from the **negative terminal to the positive terminal** inside the battery.
</details>

9. 🔴 In a cathode ray tube (CRT), electrons are emitted from a cathode on the left and travel to an anode on the right. What is the direction of the conventional current, and where does it flow in the external circuit?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

- Inside the tube: electrons move left-to-right, so the conventional current flows from **right to left** (from anode to cathode).
- In the external circuit: conventional current flows from the **positive terminal of the power supply to the anode**, and from the **cathode to the negative terminal of the power supply**.
</details>

### Type 6: Current through multiple carriers

**Pattern:** "Both positive and negative charges move. Find net current."

**Solved Example** 🔴

> In an electrolyte, 10²⁰ positive ions (each with charge +e) move to the right per second, and 5 × 10¹⁹ negative ions (each with charge −e) move to the left per second. Find the net current and its direction.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution</b></summary>

- Current due to positive ions moving right: I₁ = n₁e/t = 10²⁰ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 16 A (to the right)
- Current due to negative ions moving left: Negative charges moving left = conventional current to the right!
  I₂ = n₂e/t = 5 × 10¹⁹ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 8 A (to the right)
- **Net current = 16 + 8 = 24 A to the right**

> 🔑 Key insight: When positive charges move in one direction and negative charges move in the opposite direction, their contributions to conventional current ADD UP.
</details>

**Practice:**

1. 🟡 In a discharge tube, 10¹⁸ protons move left per second and 2 × 10¹⁸ electrons move right per second. Find the current and its direction.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

I_protons = 10¹⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 0.16 A (to the left — same as proton motion)
I_electrons = 2 × 10¹⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 0.32 A (to the left — opposite to electron motion)
**Net current = 0.16 + 0.32 = 0.48 A to the left**
</details>

2. 🔴 In a semiconductor, 5 × 10¹⁸ electrons per second move from A to B, and 3 × 10¹⁸ holes per second move from A to B. Find the net current and its direction.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

I_electrons = 5 × 10¹⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 0.8 A (from B to A — conventional, opposite to electron motion)
I_holes = 3 × 10¹⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 0.48 A (from A to B — same as hole motion)

These are in opposite directions!
Net current = 0.8 − 0.48 = **0.32 A from B to A**

Wait — let me reconsider. Electrons move A→B, so conventional current due to electrons is B→A. Holes move A→B, so conventional current due to holes is A→B.
Net = 0.8 (B→A) − 0.48 (A→B) = **0.32 A from B to A**
</details>

3. 🟡 ⭐ In a neon discharge tube, $2.5 \times 10^{18}$ singly ionized $Ne^+$ ions move to the right per second, and $3.0 \times 10^{18}$ electrons move to the left per second. Find the net current.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formula:
$$I = I_{\text{ions}} + I_{\text{electrons}} = \frac{n_{\text{ions}}e}{t} + \frac{n_{\text{electrons}}e}{t}$$
Since positive ions move right and negative electrons move left, both conventional current components point to the right and add up:
$$I = \left( 2.5 \times 10^{18} + 3.0 \times 10^{18} \right) \text{ s}^{-1} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$$
$$I = 5.5 \times 10^{18} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C} = \mathbf{0.88\text{ A}} \text{ (towards the right)}$$
</details>

4. 🟡 An aqueous solution of $NaCl$ conducts current. If $4.5 \times 10^{16}$ $Na^+$ ions pass a cross-section to the left every second, and $4.5 \times 10^{16}$ $Cl^-$ ions pass the same cross-section to the right every second, what is the total current?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formula:
$$I = \frac{(n_{\text{Na}} + n_{\text{Cl}}) \cdot e}{t}$$
Positive $Na^+$ ions move left $\rightarrow$ conventional current is to the left.
Negative $Cl^-$ ions move right $\rightarrow$ conventional current is to the left.
Total current:
$$I = (4.5 \times 10^{16} + 4.5 \times 10^{16})\text{ s}^{-1} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$$
$$I = 9.0 \times 10^{16} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C} = 1.44 \times 10^{-2}\text{ A} = \mathbf{14.4\text{ mA}} \text{ (towards the left)}$$
</details>

5. 🔴 In a semi-insulating material, under an electric field, $2.0 \times 10^{15}$ electrons/s move to the right, $1.5 \times 10^{15}$ holes/s move to the left, and $0.5 \times 10^{15}$ doubly ionized positive impurities/s move to the left. Determine the net current and direction.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Determine the conventional current direction for each carrier:
1. Electron motion: right $\rightarrow$ current $I_e$ is to the left.
   $$I_e = 2.0 \times 10^{15} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C/s} = 0.32\text{ mA}$$
2. Hole motion: left $\rightarrow$ current $I_h$ is to the left.
   $$I_h = 1.5 \times 10^{15} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C/s} = 0.24\text{ mA}$$
3. Doubly ionized positive impurity motion: left $\rightarrow$ current $I_{\text{imp}}$ is to the left.
   $$I_{\text{imp}} = 0.5 \times 10^{15} \times (2 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C/s}) = 0.16\text{ mA}$$

All three flow directions are left. Thus, the total current:
$$I_{\text{total}} = 0.32 + 0.24 + 0.16\text{ mA} = \mathbf{0.72\text{ mA}} \text{ (towards the left)}$$
</details>

6. 🟡 ⭐ A gas discharge tube has $3.8 \times 10^{17}$ doubly ionized positive gas ions moving to the right per second and $7.6 \times 10^{17}$ electrons moving to the left per second. Find the net current.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the formula:
$$I = \frac{n_{\text{ions}}(2e) + n_{\text{electrons}}(e)}{t}$$
Positive ions move right $\rightarrow$ conventional current is right.
Electrons move left $\rightarrow$ conventional current is right.
Total current:
$$I = (3.8 \times 10^{17} \times 2 + 7.6 \times 10^{17})\text{ s}^{-1} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$$
$$I = (7.6 \times 10^{17} + 7.6 \times 10^{17}) \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ A} = 15.2 \times 10^{17} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ A} = \mathbf{0.2432\text{ A}} = \mathbf{243.2\text{ mA}}$$
</details>

7. 🔴 A copper sulfate ($CuSO_4$) electrolytic bath has $Cu^{2+}$ ions and $SO_4^{2-}$ ions. If a current of $1.6\text{ A}$ is passed through it, how many ions of each type flow past any cross-section per second, assuming they carry equal shares of the current?

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Each species carries $50\%$ of the total current.
$$I_{Cu} = 0.8\text{ A} \quad \text{and} \quad I_{SO_4} = 0.8\text{ A}$$
Each copper ion $Cu^{2+}$ and sulfate ion $SO_4^{2-}$ has charge magnitude:
$$q = 2e = 2 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C} = 3.2 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$$
Number of ions of each type per second:
$$\frac{n}{t} = \frac{I}{q} = \frac{0.8\text{ A}}{3.2 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}} = \mathbf{2.5 \times 10^{18}\text{ ions/s}}$$
</details>

8. 🔴 In a semiconductor, at a certain temperature, the current is carried by both electrons and holes. If the electron current is $80\%$ of the total current of $5\text{ mA}$, and the drift speed of electrons is twice that of holes, find the ratio of the number density of electrons to holes (assume equal cross-sectional area).

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

Using the drift velocity formula:
$$I = n A e v_d$$
So:
$$I_e = n_e A e v_e \quad \text{and} \quad I_h = n_h A e v_h$$
Taking the ratio:
$$\frac{I_e}{I_h} = \frac{n_e v_e}{n_h v_h}$$
Given:
- $I_{\text{total}} = 5\text{ mA} \implies I_e = 4\text{ mA}$ and $I_h = 1\text{ mA}$
- $v_e = 2 v_h$

Substitution:
$$\frac{4}{1} = \frac{n_e}{n_h} \times \left( \frac{2v_h}{v_h} \right) \implies 4 = 2 \frac{n_e}{n_h} \implies \frac{n_e}{n_h} = \mathbf{2}$$
</details>

## 🧱 Stage 4: MCQ Mastery

**Q1.** A steady current flows through a metallic conductor of non-uniform cross-section. Which of the following quantities remains constant along the length of the conductor?
(a) Current density
(b) Drift speed of electrons
(c) Electric current
(d) Electric field

<details>
<summary><b>Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Explanation:** By conservation of charge, the rate of charge flow (current) must be the same through all cross-sections of a conductor in a steady state. Current density $J = I/A$, electric field $E = J/\sigma$, and drift speed $v_d = I/(nAe)$ all vary inversely with the cross-sectional area $A$ and are therefore not constant.
</details>

**Q2.** Why is electric current considered a scalar quantity even though it has both magnitude and direction?
(a) Its direction is arbitrary.
(b) It does not obey laws of vector addition.
(c) The net charge of a current-carrying wire is zero.
(d) The speed of charge carriers is very low.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Explanation:** A vector quantity must obey the laws of vector addition (like the triangle law or parallelogram law). When two current-carrying wires meet at a junction, the net current is the algebraic sum of the individual currents ($I = I_1 + I_2$), completely independent of the angle between the wires. Thus, current is a scalar.
</details>

**Q3.** A steady current of $2\text{ A}$ flows through a copper wire. The net charge on this current-carrying wire is:
(a) Positive
(b) Negative
(c) Zero
(d) Dependent on the current magnitude

<details>
<summary><b>Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

**Answer: (c)**

**Explanation:** A current-carrying wire remains electrically neutral at all times. The number of free electrons entering any section of the wire is always equal to the number of electrons leaving that section, and the total charge of the moving electrons is exactly balanced by the positive charges of the metal lattice ions.
</details>

**Q4.** An electron moves in a circle of radius $r$ with a constant orbital speed $v$. The equivalent orbital current is:
(a) $ev/r$
(b) $ev/(2\pi r)$
(c) $ev^2/r$
(d) $2\pi ev/r$

<details>
<summary><b>Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Explanation:** The period of revolution of the electron is the time taken to complete one orbit:
$$T = \frac{2\pi r}{v}$$
The equivalent current is the charge passing a point per unit time:
$$I = \frac{e}{T} = \frac{e}{\frac{2\pi r}{v}} = \frac{ev}{2\pi r}$$
</details>

**Q5.** **Statement-based Question**

**Explanation:** Statement I is correct because current density is defined as $J = I/A$. Since the cross-sectional area $A$ varies along the wire, $J$ must also vary. Statement II is incorrect because under steady-state conditions, there is no accumulation of charge anywhere in the circuit (conservation of charge). The flow rate is maintained constant by varying the electric field and drift velocity, not by accumulating charge.
</details>

**Q8.** If the charge passing through a conductor is given by $Q(t) = 3t^2 - 6t + 2$ (in SI units), at what time does the instantaneous current in the conductor become zero?

**Q12.** In a metallic wire with no potential difference applied across its ends, the net current is zero. This is because:

**Q15.** When a charged capacitor discharges through a resistor, the current in the circuit:

**Q18.** When we turn on a light switch, the bulb glows almost instantly, even though the drift velocity of electrons is extremely slow ($\sim 10^{-4}\text{ m/s}$). This is because:

**Answer: (b)**

**Explanation:** When the switch is closed, an electromagnetic wave travels through the conductor at nearly the speed of light, establishing the electric field everywhere. Free electrons at every point in the circuit, including inside the bulb filament, begin to drift simultaneously. Thus, the current begins to flow immediately.
</details>

**Q19.** In a superconducting loop, once an electric current is established:
(a) It decays slowly due to air resistance.
(b) It persists indefinitely without any applied electromotive force (EMF).
(c) It requires a constant external battery to maintain the electron flow.
(d) It heats up the superconductor until it loses superconductivity.

<details>
<summary><b>Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

**Answer: (b)**

**Explanation:** Below its critical temperature, a superconductor has zero electrical resistance. Since there is no resistance, there is no energy dissipation ($I^2R = 0$). Thus, once a current is started, it will circulate indefinitely without needing any external voltage source to keep it going.
</details>

**Q20.** The number of electrons that must pass through a cross-section of a wire in $1\text{ second}$ to constitute a current of $1.6\text{ A}$ is:
(a) $10^{19}$
(b) $10^{18}$
(c) $1.6 \times 10^{19}$
(d) $6.25 \times 10^{18}$

<details>
<summary><b>Answer & Explanation</b></summary>

**Answer: (a)**

**Explanation:** Since $Q = ne = I \times t$:
$$n = \frac{I \times t}{e} = \frac{1.6\text{ A} \times 1\text{ s}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}} = 10^{19} \text{ electrons}$$
</details>

## 🔀 Stage 5: Type Mixer

**Q1.** 🟡 ⭐ A steady current of 8 A flows through a conductor for 3 minutes. (a) How much charge flows?<br> (b) How many electrons pass through any cross-section?<br> (c) If the current direction reverses, in which direction do the electrons actually move now?<br>

<details>
<summary><b>Solution</b></summary>

(a) Q = It = 8 × 180 = **1440 C**

(b) n = Q/e = 1440 / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = **9 × 10²¹ electrons**

(c) If conventional current reverses direction, electrons now drift in the **original conventional current direction** (since electron flow is always opposite to conventional current).
</details>

**Q2.** 🟡 The current through a wire is given by I = (6t + 4) A. (a) Find the charge flowing between t = 0 and t = 3 s. (b) How many electrons flow in this time?<br> (c) What is the average current?<br>

<details>
<summary><b>Solution</b></summary>

(a) Q = ∫₀³ (6t + 4) dt = [3t² + 4t]₀³ = 27 + 12 = **39 C**

(b) n = 39 / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = **2.4375 × 10²⁰ electrons**

(c) I_avg = Q/t = 39/3 = **13 A**
</details>

**Q3.** 🔴 ⭐ In an electrolyte solution, Na⁺ ions move to the right at a rate of 4 × 10¹⁸ per second and Cl⁻ ions move to the left at a rate of 3.6 × 10¹⁸ per second. (a) Find the current due to each ion. (b) Find the total current. (c) How much charge passes through a cross-section in 10 seconds?<br>

<details>
<summary><b>Solution</b></summary>

(a) I_Na⁺ = 4 × 10¹⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **0.64 A** (to the right)
    I_Cl⁻ = 3.6 × 10¹⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **0.576 A** (to the right — negative ions moving left contribute current to the right)

(b) Total I = 0.64 + 0.576 = **1.216 A** (to the right)

(c) Q = It = 1.216 × 10 = **12.16 C**
</details>

**Q4.** 🔴 The current in a circuit varies as shown: I = 2 A for 0 ≤ t ≤ 5 s, then I = (12 − 2t) A for 5 < t ≤ 6 s, then I = 0. Find: (a) total charge, (b) average current over the entire 6 s interval.

Q₂ = ∫₅⁶ (12 − 2t) dt = [12t − t²]₅⁶ = (72 − 36) − (60 − 25) = 36 − 35 = 1 C

## 📋 Stage 6: Board Arsenal

**Q1.** 🟢 ⭐ Define electric current. Is it a scalar or vector?<br> Why?<br> *(2 marks)*

<details>
<summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>

**Electric current** is defined as the rate of flow of electric charge through any cross-section of a conductor. Mathematically, I = Q/t (for steady current) or I = dQ/dt (for varying current).

Electric current is a **scalar quantity**. Although current has a direction (along the wire), it does not obey the laws of vector addition. For example, when two currents meet at a junction, the resultant is found by algebraic addition, not vector addition. Hence, current is treated as a scalar.
</details>

**Q2.** 🟢 ⭐ Define the SI unit of electric current. *(1 mark)*

<details>
<summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>

The SI unit of electric current is the **ampere <br>
(A)**. One ampere is defined as the current flowing through a conductor when one coulomb of charge passes through any cross-section in one second.

1 A = 1 C / 1 s
</details>

**Q3.** 🟡 ⭐ Distinguish between conventional current and electron current. *(2 marks)*

<details>
<summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>

| Property | Conventional Current | Electron Current |
|----------|---------------------|-----------------|
| Direction | From positive to negative terminal (external circuit) | From negative to positive terminal (external circuit) |
| Carriers | Assumed to be positive charges | Actual electrons (negative charges) |
| Convention | Established by Benjamin Franklin | Discovered later |
| Relation | Opposite to electron flow | Opposite to conventional current |

Both have the same magnitude and produce the same effects in a circuit.
</details>

**Q4.** 🟡 A current of 0.4 A is drawn by a filament of an electric bulb for 25 minutes. Find the amount of electric charge that flows through the circuit. *(NCERT)* *(2 marks)*

<details>
<summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>

Given: I = 0.4 A, t = 25 min = 25 × 60 = 1500 s

Q = It = 0.4 × 1500 = **600 C**
</details>

**Q5.** 🟡 In a discharge tube, 2 × 10¹⁸ electrons and 1.5 × 10¹⁸ singly-charged positive ions pass through a cross-section per second in opposite directions. Find the total current. *(3 marks)*

<details>
<summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>

Current due to electrons = n_e × e = 2 × 10¹⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 0.32 A
Current due to positive ions = n_p × e = 1.5 × 10¹⁸ × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 0.24 A

Since electrons and positive ions move in opposite directions, both contribute to conventional current in the **same** direction.

**Total current = 0.32 + 0.24 = 0.56 A**
</details>

## 🚀 Stage 7: JEE Mains Arena

**Q1.** 🟡 ⭐ The charge flowing through a resistance R varies with time as Q = at − bt², where a and b are positive constants. The total heat produced in the resistance is:

(a) a³/6b &emsp; (b) a³R/6b &emsp; (c) a³/3bR &emsp; (d) a³R/3b

<details>
<summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

I = dQ/dt = a − 2bt

Current becomes zero at t₀ = a/(2b)

Heat H = ∫₀^(a/2b) I²R dt = R ∫₀^(a/2b) (a − 2bt)² dt

Let u = a − 2bt, du = −2b dt

H = R/(2b) ∫ₐ⁰ u² du = R/(2b) × [u³/3]ₐ⁰ = R/(2b) × a³/3 = **a³R/6b**

**Answer: (b)**
</details>

**Q4.** 🔴 ⭐ A current of 5 A is passing through a metallic conductor of cross-sectional area 4 × 10⁻⁶ m². If the density of the charge carriers is 5 × 10²⁶ m⁻³, the drift velocity of the electrons is:

(a) 1.6 × 10⁻² m/s &emsp; (b) 1.56 × 10⁻² m/s &emsp; (c) 1.56 × 10⁻³ m/s &emsp; (d) 1.56 × 10⁻⁴ m/s
