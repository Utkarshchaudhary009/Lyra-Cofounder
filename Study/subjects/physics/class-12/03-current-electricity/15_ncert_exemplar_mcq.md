## 🎯 Single Correct Option Type

**Q1.** 🟡 Consider a current carrying wire (current $I$) in the shape of a circle. Note that as the current progresses along the wire, the direction of current density $j$ changes in an exact manner, while the current $I$ remains unaffected. The agent that is essentially responsible for is
(a) source of emf.
(b) electric field produced by charges accumulated on the surface of wire.
(c) the charges just behind a given segment of wire which push them just the right way by repulsion.
(d) the charges ahead.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (b)**

Current density $\mathbf{j}$ is a vector quantity, and its direction is the same as the direction of the electric field $\mathbf{E}$ ($\mathbf{j} = \sigma \mathbf{E}$).
In a straight wire, the electric field is uniformly directed along the length.
When a wire is bent into a curve (like a circle), the electric field must bend to keep following the wire. 
How does the electric field "know" to bend? It's due to tiny amounts of charge that accumulate on the surface of the wire wherever it bends. This surface charge distribution creates a transverse electric field component that steers the electrons around the curve.
</details>

**Q2.** 🔴 Two batteries of emf $\varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon_2$ ($\varepsilon_2 > \varepsilon_1$) and internal resistances $r_1$ and $r_2$ respectively are connected in parallel.
(a) The equivalent emf $\varepsilon_{eq}$ of the two cells is between $\varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon_2$, i.e., $\varepsilon_1 < \varepsilon_{eq} < \varepsilon_2$.
(b) The equivalent emf $\varepsilon_{eq}$ is smaller than $\varepsilon_1$.
(c) The $\varepsilon_{eq}$ is given by $\varepsilon_{eq} = \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2$ always.
(d) $\varepsilon_{eq}$ is independent of internal resistances $r_1$ and $r_2$.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (a)**

The formula for the equivalent EMF of two cells in parallel is:
$\varepsilon_{eq} = \frac{\varepsilon_1 r_2 + \varepsilon_2 r_1}{r_1 + r_2}$

This is mathematically a **weighted average** of $\varepsilon_1$ and $\varepsilon_2$, with weights $1/r_1$ and $1/r_2$. 
A weighted average of two numbers *always* falls exactly between the two numbers.
Therefore, $\varepsilon_1 < \varepsilon_{eq} < \varepsilon_2$.
</details>

**Q3.** 🟡 A resistance $R$ is to be measured using a meter bridge. Student chooses the standard resistance $S$ to be $100\text{ } \Omega$. He finds the null point at $l_1 = 2.9\text{ cm}$. He is told to attempt to improve the accuracy. Which of the following is a useful way?
(a) He should measure $l_1$ more accurately.
(b) He should change $S$ to $1000\text{ } \Omega$ and repeat the experiment.
(c) He should change $S$ to $3\text{ } \Omega$ and repeat the experiment.
(d) He should give up hope of a more accurate measurement with a meter bridge.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (c)**

A meter bridge is most accurate when the null point is near the center of the wire (around $50\text{ cm}$).
Currently, the null point is at $2.9\text{ cm}$. This means $R/S = 2.9 / (100 - 2.9) \approx 3 / 97$.
So $R$ is very small compared to $S$ ($R \approx 3\text{ } \Omega$).
To shift the null point to the center, the standard resistance $S$ should be roughly equal to $R$.
Therefore, he should change $S$ to roughly $3\text{ } \Omega$.
</details>

**Q4.** 🔴 Two cells of emf's approximately $5\text{V}$ and $10\text{V}$ are to be accurately compared using a potentiometer of length $400\text{ cm}$.
(a) The battery that runs the potentiometer should have voltage of $8\text{V}$.
(b) The battery of potentiometer can have a voltage of $15\text{V}$ and $R$ adjusted so that the potential drop across the wire slightly exceeds $10\text{V}$.
(c) The first portion of $50\text{ cm}$ of wire itself should have a potential drop of $10\text{V}$.
(d) Potentiometer is usually used for comparing resistances and not voltages.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (b)**

To measure or compare cells up to $10\text{V}$, the total potential drop across the potentiometer wire must be **greater than $10\text{V}$**.
If the driver battery is $8\text{V}$, it cannot balance a $10\text{V}$ cell. So (a) is wrong.
If the driver battery is $15\text{V}$, we can use a rheostat to drop some voltage, ensuring the voltage across the $400\text{ cm}$ wire is slightly greater than $10\text{V}$ (e.g., $11\text{V}$). This allows balance points for both $5\text{V}$ and $10\text{V}$ to fall on the wire. So (b) is correct.
If the first $50\text{ cm}$ has a drop of $10\text{V}$, the remaining $350\text{ cm}$ would be useless, and the potential gradient would be too high, making it very insensitive. So (c) is wrong.
</details>

**Q5.** 🟡 A metal rod of length $10\text{ cm}$ and a rectangular cross-section of $1\text{ cm} \times 1/2\text{ cm}$ is connected to a battery across opposite faces. The resistance will be
(a) maximum when the battery is connected across $1\text{ cm} \times 1/2\text{ cm}$ faces.
(b) maximum when the battery is connected across $10\text{ cm} \times 1\text{ cm}$ faces.
(c) maximum when the battery is connected across $10\text{ cm} \times 1/2\text{ cm}$ faces.
(d) same irrespective of the three faces.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (a)**

Resistance $R = \rho \frac{L}{A}$.
To get maximum resistance, we need the **maximum possible length ($L$)** and the **minimum possible area ($A$)**.
The longest path through the block is along the $10\text{ cm}$ side.
If current flows along the $10\text{ cm}$ side, the area it passes through is the small $1\text{ cm} \times 1/2\text{ cm}$ face.
So, connect the battery across the two $1\text{ cm} \times 1/2\text{ cm}$ faces.
</details>

## 🔀 Multiple Correct Options Type

*(Note: These are crucial for JEE Advanced. One or more options may be correct).*

**Q6.** 🔴 Which of the following characteristics of electrons determines the current in a conductor?
(a) Drift velocity alone.
(b) Thermal velocity alone.
(c) Both drift velocity and thermal velocity.
(d) Neither drift nor thermal velocity.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (a)**

Current $I = n e A v_d$.
The current depends entirely on the **drift velocity** ($v_d$).
Thermal velocity of electrons is massive ($\sim 10^5 \text{ m/s}$), but it is completely random. The average thermal velocity of all electrons is zero, so it contributes exactly zero to the net flow of charge (current).
Therefore, only the drift velocity determines the macroscopic current.
</details>

**Q7.** 🔴 Kirchhoff's junction rule is a reflection of
(a) conservation of current density vector.
(b) conservation of charge.
(c) the fact that the momentum with which a charged particle approaches a junction is unchanged (as a vector) as the charged particle leaves the junction.
(d) the fact that there is no accumulation of charges at a junction.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (b) and (d)**

Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL) states that $\sum I_{in} = \sum I_{out}$.
This directly implies that charge cannot be created or destroyed (**conservation of charge**, option b).
It also implies that charge cannot build up or pool at a junction point; it must keep flowing (**no accumulation**, option d).
</details>

**Q8.** 🔴 Consider a simple circuit containing a battery and a resistance $R$. If the internal resistance of the battery is $r$, then:
(a) the potential difference across the battery is less than its EMF.
(b) the potential difference across the battery is equal to its EMF if $R = \infty$.
(c) the potential difference across the battery is equal to its EMF if $R = 0$.
(d) the power dissipated in $R$ is maximum when $R = r$.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (a), (b), and (d)**

- (a) When discharging, $V = \varepsilon - Ir$, so $V < \varepsilon$. (Correct)
- (b) If $R = \infty$ (open circuit), $I = 0$, so $V = \varepsilon - 0 = \varepsilon$. (Correct)
- (c) If $R = 0$ (short circuit), $I = \varepsilon/r$. Terminal voltage $V = IR = (\varepsilon/r) \times 0 = 0$. (Incorrect)
- (d) This is the exact statement of the Maximum Power Transfer theorem. (Correct)
</details>

**Q9.** 🔴 Temperature dependence of resistivity $\rho(T)$ of semiconductors, insulators and metals is significantly based on the following factors:
(a) number of charge carriers can change with temperature $T$.
(b) time interval between two successive collisions can depend on $T$.
(c) length of material can be a function of $T$.
(d) mass of carriers is a function of $T$.

<details>
<summary><b>Solution & Reasoning</b></summary>

**Answer: (a) and (b)**

Resistivity $\rho = \frac{m}{n e^2 \tau}$.
- $m$ (mass of electron) and $e$ (charge) are fundamental constants, independent of $T$. So (d) is wrong.
- $n$ (number density of carriers) increases exponentially with $T$ in semiconductors and insulators. So (a) is correct.
- $\tau$ (relaxation time between collisions) decreases with $T$ in metals due to increased lattice vibrations. So (b) is correct.
- (c) is wrong because resistivity is a fundamental property of the material, independent of macroscopic dimensions like length.
</details>
