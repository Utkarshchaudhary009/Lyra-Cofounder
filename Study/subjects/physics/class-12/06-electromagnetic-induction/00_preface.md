## 🪤 Top 10 Trap Questions — Read Before You Start

These are the ten mistakes that cost students the most marks in this chapter. Read them now. Revisit them after completing the book.

> ⚠️ **Trap 1 — Angle with PLANE vs. Normal in Flux Formula**
> The formula Φ = BA cosθ uses the angle θ between **B** and the **area normal (n̂)** — NOT the angle between **B** and the plane of the coil. If B is parallel to the plane, the angle with the normal is 90°, so flux = 0. Students routinely flip this.

> ⚠️ **Trap 2 — Open Circuit: EMF Exists, Current Does Not**
> An EMF is *always* induced whenever flux changes — whether the circuit is open or closed. In an open circuit, no current flows, but the induced EMF is real and measurable as a potential difference across the gap.

> ⚠️ **Trap 3 — Rotating Bar Magnet About Its Own Axis — No Current**
> If a bar magnet spins about its own symmetry axis (the axis joining its poles), the magnetic flux through any surrounding loop does **not change**. By Faraday's law, induced EMF = 0. Many students assume "moving magnet = induction."

> ⚠️ **Trap 4 — The N in Faraday's Law — Don't Double-Count**
> For a coil of N turns: ε = −N dΦ/dt. Here, Φ is the flux through **one turn**. If you already include N inside Φ (using flux linkage Ψ = NΦ), do NOT multiply by N again.

> ⚠️ **Trap 5 — Rotating Rod: Use ε = ½Bωl², NOT ε = Blv**
> For a rod rotating about one end in a uniform field B (perpendicular to the plane of rotation), use the result ε = ½Bωl². Using ε = Blv is incorrect because different points of the rod have different velocities.

> ⚠️ **Trap 6 — EMF is Maximum When Coil is PARALLEL to B**
> In an AC generator, EMF = NBAω sinθ. EMF is **maximum** when sinθ = 1, i.e., when the plane of the coil is **parallel** to **B** (θ = 90° from the normal). Flux is zero at this instant — maximum rate of change of flux means maximum EMF.

> ⚠️ **Trap 7 — Lenz's Law: Find B-direction First, Then Right-Hand Rule**
> Always work in this order: (1) determine if flux through the loop is increasing or decreasing, (2) decide the direction of the induced B-field that opposes the change, (3) use the right-hand rule to find the induced current direction. Jumping straight to current direction causes errors.

> ⚠️ **Trap 8 — Self-Inductance Depends on Geometry, NOT Current**
> The self-inductance L of a coil is a property of its geometry (number of turns, cross-sectional area, length, core material) — it does **not** depend on the current flowing through it. The formula L = μ₀n²V for a solenoid has no current in it.

> ⚠️ **Trap 9 — Eddy Currents Are NOT Always Harmful**
> Yes, eddy currents cause energy loss in transformer cores (bad). But they are intentionally used beneficially in: induction stoves, magnetic braking systems, eddy-current dynamometers, and electromagnetic damping in galvanometers. A question asking for "applications" expects both uses.

> ⚠️ **Trap 10 — Back EMF Magnitude Omits the Negative Sign**
> Faraday's law has a negative sign: ε = −dΦ_B/dt. The negative sign indicates direction (Lenz's law). When the question asks for the **magnitude** of induced EMF, drop the negative sign and write |ε| = dΦ_B/dt. Leaving a negative answer for magnitude costs marks.

## 🎓 A Note Before You Begin

Electromagnetic Induction was discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831 — with no formal mathematical training. He worked by intuition, persistence, and thousands of careful experiments. The beauty of his discovery is that **change is the source of everything**: it is not the magnetic field itself, but the *change* in magnetic flux that creates electricity.

As you work through these ten chapters, you will find that every concept here is connected. Lenz's law protects energy conservation. Eddy currents are Faraday's law in bulk conductors. The AC generator is Faraday's law in mechanical form. Self-inductance is Faraday's law turned inward on a coil.

One law. Endless consequences. Understand it deeply, and this chapter becomes one of your strongest weapons in the exam.
