## 🎯 Stage 1: The Core Idea

Imagine a world without electricity. No smartphones, no internet, no lights. Now realize this: almost every watt of electricity powering our modern civilization—whether it comes from a coal power plant, a nuclear reactor, a hydroelectric dam, or a wind turbine—is generated by the exact same device: the **AC Generator**. At its heart, an AC generator does something magically simple: it turns physical motion into electrical power. 

How? By spinning a wire loop inside a magnetic field. According to Faraday's Law, this continuous spinning constantly changes the magnetic flux passing through the loop, which "pushes" the electrons to move, creating an induced electromotive force (EMF). It is the ultimate application of Electromagnetic Induction (EMI).

The AC generator consists of four main parts:
1. **Armature (Coil):** A rectangular coil (like ABCD) with many turns of insulated copper wire, usually wound on a soft iron core to increase the magnetic flux.
2. **Field Magnets:** Strong permanent magnets (or electromagnets) providing a uniform magnetic field from the North to the South pole.
3. **Slip Rings ($R_1, R_2$):** Two hollow metallic rings that rotate *with* the coil. The ends of the coil are permanently connected to these rings. This is what keeps the current *alternating* rather than becoming direct.
4. **Carbon Brushes ($B_1, B_2$):** Stationary conductive pieces that press against the spinning slip rings. They act as the bridge, transferring the generated electricity from the spinning machine to the stationary external circuit.

> ⚠️ **Critical Insights**
> **The Parallel vs Perpendicular Paradox:** The most common trap in this topic involves angles. When the coil's plane is *perpendicular* to the magnetic field, the magnetic flux passing through it is at its absolute maximum. But because the *rate of change* of flux is zero at that exact split second, the induced EMF is **ZERO**. Conversely, when the coil is *parallel* to the field, flux is zero, but the rate of change is maximum, so induced EMF is at its **MAXIMUM**.

> 💡 **Tips**
> To convert an AC generator into a DC generator, you only need to change one part: replace the two continuous **slip rings** with a split-ring **commutator**.

> 🔑 **Key Takeaways**
> An AC generator converts **mechanical energy into electrical energy** using the principle of **Faraday’s Law of Electromagnetic Induction**.

## 🔬 Stage 2: The Formula Lab

Here is the master equation that dictates how electricity is generated in a spinning coil.

### The Equation for Instantaneous EMF
$$ \varepsilon = \varepsilon_0 \sin(\omega t) $$
Where the peak EMF ($\varepsilon_0$) is given by:
$$ \varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega $$

### Variable Table
| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| $\varepsilon$ | Instantaneous induced EMF | Volt ($\text{V}$) |
| $\varepsilon_0$ | Peak (Maximum) induced EMF | Volt ($\text{V}$) |
| $N$ | Total number of turns in the coil | - (unitless) |
| $B$ | Uniform magnetic field strength | Tesla ($\text{T}$) |
| $A$ | Area of the rectangular coil | Square meter ($\text{m}^2$) |
| $\omega$ | Angular velocity (speed of rotation) | Radian per second ($\text{rad/s}$) |
| $t$ | Time | Second ($\text{s}$) |

> **What this formula says:**
> The amount of voltage ($\varepsilon$) an AC generator produces at any exact second fluctuates like a sine wave. The maximum possible voltage ($\varepsilon_0$) it can ever reach depends directly on four things: how many turns of wire you have ($N$), how strong your magnets are ($B$), how big your coil is ($A$), and how fast you spin it ($\omega$). If you double the spinning speed, you double the maximum voltage!

### Mathematical Derivation 
Let the coil be rotated with a constant angular velocity $\omega$. The angle $\theta$ between the normal to the coil (area vector $\vec{A}$) and the magnetic field $\vec{B}$ at any time $t$ is $\theta = \omega t$.

**Step 1:** The magnetic flux $\Phi$ linked with one turn of the coil is:
$$ \Phi = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A} = BA \cos(\theta) = BA \cos(\omega t) $$

**Step 2:** For a coil with $N$ turns, the total flux linkage is:
$$ \Phi_{\text{total}} = NBA \cos(\omega t) $$

**Step 3:** By Faraday’s law of electromagnetic induction, the induced EMF $\varepsilon$ is the negative rate of change of magnetic flux:
$$ \varepsilon = - \frac{d\Phi_{\text{total}}}{dt} $$
$$ \varepsilon = - \frac{d}{dt} [NBA \cos(\omega t)] $$

Since $N, B,$ and $A$ are constants, they come out of the derivative:
$$ \varepsilon = - NBA \left[ -\omega \sin(\omega t) \right] $$
$$ \varepsilon = NBA\omega \sin(\omega t) $$

The maximum value of sine is 1, so the peak EMF $\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega$.
Therefore:
$$ \varepsilon = \varepsilon_0 \sin(\omega t) $$

### The Flux vs EMF Phase Table
| Angle $\theta = \omega t$ | Plane of Coil relative to $B$ | Magnetic Flux ($\Phi \propto \cos\theta$) | Induced EMF ($\varepsilon \propto \sin\theta$) |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :---: |
| $0^\circ$ (0) | Perpendicular | **Maximum** ($+NBA$) | **Zero** (0) |
| $90^\circ$ ($\pi/2$) | Parallel | **Zero** (0) | **Maximum** ($+\varepsilon_0$) |
| $180^\circ$ ($\pi$) | Perpendicular (Flipped) | **Maximum** ($-NBA$) | **Zero** (0) |
| $270^\circ$ ($3\pi/2$) | Parallel (Flipped) | **Zero** (0) | **Minimum/Max Neg** ($-\varepsilon_0$) |
| $360^\circ$ ($2\pi$) | Perpendicular | **Maximum** ($+NBA$) | **Zero** (0) |

### Type 1: Peak EMF Calculation ($\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega$) ⭐
**Pattern:** "A coil of N turns and area A rotates at angular speed $\omega$ in field B. Find the maximum EMF."

**Solved Example** 🟡
> A circular coil of 100 turns, radius $10\text{ cm}$, is rotated at an angular speed of $50\text{ rad/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field of $0.5\text{ T}$. Calculate the maximum induced EMF in the coil.
<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
**Given:**
$N = 100$
$R = 10\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ m} \implies A = \pi R^2 = 3.14 \times (0.1)^2 = 0.0314\text{ m}^2$
$\omega = 50\text{ rad/s}$
$B = 0.5\text{ T}$
<br>
**Formula:**
$$ \varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega $$
$$ \varepsilon_0 = 100 \times 0.5 \times 0.0314 \times 50 $$
$$ \varepsilon_0 = 50 \times 0.0314 \times 50 = 2500 \times 0.0314 = 78.5\text{ V} $$
**Answer:** The peak induced EMF is $78.5\text{ V}$.
</details>

**Practice:**

$\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega = 50 \times 0.1 \times 0.2 \times 10 = 10\text{ V}$.
</details>

$\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega \implies 314 = 100 \times B \times 0.1 \times 100 \implies 314 = 1000B \implies B = 0.314\text{ T}$.
</details>

6. 🔴 A coil produces a peak EMF of $E_0$. If its area is halved, the magnetic field is doubled, and the angular velocity is tripled, what is the new peak EMF in terms of $E_0$?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
New $\varepsilon_0' = N(2B)(A/2)(3\omega) = 3(NBA\omega) = 3E_0$.
</details>

7. 🔴 An armature coil has an area of $500\text{ cm}^2$ and 200 turns. It is spinning in a magnetic field of $0.04\text{ T}$. What must be the angular speed to produce a maximum EMF of $120\text{ V}$?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
$A = 500 \times 10^{-4} = 0.05\text{ m}^2$.
$\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega \implies 120 = 200 \times 0.04 \times 0.05 \times \omega \implies 120 = 0.4\omega \implies \omega = 300\text{ rad/s}$.
</details>

### Type 2: Instantaneous EMF ($\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0 \sin \omega t$) ⭐
**Pattern:** "Find the EMF at time $t = \dots$" or "Given $\varepsilon = 200 \sin(100\pi t)$..."

**Solved Example** 🟡
> An AC generator produces an EMF given by the equation $\varepsilon = 300 \sin(50\pi t)$. Find: (i) the peak EMF, (ii) the angular frequency, and (iii) the instantaneous EMF at $t = 1/150\text{ s}$.
<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
Comparing with $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0 \sin(\omega t)$:
(i) Peak EMF $\varepsilon_0 = 300\text{ V}$.
(ii) Angular frequency $\omega = 50\pi\text{ rad/s}$.
(iii) At $t = 1/150\text{ s}$:
$$ \varepsilon = 300 \sin\left(50\pi \times \frac{1}{150}\right) = 300 \sin\left(\frac{\pi}{3}\right) $$
Since $\sin(\pi/3) = \sqrt{3}/2 \approx 0.866$:
$$ \varepsilon = 300 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 150\sqrt{3}\text{ V} \approx 259.8\text{ V} $$
</details>

### Type 3: Understanding the phases (Angles) ⭐
**Pattern:** "When the coil is parallel/perpendicular to the magnetic field, what is the flux/EMF?"

**Solved Example** 🟡
> In an AC generator, the plane of the armature coil is kept parallel to the magnetic field. What is the magnetic flux linked with the coil, and what is the induced EMF at this instant?
<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
When the plane of the coil is **parallel** to the magnetic field, the angle $\theta$ between the *normal* (area vector) and the magnetic field is $90^\circ$ (or $\pi/2$).
- Magnetic flux: $\Phi = NBA \cos(90^\circ) = 0$.
- Induced EMF: $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0 \sin(90^\circ) = \varepsilon_0 \times 1 = \varepsilon_0$ (Maximum).
**Answer:** Flux is zero, induced EMF is maximum.
</details>

<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

</details>

4. 🟡 A generator coil rotates from $\theta = 0^\circ$ to $\theta = 90^\circ$. How does the induced EMF change?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

</details>

5. 🟡 If the coil starts rotating from a position where its plane is parallel to the magnetic field, what function (sine or cosine) will describe the EMF?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
False. When parallel, flux is zero, but its *rate of change* ($d\Phi/dt$) is at a maximum, producing peak EMF.
</details>

### Type 4: Power and current calculations
**Pattern:** "If the coil is connected to a resistance R, find the maximum current or power."

**Solved Example** 🟡
> An AC generator produces a peak EMF of $100\text{ V}$. If the internal resistance of the generator is negligible and it is connected across a $20\ \Omega$ load, find the peak current and the average power dissipated in the load over one full cycle.
<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
**Peak Current ($I_0$):**
$$ I_0 = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{R} = \frac{100}{20} = 5\text{ A} $$
**Average Power ($P_{avg}$):**
For an AC circuit, average power $P_{avg} = \frac{\varepsilon_0 I_0}{2}$ (or $\varepsilon_{rms} I_{rms}$).
$$ P_{avg} = \frac{100 \times 5}{2} = 250\text{ W} $$
**Answer:** Peak current is $5\text{ A}$ and average power is $250\text{ W}$.
</details>

</details>

<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>

6. 🔴 If the speed of rotation $\omega$ is doubled, by what factor does the maximum power dissipated in a fixed resistor increase?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
$\varepsilon_0 \propto \omega$. If $\omega$ doubles, $\varepsilon_0$ doubles. Maximum power $P_{max} = \varepsilon_0^2 / R$. Since $\varepsilon_0$ doubles, power increases by a factor of $2^2 = 4$.

$P_{avg} = \frac{\varepsilon_0^2}{2R} = \frac{100}{2 \times 5} = 10\text{ W}$.
Energy = $P_{avg} \times T = 10 \times (\pi/10) = \pi\text{ Joules}$.
</details>

### Type 5: Converting RPM to rad/s

<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
**Frequency conversion:**
Frequency $f = \frac{600}{60}\text{ revolutions per second (Hz)} = 10\text{ Hz}$.
Angular frequency $\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi(10) = 20\pi\text{ rad/s} \approx 62.83\text{ rad/s}$.
<br>
**Peak EMF:**
$$ \varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega = 250 \times 0.05 \times 0.04 \times (20\pi) $$

**Answer:** The peak EMF is $10\pi\text{ V}$ or $31.4\text{ V}$.
</details>

### Type 7: AC vs DC conceptual (slip rings vs split rings)
**Pattern:** "How do we modify this to get DC? What is the function of..."

**Solved Example** 🟢
> State the function of slip rings and carbon brushes in an AC generator. How can this generator be modified to supply direct current (DC)?
<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
- **Slip Rings:** They provide continuous sliding contact with the rotating coil without tangling the wires, allowing the alternating nature of the current in the coil to pass to the external circuit unrectified.
- **Carbon Brushes:** They remain stationary and press against the rotating slip rings to transfer the generated current from the moving armature to the external circuit.
- **Modification for DC:** Replace the two separate continuous **slip rings** with a single split-ring **commutator**. The commutator acts as a mechanical rectifier, reversing the external connection exactly when the current in the coil reverses, keeping the external current unidirectional.
</details>

**Practice:**
1. 🟢 Which component is present in an AC generator but absent in a DC generator?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
Slip rings.
</details>

2. 🟢 Which component acts as a "mechanical rectifier" in a DC generator?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
Split-ring commutator.
</details>

3. 🟡 Why are brushes typically made of carbon?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
Carbon is a good conductor, has a high melting point, and acts as a solid lubricant, minimizing wear on the spinning slip rings.
</details>

4. 🟡 Does the current *inside* the armature coil alternate in a DC generator?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
Yes. The current induced inside the rotating coil is *always* alternating. The commutator only rectifies it for the *external* circuit.
</details>

5. 🟡 What is the purpose of the soft iron core inside the armature?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
It increases the permeability, which greatly increases the magnetic flux linkage with the coil, thus increasing the generated EMF.
</details>

6. 🔴 If the slip rings in an AC generator are welded directly to the external wires, what will happen when the coil spins?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
The wires will twist, tangle, and eventually snap after a few rotations.
</details>

7. 🔴 An AC generator and a DC generator are spun at the same speed. Which one produces a higher *average* EMF over one full cycle?
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
The DC generator. The AC generator's average EMF over a full cycle is zero (positive and negative half-cycles cancel). The DC generator's average EMF is $2\varepsilon_0/\pi$.
</details>

The sine wave curve has equal positive and negative areas over one full period $T$. Thus, they cancel out exactly, making the average EMF zero. The reversal of direction (Reason) directly causes this.

Placing a soft iron core significantly increases the magnetic field $B$ within the coil due to its high permeability, directly increasing $\varepsilon_0$.

8. **Assertion (A):** If the coil is kept stationary and the magnetic poles are rotated around it, no EMF is induced.
**Reason (R):** Relative motion between the coil and the magnetic field is required to change the magnetic flux.

9. **(NCERT Exemplar Trap) A circular coil expands radially in a region of magnetic field and no electromotive force is produced in the coil. This can be because:**
(a) the magnetic field is constant.
(b) the magnetic field is in the same plane as the circular coil and it may or may not vary.
(c) the magnetic field has a perpendicular component whose magnitude is decreasing suitably.
(d) both (b) and (c).

**Answer: (d)**
If the magnetic field is in the plane of the coil (parallel), the flux is zero regardless of area, so expanding it doesn't change the flux (rate = 0). </details>

$\varepsilon_{old} = NBA\omega$. $\varepsilon_{new} = (2N)BA(\omega/2) = NBA\omega$. It remains exactly the same.

## 🔀 Stage 5: Type Mixer

1. **The Hydro-Generator (RPM + Power):** Water falls from a dam, spinning an AC generator at $120\text{ rpm}$. The generator coil has 1000 turns, an area of $2\text{ m}^2$, and operates in a $0.5\text{ T}$ field. If connected to a town with a total equivalent resistance of $100\ \Omega$, find the peak power delivered by the generator.
<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
1. **Find $\omega$:** $120\text{ rpm} = 2\text{ rev/s} \implies f = 2\text{ Hz} \implies \omega = 4\pi\text{ rad/s}$.
2. **Find $\varepsilon_0$:** $\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega = 1000 \times 0.5 \times 2 \times 4\pi = 4000\pi\text{ V}$.
3. **Find Peak Power ($P_{max}$):**
$$ P_{max} = \frac{\varepsilon_0^2}{R} = \frac{(4000\pi)^2}{100} = \frac{16,000,000 \times \pi^2}{100} = 160,000\pi^2\text{ W} $$
Approximating $\pi^2 \approx 10$, $P_{max} \approx 1,600,000\text{ W} = 1.6\text{ MW}$.
</details>

2. **The Graph + Formula Combo:** A student observes the output of an AC generator on an oscilloscope. The time between consecutive voltage peaks (crests) is $0.05\text{ s}$, and the crest-to-trough (peak-to-peak) voltage is $400\text{ V}$. If the coil has 50 turns and area $0.2\text{ m}^2$, what is the strength of the magnetic field $B$?
<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
1. **Extract variables from graph data:**
Time between consecutive peaks = Time Period $T = 0.05\text{ s}$.
$\omega = 2\pi/T = 2\pi/0.05 = 40\pi\text{ rad/s}$.
Peak-to-peak voltage = $2\varepsilon_0 = 400\text{ V} \implies \varepsilon_0 = 200\text{ V}$.
2. **Calculate B:**
$\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega \implies 200 = 50 \times B \times 0.2 \times 40\pi$
$200 = 10 \times B \times 40\pi \implies 200 = 400\pi B$
$B = \frac{200}{400\pi} = \frac{1}{2\pi} \approx 0.16\text{ T}$.
</details>

3. **Phase + Current:** A coil of resistance $10\ \Omega$ generates an EMF given by $\varepsilon = 100 \sin(50\pi t)$. Find the magnitude of the current in the coil exactly when its plane makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the magnetic field.
<details><summary><b>Solution</b></summary>
1. **Find angle:** If the plane makes $30^\circ$ with the magnetic field, the angle $\theta$ between the *normal* (area vector) and the field is $90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$ (or $\pi/3$).
2. **Find EMF:** The instantaneous EMF formula is $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0 \sin(\theta)$ where $\theta$ is the angle with the normal. (Notice $\varepsilon = 100 \sin(\theta)$ since $100$ is the peak).
$\varepsilon = 100 \sin(60^\circ) = 100 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 50\sqrt{3}\text{ V}$.
3. **Find Current:** $I = \varepsilon / R = 50\sqrt{3} / 10 = 5\sqrt{3}\text{ A} \approx 8.66\text{ A}$.
</details>

## 📋 Stage 6: Board Arsenal

1. **(CBSE 2019, 2022 - 5 Marks) Explain with the help of a labeled diagram, the principle and working of an AC generator. Deduce the expression for the EMF generated.**
<details><summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>
**Principle:** It works on the principle of Faraday’s Law of Electromagnetic Induction. When a coil is continuously rotated in a uniform magnetic field, the magnetic flux linked with it changes, inducing an alternating EMF.
**Construction/Diagram:** *(Students must draw a rectangular coil ABCD between N and S poles, attached to slip rings R1 and R2, touched by brushes B1 and B2, connected to load R).*
**Working:** As armature ABCD rotates clockwise, arm AB moves up and CD moves down. The magnetic flux changes. By Fleming's Right-Hand Rule, current flows A $\to$ B and C $\to$ D. After half a rotation, AB moves down and CD moves up, reversing the current direction. The slip rings ensure this alternating polarity reaches the external circuit.
**Derivation:**
Let $N$ = number of turns, $A$ = area, $B$ = magnetic field, $\omega$ = angular velocity.
Flux at time $t$ is $\Phi = NBA \cos(\omega t)$.
By Faraday’s law, $\varepsilon = - \frac{d\Phi}{dt} = - \frac{d}{dt} [NBA \cos(\omega t)]$.
$\varepsilon = -NBA [-\omega \sin(\omega t)] = NBA\omega \sin(\omega t)$.
Thus, $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_0 \sin(\omega t)$, where $\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega$ is the peak EMF.
</details>

(ii) Plot a graph showing the variation of induced EMF with time for one complete revolution of the coil.**
<details><summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>
**(i)** The maximum induced EMF is $\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega$.
**(ii)** *(Students must draw a sinusoidal wave graph)*. 
- X-axis: Time $t$ (marked at $0, T/4, T/2, 3T/4, T$) or Angle $\omega t$ ($0, \pi/2, \pi, 3\pi/2, 2\pi$).
- Y-axis: Induced EMF $\varepsilon$ (marked at $+\varepsilon_0$ and $-\varepsilon_0$).

</details>

3. **(CBSE 2021 - 3 Marks) An AC generator consists of a coil of 50 turns and area $2.5\text{ m}^2$ rotating at an angular speed of $60\text{ rad/s}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.3\text{ T}$. Calculate the resistance of the circuit if the maximum current drawn from the generator is $3\text{ A}$.**
<details><summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>
**Step 1: Calculate Maximum EMF ($\varepsilon_0$)**
$\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega = 50 \times 0.3 \times 2.5 \times 60$
$\varepsilon_0 = 15 \times 150 = 2250\text{ V}$.
**Step 2: Calculate Resistance ($R$)**
From Ohm's Law for peak values, $I_0 = \varepsilon_0 / R$
$3 = 2250 / R \implies R = 2250 / 3 = 750\ \Omega$.
**Answer:** The resistance of the circuit is $750\ \Omega$.
</details>

4. **(CBSE 2018 - 2 Marks) How can an AC generator be converted into a DC generator? Explain briefly.**
<details><summary><b>Model Answer</b></summary>
An AC generator can be converted into a DC generator by replacing the **slip rings** with a **split-ring commutator**.
**Reason:** The commutator consists of two half-rings that reverse the contact with the carbon brushes exactly at the moment the current in the rotating coil reverses direction (every half cycle). This mechanical switching ensures that the current in the external circuit always flows in the same continuous direction (DC).
</details>

## 🚀 Stage 7: JEE Mains Arena

1. **A flat coil of $500$ turns, each of area $50\text{ cm}^2$, rotates in a uniform magnetic field of $0.14\text{ T}$ at an angular speed of $150\text{ rad/s}$. The coil has a resistance of $5\ \Omega$. What is the average value of the alternating current over a half-cycle?**
&emsp;(a) $10.5\text{ A}$ &emsp;(b) $6.68\text{ A}$ &emsp;(c) $7.5\text{ A}$ &emsp;(d) $0\text{ A}$
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
**Answer: (b)**
Peak EMF $\varepsilon_0 = NBA\omega = 500 \times 0.14 \times (50 \times 10^{-4}) \times 150 = 52.5\text{ V}$.
Peak Current $I_0 = \varepsilon_0 / R = 52.5 / 5 = 10.5\text{ A}$.
Average AC current over a **half-cycle** is given by $I_{avg} = \frac{2 I_0}{\pi}$.
$I_{avg} = \frac{2 \times 10.5}{\pi} = \frac{21}{3.1415} \approx 6.68\text{ A}$.
*(Note: If asked over a full cycle, the answer would be zero).*
</details>

2. **An AC generator has a coil of $N$ turns and area $A$. It rotates at frequency $f$ in field $B$. A second generator has a coil of $2N$ turns, area $A/2$, rotates at frequency $2f$ in field $B$. The ratio of the maximum EMF of the second generator to the first is:**
&emsp;(a) $1:1$ &emsp;(b) $2:1$ &emsp;(c) $4:1$ &emsp;(d) $1:2$
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
**Answer: (b)**
$\varepsilon_{01} = NBA(2\pi f)$.
$\varepsilon_{02} = (2N)B(A/2)(2\pi \cdot 2f) = 2 \times NBA(2\pi f) = 2\varepsilon_{01}$.
Ratio is $2:1$.
</details>

3. **A generator coil of resistance $100\ \Omega$ is rotated at $60\text{ Hz}$ in a magnetic field. The peak voltage is $200\text{ V}$. What is the average power required to rotate the generator if mechanical losses are negligible? (Assume it is connected to a matched load that allows max current, but for a simple shorted coil just use its internal R).**
&emsp;(a) $400\text{ W}$ &emsp;(b) $200\text{ W}$ &emsp;(c) $100\text{ W}$ &emsp;(d) $0\text{ W}$
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
**Answer: (b)**
By conservation of energy, mechanical power input equals electrical power output (heat dissipated in resistance).
$P_{avg} = \frac{\varepsilon_{rms}^2}{R} = \frac{(\varepsilon_0 / \sqrt{2})^2}{R} = \frac{\varepsilon_0^2}{2R}$.
$P_{avg} = \frac{200^2}{2 \times 100} = \frac{40,000}{200} = 200\text{ W}$.
</details>

4. **In a region, a uniform magnetic field $B$ is applied along the positive z-axis. A rectangular coil of area $A$ lies in the xy-plane and rotates about the x-axis with angular speed $\omega$. The induced EMF at time $t$ (assuming $t=0$ when it starts in xy-plane) is:**
&emsp;(a) $-NBA\omega \sin(\omega t)$
&emsp;(b) $NBA\omega \cos(\omega t)$
&emsp;(c) $NBA\omega \sin(\omega t)$
&emsp;(d) Zero
<details><summary><b>Answer</b></summary>
**Answer: (c)**
At $t=0$, the coil is in the xy-plane. Its area vector is along the z-axis (parallel to $B$). So the angle $\theta = 0^\circ$ at $t=0$.
Flux $\Phi = NBA \cos(\omega t)$.
$\varepsilon = -d\Phi/dt = -NBA (-\omega \sin(\omega t)) = NBA\omega \sin(\omega t)$.
</details>
