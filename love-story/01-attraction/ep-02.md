# Season 1, Episode 2 — The Pattern of Two

**Draft status:** blueprint
**Prose target:** 4,500–5,500 words

## Episode purpose
Turn coincidence into ritual. The audience should feel the relationship acquiring a private world: jokes, places, timings, habits, and little expectations. Nothing is official, but the emotional infrastructure is being built.

## Surface story
Mira asks Arjun to help clean up the festival mess. The task that should take an afternoon stretches across several days because new problems keep appearing. During those days, practical cooperation turns into voluntary companionship.

## Scene spine

### Scene 1 — The next morning
Arjun arrives early and discovers Mira already reorganizing the schedule. He expects gratitude; she immediately gives him another task. Their rhythm is already less formal than it should be after only one meeting.

### Scene 2 — The first ritual
They begin taking tea after each work session. A harmless disagreement over which tea stall is best turns into a recurring joke. The audience should see how rituals create memory without anyone deciding to create a relationship.

### Scene 3 — Outside the classroom
They discover overlapping interests: Arjun likes building things and dislikes explaining unfinished ideas; Mira likes writing and hates showing unfinished work. They make a pact to show each other unfinished work first.

This is an important intimacy marker: vulnerability becomes reciprocal.

### Scene 4 — Kabir notices
Kabir meets Arjun and points out that Arjun has started arranging his day around Mira without realizing it. Arjun denies it because he thinks intentionality would make the attraction less spontaneous.

### Scene 5 — Mira and Naina
Naina notices Mira also knows the time of Arjun's last class, his favorite tea, and when he tends to disappear. Mira argues that this is simply what happens when two people spend time together. Naina asks whether she is enjoying the person or enjoying how she feels around the person.

Mira dislikes the distinction because she cannot yet answer it.

### Scene 6 — The private joke becomes public
During a larger group gathering, Arjun and Mira accidentally use one of their private jokes in front of everyone. Their friends immediately notice there is a hidden shared history beginning.

### Scene 7 — A late-night message
Mira sends Arjun a message unrelated to the project. It is the first deliberately non-utilitarian contact. They talk late into the night about absurd childhood memories. Arjun falls asleep with the phone beside him.

**Episode ending state:** the relationship now exists as a routine even though neither has named it.

## Psychological movement
Arjun moves from "I enjoy seeing her" to "my day is different depending on whether I see her." This is a subtle but crucial shift: attraction begins taking up time and attention.

Mira discovers she looks for Arjun even when she has no practical reason to.

## Reader learning
Closeness is often constructed from repetition. The mind gives repeated encounters increasing significance because shared routines create a sense of continuity.

Do not state this as exposition. Let the repetition itself teach it.

## Plant for later
- One of their rituals should become impossible to continue after a later conflict.
- Their unfinished-work pact should become important when one character hides something important.
- Mira's delayed replies should be established as normal rather than malicious.
- Arjun's tendency to interpret patterns as evidence should become visible to observant readers before it becomes visible to him.

## Ending image
At 1:12 a.m., Arjun receives a final message from Mira: "Goodnight. Tomorrow, same tea?" He smiles at the screen. The reader should understand that "same tea" has already become shorthand for "same us."
