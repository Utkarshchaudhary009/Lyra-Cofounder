# Season 1, Episode 3 — The Person We Invent

**Draft status:** blueprint
**Prose target:** 4,500–5,500 words

## Episode purpose
Introduce the first difference between knowing a person and knowing the version of them we have constructed. Arjun's attraction deepens because he fills uncertainty with meaning. Mira notices the gap and resists being turned into a symbol.

## Surface story
A student showcase gives Arjun and Mira a long day together. They see each other under stress, around different friends, and in settings where they are not performing for one another. Arjun discovers qualities he likes and qualities he did not expect.

## Scene spine

### Scene 1 — Public Mira
Arjun watches Mira handle a conflict during a presentation. She is sharper and more political than he expected. He is impressed but slightly unsettled because his internal image of her had been softer.

### Scene 2 — The wrong assumption
Arjun casually claims Mira dislikes social gatherings. She corrects him: she dislikes meaningless gatherings, not people. He realizes he has been turning isolated observations into traits.

### Scene 3 — Arjun's unfinished project
He shows Mira an ambitious product prototype. She gives useful criticism rather than praise. He initially feels disappointed, then recognizes that her honesty is part of why he values her.

### Scene 4 — Ishaan appears
Ishaan joins a group conversation. Mira becomes more relaxed around him because their history is long and ordinary. Arjun notices a kind of shorthand between them that he cannot manufacture.

Crucially, Ishaan is not flirtatious. The tension comes from Arjun discovering that Mira has a life with history before him.

### Scene 5 — Naina's question
Naina asks Arjun what exactly he knows about Mira that is not based on how she makes him feel. He struggles to answer. He knows dozens of details but few conflicts, limits, or independent goals.

### Scene 6 — A disagreement
Mira and Arjun argue about ambition. Arjun thinks she underestimates how much risk matters; Mira thinks he uses ambition to avoid asking what a successful life is for. The argument is meaningful because neither person becomes less attractive to the other after it.

### Scene 7 — Repair without romance
They apologize without making a romantic move. Arjun admits, "I had you figured out too quickly." Mira replies, "Most people do. They just don't tell me."

**Episode ending state:** Arjun becomes more attracted after discovering that Mira is less like his imagined version and more like a complicated person.

## Psychological movement
Idealization begins. Arjun's mind takes genuine information and creates a coherent story around it. The danger is not that the story is false; it is that it is incomplete.

Mira becomes aware that Arjun sees her strongly, which feels flattering and slightly dangerous.

## Reader learning
Attraction contains projection. The more we desire coherence, the more quickly we turn fragments of information into a complete character model.

## Plant for later
- Arjun's habit of reading patterns into small signals becomes explicit.
- Mira's line about people "figuring her out too quickly" foreshadows later resistance to certainty.
- Ishaan's historical familiarity is established as emotionally real but not romanticized.
- Arjun's ambition becomes a future source of competing priorities.

## Ending image
Mira leaves after the argument. Arjun watches her go and realizes the strange thing: he had liked her more when he thought he understood her. He likes her differently now because he knows he does not.
