# Season 1, Episode 7 — The First Cost

**Draft status:** blueprint
**Prose target:** 4,500–5,500 words

## Episode purpose
End the season by making attraction consequential without turning anyone into a villain. The characters should discover that genuine feeling does not automatically resolve competing priorities. This is the first crack in the romantic fantasy without destroying the romance itself.

## Surface story
Arjun receives an opportunity connected to his product work that requires significant time and possibly a future move. Mira has an equally important academic/family commitment that takes her in another direction. They both initially assume the other will naturally understand and make room.

## Scene spine

### Scene 1 — Good news
Arjun receives a chance to demo his product to an important group. It could meaningfully change his trajectory. He tells Mira first because her opinion has become important to him.

### Scene 2 — Her good news
Mira receives an opportunity that involves traveling or taking responsibility for a literary project. She expects Arjun to be happy for her. He is happy, but the news creates an unfamiliar anxiety: both futures may consume the time that currently belongs to their shared routine.

### Scene 3 — The calendar
They attempt to solve the problem practically. Their calendars become crowded. The scene should be funny at first, then subtly painful as they realize they are trying to schedule something that previously happened naturally.

### Scene 4 — The missed evening
Arjun misses a promised tea because his product demo preparation runs late. Mira says she understands. Later, he learns she had rearranged her own schedule to make the meeting possible. Neither person is objectively wrong.

### Scene 5 — The argument about priority
Mira asks, "Where do I fit?" Arjun hears the question as a demand to choose her over his ambition. Mira means, "Do I matter enough that you will deliberately make space?"

Arjun responds defensively. Mira responds by emphasizing that she never asked him to stop pursuing his work. The argument exposes that they have different ideas of what care looks like.

### Scene 6 — The blue notebook
They encounter Dr. Rao's notebook again. A new question appears: **"When two good things require the same hour, what does love decide?"**

They do not answer it.

Arjun says, "Why does everything have to be a question?"

Mira replies, "Because answers make us feel finished too quickly."

### Scene 7 — The partial repair
They apologize for the ways they hurt each other but do not solve the scheduling conflict. Instead they make one practical agreement: when something is genuinely important, they will say so rather than expecting the other person to infer it.

This is a small but real improvement.

### Scene 8 — The first cost
Arjun chooses the product opportunity. Mira chooses her academic/literary opportunity. Neither sacrifices the opportunity for the relationship. They remain close, but the old effortless rhythm is gone.

**Episode ending state:** Both understand that whatever is happening between them has become meaningful enough that choices outside the relationship now affect it. That is the first cost of attraction.

## Psychological movement
Arjun learns that being important does not guarantee priority. Mira learns that respecting another person's freedom does not remove the need for explicit negotiation.

Both are still tempted to interpret prioritization as proof of love.

## Reader learning
A genuine feeling does not eliminate scarcity, conflicting goals, or opportunity cost. Love may influence choices, but it cannot make competing goods stop competing.

## Plant for Season 2
- Arjun's discomfort with being non-prioritized becomes the foundation for possessiveness.
- Mira's fear that commitment will consume her autonomy becomes sharper.
- Their communication pact exists but remains fragile.
- The tea ritual is broken for the first time.
- Rhea observes the conflict and becomes a practical countervoice later.

## Ending image
On different trains or buses, Arjun and Mira each look at the same old chat thread. Neither sends a message. Both are thinking some version of the same sentence: **"I thought this was supposed to make life easier."** The season ends there—not with a breakup, but with the discovery that making someone important also gives the world the power to hurt you through them.
