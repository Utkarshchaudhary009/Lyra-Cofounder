# The Things We Call Love

A 10-episode contemporary philosophical drama about love as a human hypothesis.

## Format
- 10 episodes
- Minimum target: 4,000 words per episode
- Dense scene-based prose rather than exposition
- Surface genre: coming-of-age relationship drama
- Underlying genre: philosophical investigation of desire, attachment, freedom, responsibility, choice, and self-deception

## Core design
The audience is never handed a single definition of love. Each major character lives by a different working model. The plot repeatedly creates situations in which those models produce unexpected consequences. Characters appear to "learn" and move on, but several initially repeat the same structural pattern with different people. The deeper arc is learning to distinguish feeling, desire, possession, validation, care, choice, and responsibility.

## Episode sequence
1. Attraction — the first pleasant story we tell ourselves
2. Possession — jealousy masquerades as proof
3. Rejection — desire meets another person's freedom
4. Substitution — a new person, the same hunger
5. Collision — private motives become public consequences
6. Consequence — good intentions can still become control
7. Separation — identity after the object of attachment is gone
8. Recognition — the pattern is finally visible
9. Responsibility — love tested against incompatibility and freedom
10. Choice — maturity shown through action rather than declaration

## Writing rule
Never solve a philosophical question with a speech when the same truth can be demonstrated through a character's choice and its consequence.
