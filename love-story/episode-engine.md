# Episode Engine

Every episode is a complete dramatic unit while advancing the season question and the larger saga.

## Hard requirements

- Minimum **4,000 words**. Target 4,500–5,500 where the story needs room.
- At least 4 substantial scenes unless pacing clearly demands otherwise.
- Every episode changes at least one relationship, belief, decision, or external circumstance.
- No filler scenes whose only purpose is to increase word count.
- No episode ends with a philosophical summary explaining what the audience should think.

## Scene logic

A strong default scene follows:

**Desire → friction → choice → consequence → altered information.**

The next scene should inherit at least one consequence from the previous scene. Avoid disconnected vignettes.

## Episode layers

### Surface plot
What literally happens that day?

### Relationship movement
Who becomes closer, farther, safer, more uncertain, or more honest?

### Psychological movement
What assumption gets reinforced, weakened, contradicted, or replaced?

### Philosophical movement
What question becomes more difficult?

### Long-arc movement
What future event becomes more or less likely because of today's choices?

## Episode ending rule

End on a change in the story state—not on a moral. The reader should want the next episode because something has become newly possible, newly impossible, or newly understood.

## Dialogue rule

Characters speak from what they currently believe, not from what the author knows. Subtext should often carry more truth than explicit dialogue.

## Pacing rule

Allow quiet episodes and ordinary scenes when they create attachment, contrast, or future payoff. Density means meaningful causality, not constant drama.
