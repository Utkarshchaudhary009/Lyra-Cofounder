# Episode 2 — Possession

By October, the ordinary had acquired rules.

Mira replied late.

Arjun noticed.

Mira spoke warmly to Ishaan.

Arjun noticed that too.

Arjun told himself noticing was not jealousy.

It was observation.

Observation was what rational people called it when they did not like the name of the feeling.

---

The blue notebook sat on a table in Dr. Rao's seminar room.

Someone had added a new question beneath last month's definitions.

**When does care become control?**

Arjun saw it while waiting for Mira.

He smiled at first.

"She'll hate this one," he thought.

Then he read the rest of the page.

Someone had written:

**When your care requires the other person to behave the way you need.**

Below it:

**When you call your fear their responsibility.**

Below that:

**When you prefer their safety to their freedom.**

He disliked all three.

Not because they were obviously wrong.

Because all three felt slightly aimed at him.

---

Mira arrived twenty minutes late.

"Sorry."

"You always say that."

"Because I'm always late."

"Then stop being late."

She dropped her bag onto the chair.

"You make simple things sound like moral failures."

"I'm not moralizing."

"You are."

He wanted to argue.

Instead he said, "How was your class?"

"Good."

"With Ishaan?"

The question came out too quickly.

Mira looked at him.

"Yes."

"Okay."

"Why?"

"Nothing."

She sat down.

A silence opened.

Not a dramatic silence.

Just the kind that grows when two people realize one sentence means more than it appears to mean.

"You're doing it again," she said.

"Doing what?"

"Pretending nothing is happening."

"Nothing is happening."

"Arjun."

He looked away.

"I don't like him."

Mira blinked.

"You don't know him."

"I know enough."

"That's not the same thing."

"What exactly is between you two?"

She was quiet.

"Nothing clear."

He hated the phrase.

"Then why does he keep showing up?"

"Because we are friends."

"Are you sure?"

She stared at him.

"What answer would make you comfortable?"

He had none.

He wanted a fact that would erase the uncertainty.

No fact could do that.

---

That weekend the group went to a small café near the riverfront.

Kabir was late.

Naina was taking photographs.

Sameer was arguing with the waiter over a missing bill.

Mira sat opposite Arjun.

Ishaan arrived twenty minutes later.

He greeted everyone normally.

He asked Mira about a book she had mentioned.

They began talking.

Arjun listened.

There was no flirting, at least not in any way he could prove.

That made it worse.

If Ishaan had been obviously interested, Arjun could have classified the situation.

Instead it remained ambiguous.

Ambiguity gave imagination space.

Mira laughed at something Ishaan said.

Arjun looked down at his phone.

He wasn't reading anything.

Kabir noticed.

"You've been looking at the same screen for five minutes."

"I'm checking something."

"What?"

"Nothing."

Kabir smiled.

"You want advice?"

"No."

"Good. I wasn't going to give any."

Across the table, Mira looked at Arjun.

Their eyes met.

For a moment, everything seemed fine.

Then Ishaan said, "Mira, remember that place we used to go after class?"

Arjun heard it.

Used to.

The phrase entered his mind like a nail.

---

That evening, Mira texted him.

**Had fun today.**

He typed:

**Me too.**

Deleted it.

Typed:

**You and Ishaan seem close.**

Deleted that.

Typed:

**Are you still figuring things out with him?**

Deleted that too.

Finally:

**Good night.**

Three dots appeared.

Disappeared.

Returned.

Then:

**Good night :)**

He stared at the smiley face.

His brain treated it as evidence.

Evidence of what, he did not know.

That was the problem.

Humans are very good at collecting evidence before deciding what question they are answering.

---

The next day Mira was talking to Naina outside the library.

Arjun walked past them.

He caught only the end of the conversation.

"...I don't know if I want him to move on."

Arjun stopped.

Naina looked over.

Mira saw him.

Their eyes met.

He walked away.

That night he asked Mira about it.

She denied nothing.

"I said I don't know."

"About me?"

"Partly."

"What does that mean?"

"It means I care about you."

His heart lifted.

Then she continued.

"But I don't know what kind of care it is."

He felt the lift disappear.

"So you care about me but you don't know what you want?"

"Yes."

"And you're okay with that?"

"Should I lie to make you feel better?"

He had no answer.

---

A week later, Arjun's friend Riya from the product club asked him to help prepare for a presentation.

Mira saw them leaving the library together.

She said nothing.

That evening, her messages became shorter.

Arjun noticed.

The next afternoon she asked casually, "Who was that?"

"Riya."

"You're working together?"

"Yes."

"Okay."

The word sounded familiar.

He suddenly understood.

Not fully.

But enough to recognize that her behavior was changing.

He could feel the shape of jealousy without needing to name it.

The realization gave him a strange satisfaction.

Now she could feel what he felt.

That satisfaction frightened him less than it should have.

---

They argued in an empty classroom after sunset.

"You knew I would notice," Mira said.

"Notice what?"

"Her."

"She's my friend."

"I'm not accusing you of anything."

"Then why are you asking?"

Mira crossed her arms.

"Because I don't like how close you are becoming."

Arjun almost smiled.

"Why?"

"I don't know."

"Maybe you're jealous."

She looked away.

"Maybe."

He felt triumphant for one second.

Then ashamed of feeling triumphant.

"Does this mean you like me?" he asked.

She looked at him.

"I don't know."

Again.

The same answer.

But now it meant something different.

"Then why does this bother you?"

She paused.

"Because the idea of you becoming less available to me hurts."

He stared at her.

"That's basically love."

"Is it?"

He expected agreement.

Instead she asked, "What if I don't want a relationship but I don't want to lose you?"

"That's unfair."

"I know."

"Then why are you doing it?"

"Because I'm trying to tell the truth before I understand it."

They went quiet.

Outside, a guard locked the far gate.

A metal clang traveled through the building.

Arjun looked at the notebook on the teacher's table.

He remembered the sentence.

Your fear can become another person's responsibility.

He did not say it aloud.

---

The next morning Mira added a sentence to the notebook.

**Jealousy is not proof of love. It is proof that something feels threatened.**

Arjun saw it.

Underneath, someone had written:

**Then what is threatened?**

Mira wrote:

**Sometimes the relationship. Sometimes the ego. Sometimes the fantasy.**

Arjun added:

**Sometimes all three.**

---

For several weeks they stayed close and undefined.

This arrangement was comfortable precisely because it could mean whatever each person needed it to mean.

They studied together.

They had tea after class.

They argued over music.

Mira sent him photos of strange signs she found around the city.

Arjun sent her screenshots of absurd bugs from the software he was building.

When she had a bad day, he listened.

When he was frustrated about his project, she sat beside him without asking him to explain.

The affection was real.

That was what made the problem difficult.

Real affection can coexist with bad interpretation.

---

One evening, Mira asked him a question.

"Do you want me?"

He laughed nervously.

"That's a strange question."

"It's simple."

"Yes."

"What does want mean?"

He looked at her.

"I want to be with you."

"Why?"

"Because I like you."

"And if I say no?"

He paused.

"I'll respect it."

"Will you?"

He disliked the question.

"Yes."

She nodded.

"Good."

Then she said, "Because if your love disappears the moment I don't give you what you want, maybe it wasn't love."

He said nothing.

She looked embarrassed by her own seriousness.

"Sorry. That sounded like a quote from a terrible film."

He smiled.

"A little."

She smiled back.

The moment passed.

But the question stayed.

---

Three days later, Arjun saw Mira and Ishaan walking across campus.

They were carrying the same book.

Probably accidental.

Probably nothing.

Arjun knew this.

His body did not care.

He felt heat in his chest.

His first impulse was to walk toward them.

His second was to leave.

He chose neither.

He stood still.

For the first time, he watched the feeling itself.

He noticed what it said.

Not:

**I love her.**

But:

**I might lose my place.**

The distinction was small.

It changed everything.

---

That evening he met Kabir at the hostel terrace.

"I think I'm jealous," Arjun said.

Kabir looked delighted.

"Congratulations."

"Why are you happy?"

"Because now you're finally normal."

Arjun laughed.

"Does jealousy mean love?"

Kabir thought for a second.

"No. It means you don't like the possibility of losing something you value."

"That's almost intelligent."

"I contain multitudes."

Arjun looked at the city.

"Then what's love?"

Kabir pointed toward the lights.

"No idea. Ask me after my next three disasters."

Arjun smiled.

"How many are you planning?"

"Minimum three."

They laughed.

Neither knew that Kabir would soon turn the question into a practical experiment.

---

The next week, Mira finally asked Arjun the question he had been avoiding.

"What are we?"

He looked at her.

There was no correct answer hidden somewhere waiting to be selected.

There were only two people bringing private definitions into the same room.

"I don't know," he said.

Mira seemed surprised.

"You always have an answer."

"Maybe that's the problem."

She smiled faintly.

Then she touched the blue notebook lying between them.

"Write one."

He took the pen.

He looked at the page.

Then he wrote:

**Maybe love is wanting someone without turning that want into a claim.**

Mira read it.

For once she did not immediately argue.

She only said:

"That's better."

He looked at her.

"Better than what?"

"Your first definition."

He laughed.

"You remember that?"

"Unfortunately."

They closed the notebook.

Outside the window, students crossed the courtyard in pairs and groups.

Everyone was carrying some version of the same ancient problem:

How do I care about another person without making my need the definition of their freedom?

No one had solved it.

Not yet.
