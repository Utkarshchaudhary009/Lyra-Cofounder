# Episode 3 — Rejection

There are rejections that end a possibility.

There are rejections that only end a fantasy.

The second kind is harder because the person receiving it often keeps enough evidence to construct a private argument against reality.

Arjun had become very good at collecting evidence.

A late-night conversation.

A hand on his shoulder after a difficult presentation.

A message sent first.

The fact that Mira noticed when he skipped lunch.

The way she sometimes looked at him and then looked away.

None of these facts were false.

The story he built from them was uncertain.

---

The conversation happened on a Tuesday beside the old canteen.

It was raining lightly. The tables under the awning were full, so Mira and Arjun stood near the notice board pretending the weather was not wet enough to matter.

"I think we should say it properly," Mira said.

Arjun already knew what she meant.

"Okay."

"I care about you."

He nodded.

"I know."

"And I like being with you."

"I know that too."

She exhaled.

"But I don't want a relationship with you right now."

The sentence was not cruel.

That made it harder.

Cruelty gives the mind something to fight.

A calm no leaves fewer doors.

Arjun looked at the wet pavement.

"Why?"

"Because I don't know if what I feel is enough."

"Enough for what?"

"For choosing a relationship over keeping my life as it is."

He thought of all the things he had imagined.

He had imagined a confession followed by relief.

He had imagined a relationship would solve the ambiguity.

He had not imagined that she might simply not choose him.

"Is it Ishaan?" he asked.

She closed her eyes briefly.

"Partly. But if I say it's him, you'll think removing him solves the problem. It doesn't."

That was almost painful because it was fair.

"So it's me?"

"No."

"Then what is it?"

She looked at him.

"It's me not knowing."

Arjun wanted to tell her that uncertainty could be solved.

He wanted to persuade.

He wanted to demonstrate that he was worth choosing.

Instead he remembered the library steps.

He remembered what it meant to let another person have the final word about their own decision.

"Okay," he said.

Mira looked surprised.

"Okay?"

"You said no. I heard you."

"Are you angry?"

"Yes."

She blinked.

He smiled without humor.

"I'm allowed to be angry. You're allowed to say no. Those can both be true."

Something softened in her face.

"Thank you."

He hated that the sentence made him feel noble.

He did not want nobility.

He wanted her.

The difference between those desires would become important later.

---

For three days Arjun behaved like a man who had accepted rejection.

He went to class.

He trained at the gym.

He worked on his project.

He answered messages.

He did not contact Mira first.

Kabir was impressed.

"You are handling this very maturely," he announced over lunch.

"Don't say that."

"Why?"

"Because now if I do anything stupid, I'll look worse."

Kabir laughed.

"There. That's the old Arjun."

Arjun poked at his food.

"I don't want to make her regret it."

"Then don't."

"What if she changes her mind?"

Kabir shrugged.

"Then she changes it."

"And if she doesn't?"

"Then you don't spend the next six months auditioning for a role she didn't cast you for."

The sentence was blunt enough to be useful.

Arjun laughed.

"You should write books."

"I can't read them."

"Fair."

But the idea had already appeared in Arjun's mind.

He was not going to audition.

He was going to move on.

He said it often enough that the sentence began to sound like a goal rather than a description.

---

The next week, Arjun accepted an invitation to a college debate practice from a classmate named Tara.

Tara was funny, direct, and interested in the same product work he was doing.

They talked easily.

Arjun enjoyed it.

He noticed that he compared almost everything to Mira.

Tara liked bitter coffee.

Mira hated it.

Tara interrupted him.

Mira usually waited until he finished.

Tara laughed at his worst jokes.

Mira made him explain them.

The comparison itself was a warning, but Arjun treated it as natural.

When Tara asked whether he was seeing anyone, he said, "Not exactly."

The answer was honest.

It was also strategically vague.

---

Mira heard about Tara from Naina.

She asked once, "Are Arjun and Tara together?"

Naina looked at her for a second.

"Why do you care?"

"I don't know."

"That's not an answer."

Mira stirred her tea.

"I just asked."

"Then you just asked."

Naina did not answer.

Mira looked irritated.

Later that evening she saw Arjun laughing with Tara outside the seminar hall.

The feeling arrived before thought.

A small tightening in the chest.

A sudden urge to walk over.

An even stronger urge not to.

She hated the feeling because it seemed to prove something she wasn't ready to admit.

She told herself it was not love.

Maybe it was simply loss.

Maybe it was habit.

Maybe it was ego.

Maybe all three.

The uncertainty was unbearable precisely because none of the explanations excluded the others.

---

Mira called Arjun that night.

"How's the debate?"

"Good."

"Tara seems nice."

He smiled.

"She is."

Silence.

"Are you dating?" Mira asked.

"No."

"Would you?"

"I don't know."

The answer made her uncomfortable.

"You move fast," she said.

"I didn't move anywhere."

"It feels like you did."

"You asked me not to wait for you."

She said nothing.

"Mira, I meant what I said. I respect your answer. But respecting it doesn't mean I'm required to stay emotionally frozen."

"I know."

"Then what's wrong?"

She almost said, I don't want you to become less important to me.

Instead she said, "Nothing."

The old word.

Nothing, when something is happening.

They ended the call politely.

Mira stayed awake another hour.

---

The next morning, the blue notebook contained three new lines.

**Can you miss a person you do not want to be with?**

**Can you feel jealous of a person you rejected?**

**Can a no be sincere and still hurt the person who receives it?**

Someone had written underneath:

**Yes. Yes. Yes. Human beings are allowed to be inconsistent. They are not allowed to make their inconsistency another person's prison.**

Mira stared at the sentence.

She did not know who had written it.

She suspected Sameer.

She disliked him for being right.

---

Meanwhile, Arjun decided to make a clean break.

He returned a book Mira had lent him.

He stopped sitting beside her in seminar.

He muted the group chat for a while.

He did not announce any of this.

His silence looked like maturity from the outside.

Inside, it was partly strategy.

He wanted Mira to notice his absence.

He wanted her to miss him.

He wanted her to ask where he had gone.

He told himself he was creating healthy distance.

In truth, some of the distance was a message.

This was the first time the boundary between self-respect and manipulation became difficult to see.

---

Mira noticed.

Of course she noticed.

She asked Naina whether Arjun was angry.

Naina said, "Probably."

"He said he wasn't."

"People can say they aren't angry and still behave differently because they're hurt."

"So what should I do?"

Naina looked at her.

"What do you want?"

Mira hated the question because it was the same question she had avoided.

She wanted Arjun close.

She did not want the relationship.

She wanted him to remain emotionally available.

But she could not honestly demand that availability after refusing the commitment he wanted.

The realization felt unfair.

Not unfair because it was false.

Unfair because reality offered no arrangement in which everyone got exactly what they wanted.

---

Two weeks later, Mira confronted him.

"Why are you avoiding me?"

"I'm not."

"You are."

"I'm giving you what you asked for."

"I didn't ask you to disappear."

"You asked me not to turn uncertainty into a relationship."

"That's not the same thing."

He nodded.

"You're right."

She looked relieved.

Then he continued.

"But I need to decide what being around you does to me."

Her expression changed.

"And?"

"Some days it makes me happy. Some days it makes me wait for something you've already said you're not offering."

She said nothing.

"So I need distance sometimes. Not to punish you. To stop myself from pretending I can be your friend while secretly negotiating a different future in my head."

Mira looked at him for a long time.

"That's the most honest thing you've said to me."

"Probably."

"I don't want to lose you."

He took a breath.

"You might."

She flinched.

"I don't mean forever. I mean there is no guarantee that the shape of our relationship will survive every truth we tell each other."

The sentence frightened both of them.

Because this time the risk was real.

---

Kabir, meanwhile, had become interested in the possibility of moving on.

He had been rejected by a girl from his economics class months earlier.

He had told everyone he was over it.

Then he met Rhea.

Rhea liked late-night drives, bad films, and arguing about whether people were fundamentally selfish.

Kabir liked that she laughed loudly.

"I think this is it," he told Arjun.

"You said that last time."

"Last time I was wrong."

"How do you know you're right now?"

"Because it feels different."

Arjun almost laughed.

"What feels different?"

Kabir paused.

"I don't know. Everything."

That answer was useless and sincere.

It also planted the season's central problem in a new person.

Kabir did not know whether he was discovering someone new or repeating himself with new scenery.

---

Rhea asked him an uncomfortable question one evening.

"Why do you always talk about the girl before me?"

Kabir frowned.

"I don't."

"You do."

"That's not true."

"You compare me to her."

He looked down.

"I don't mean to."

"I know. That's why it's interesting."

"Interesting?"

"You're not comparing people. You're comparing how you feel."

Kabir looked confused.

Rhea continued.

"You like me when I make you feel impressive. You liked her when she made you feel impressive. The details are different. The reward might not be."

Kabir laughed nervously.

"You make everything sound like psychology homework."

"Maybe because you keep giving me experiments."

He didn't answer.

For the first time, someone had described his pattern before he could romanticize it.

---

The semester moved forward.

Arjun and Mira were still part of the same world.

Some days they spoke.

Some days they didn't.

When they did, the conversations were often more honest than before.

That honesty was painful because it removed some of their favorite illusions.

Mira could no longer call Arjun's availability an unquestioned fact.

Arjun could no longer call his desire evidence that he had a right to be chosen.

Neither had become wise.

They had simply lost two convenient lies.

---

At the end of November, Arjun found the blue notebook on the library steps.

The latest page contained one question.

**If someone says no to the relationship you want, what exactly have you lost?**

He sat down.

At first he wanted to write:

**Her.**

Then:

**The future I imagined.**

Then:

**The feeling of being chosen.**

He wrote all three.

Underneath he added:

**The person remains a person. The fantasy is what disappears first.**

He looked at the sentence for a long time.

Across the courtyard, Mira was talking to Naina.

She noticed him.

She waved.

He waved back.

Neither walked over.

The distance between them was not punishment anymore.

It was information.

They were learning what remained when desire could no longer command the outcome.

And neither of them yet knew that jealousy would soon tempt them back into the exact loop they thought they had escaped.
