# Episode 3 — Rejection, continued

The week after the library conversation, Mira discovered that there was a peculiar freedom in not being chosen.

It was painful, but clean.

When Arjun had wanted her to choose him, every unanswered message had contained a question. Every invitation had carried a hidden referendum. Every laugh with someone else seemed to count for or against him.

Now there was no referendum.

She had said no.

He had heard it.

The world had not ended.

That should have been reassuring.

Instead, it made her wonder how much of the intensity had been produced by uncertainty itself.

---

Mira met Dr. Rao after class.

"Can I ask something?"

"You just did."

Mira rolled her eyes.

"A real question."

Rao closed her folder.

"Go on."

"Can you care about someone and still choose not to be with them?"

Rao did not answer immediately.

"Yes."

"Then why does everyone act as if not being together means the feeling was fake?"

"Because people like binary stories. They are easier to remember."

"So what do you believe?"

"I don't know that beliefs about love are useful if you can't update them."

Mira frowned.

"Update?"

"Like a hypothesis. You begin with a model. Reality gives you observations. You revise. The dangerous move is protecting the model instead of learning from the observation."

Mira looked toward the hallway.

"What if the observation hurts?"

"It still counts."

That was all Rao said.

Mira left with the uncomfortable feeling that pain had just been upgraded from an enemy to evidence.

---

Arjun was doing something similar without realizing it.

His new routine was intentionally full.

Classes.

Project work.

Debate practice.

Running.

Evenings with Kabir.

It worked.

Until it didn't.

One night he opened the chat with Mira and scrolled upward.

He read old messages.

A joke about a professor.

A photograph of rain.

An argument over a poem.

A voice note he had saved without remembering why.

He felt the old ache.

His first thought was to message her.

His second was that messaging her would not solve the ache.

It might simply transfer it.

He put the phone down.

The ability to refrain did not feel romantic.

But it felt clean.

---

Two days later Tara asked him to help with the debate finals.

They stayed in the library until closing.

When they stepped outside, Tara said, "Can I ask something?"

"Sure."

"Are you actually interested in me?"

Arjun stopped.

He could have said yes.

He could have said no.

Instead he said, "I like being around you, but I'm not sure whether I'm ready to turn that into something."

Tara smiled faintly.

"Thank you."

"For what?"

"For not giving me the answer you think will be easiest."

They walked toward the gate.

Arjun realized he had spent months wanting other people to give him certainty while he avoided giving them uncertainty honestly.

The insight was small.

But it changed his behavior.

---

Mira heard about Tara again.

This time she did not become immediately jealous.

She became curious.

That difference mattered.

She wondered what would happen if she allowed a feeling to exist without turning it into a command.

She did not text Arjun.

She did not ask Naina for updates.

She simply noticed the jealousy, named it privately, and continued her day.

The feeling rose.

The feeling fell.

Nothing required immediate action.

That was a new discovery.

---

The semester ended with a university trip.

Everyone returned to the same hostel courtyard where the season's first arguments had started.

Kabir arrived with Rhea.

Mira came with Naina.

Arjun came alone.

They all sat together.

For once, no one seemed to know what the group was supposed to be.

Kabir joked about it.

"We've become a failed social experiment."

Sameer said, "Most social experiments are failed."

"That is not encouraging."

"It is honest."

Mira looked at Arjun.

"How is your project?"

"Good. Better than last month."

"What changed?"

"I stopped trying to make it impressive and started trying to make it useful."

She smiled.

"That's very you."

He laughed.

"I don't know if that's a compliment anymore."

"It isn't."

The group laughed.

For ten minutes they were simply friends.

Then Rhea asked Kabir whether he was planning to stay in Lucknow after graduation.

He answered jokingly.

Mira listened.

Arjun listened.

The future entered the conversation without asking permission.

Everyone realized that the relationships around them were not permanent structures. College itself was temporary.

That made every friendship feel simultaneously more valuable and more fragile.

---

Later that night, Mira found Arjun near the stairs.

"Can we talk?"

He nodded.

They walked toward the empty corridor.

"I was jealous," she said.

"Of Tara?"

"Yes."

He waited.

"I don't know what that means."

"Maybe it means you care."

"That's not specific enough."

He smiled.

"You've become difficult."

"You made me this way."

They both laughed.

Then Mira became serious.

"I don't want to be in a relationship right now."

"Okay."

"But I don't want to pretend you don't matter."

"You don't have to."

"And I don't want you waiting for me."

"I'm not."

She studied his face.

"Are you sure?"

He thought before answering.

"I'm trying not to."

That answer was more honest than certainty.

She nodded.

"Good."

They stood there quietly.

No confession.

No promise.

No kiss.

Nothing cinematic happened.

Instead, something more difficult happened: two people allowed uncertainty to remain uncertainty without immediately converting it into a future.

---

At the end of the trip, Professor Rao reopened the blue notebook.

She asked everyone to read their previous answers.

Kabir read "chemistry" and laughed.

Naina read "sacrifice" and defended it.

Sameer read "freedom" and added a question mark.

Mira read "feeling."

Arjun read his old definition aloud.

"The person who makes an ordinary day feel less ordinary."

Rao asked, "Would you write it again?"

Arjun looked at Mira.

Then he looked at the page.

"Maybe. But now I'd add something."

He turned the page.

**Feeling may begin the story. It does not decide the relationship.**

Mira read it.

She nodded.

"That's better."

Kabir leaned over.

"You two are turning rejection into homework."

Mira laughed.

"Maybe homework is cheaper than therapy."

Rao raised an eyebrow.

"Don't test that theory."

Everyone laughed.

The semester ended.

The next semester would begin with the oldest trick in the book:

a new person, a new excitement, and the comforting belief that a different face meant a different self.
