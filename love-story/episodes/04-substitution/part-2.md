# Episode 4 — Substitution, continued

The showcase became a machine that forced everyone to cooperate with people they had privately categorized.

Arjun had once categorized Ishaan as a threat.

Mira had once categorized Tara as a possibility she disliked.

Kabir had categorized every new crush as evidence that he had finally found the right person.

Reality had no interest in their categories.

It simply put everyone in the same room.

---

Three days before the showcase, Tara discovered that Arjun had changed part of the digital installation without telling her.

"Why did you do this?"

"Because the load time was terrible."

"You could have told me."

"It was an engineering fix."

"It was my design."

He stopped.

The argument suddenly looked familiar.

He had been so focused on solving the problem that he had skipped the relationship between the people solving it.

"You're right," he said.

Tara looked surprised.

"That's it?"

"No. I'm sorry."

She nodded.

"Thank you."

They fixed the issue together.

Nothing dramatic happened.

That was precisely the lesson.

A relationship was not tested only by grand sacrifices.

It was tested by thousands of ordinary choices about whose reality counted.

---

That evening, Arjun saw Mira reading alone.

He sat across from her.

"How's Ishaan?"

She looked up.

"Why are you asking?"

"Curiosity."

She smiled.

"Suspicious."

He laughed.

"Fine. Maybe a little suspicious."

"We're friends."

"Good."

She watched him.

"Does it still bother you?"

"Sometimes."

"And what do you do?"

"Nothing."

"Nothing?"

"I notice it. Then I decide whether doing something would improve the situation or merely relieve me."

Mira looked down.

"That's difficult."

"Very."

"What if relieving yourself also improves the situation?"

"Then I'll do it."

She smiled.

"You're learning."

"Don't say that."

"Why?"

"Because then I'll become proud of myself and immediately fail a test."

She laughed.

They returned to their work.

---

The test came that same night.

Tara called Arjun.

Her voice sounded tired.

"I need to ask you something."

"Okay."

"Do you still want Mira?"

He went silent.

"Why are you asking?"

"Because sometimes I feel like I'm standing beside a person who is looking through me at someone else."

The sentence hurt because it was specific.

He wanted to reassure her immediately.

Instead he told the truth.

"Part of me still cares about Mira deeply."

Tara waited.

"And part of me is trying to understand what that care actually is."

"Do you want to be with Mira?"

"I don't know."

"Do you want to be with me?"

He closed his eyes.

"I don't know yet."

The answer was painful to give.

It was also fairer than certainty he did not possess.

Tara was quiet.

Then she said, "I respect the honesty. I don't know if I can stay in something undefined, though."

"You shouldn't."

There was no accusation in the sentence.

Just consequence.

---

The next day Tara stepped back from the relationship.

Arjun felt loss.

He also felt relief.

Then guilt about relief.

Then sadness again.

The emotional sequence confused him.

He wrote in the notebook:

**A feeling can be real and still be the wrong instruction.**

Mira found the page later.

She did not add anything.

For once, she let the statement stand.

---

Kabir's relationship with Rhea reached its own crisis.

She said, "You only notice how much you love me when I'm leaving."

He started to deny it.

Then stopped.

"Maybe you're right."

Rhea nodded.

"What will you do with that?"

He had no answer.

"I don't want to lose you," he said.

"I believe you."

"Then stay."

"That's not the deal."

Kabir looked frightened.

"What is?"

"You changing because you understand something, not because you are terrified of the consequence."

He sat with that.

For the first time, he saw the difference between motivation and reaction.

The same action could be produced by fear or by responsibility.

From the outside, both might look identical.

Inside, they were different systems.

---

The showcase opened.

Crowds moved through the buildings.

The installation worked.

The poetry room filled.

People laughed.

People argued.

Students took photographs.

For several hours, nothing was about relationships.

That was a relief.

At sunset, Arjun stood outside the auditorium and watched Mira speaking with Ishaan.

He felt the familiar pinch.

He did not approach.

He did not flee either.

He simply noticed.

Ishaan looked toward him and walked over.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Sure."

"Do you think I was trying to get Mira back?"

Arjun looked at him carefully.

"Sometimes."

Ishaan nodded.

"I was."

The admission surprised Arjun.

"But I don't want to anymore."

"Why?"

Ishaan looked toward the auditorium.

"Because wanting someone and being good with someone aren't automatically the same thing."

Arjun smiled.

"That's very inconvenient wisdom."

"Most useful wisdom is."

They laughed.

The old rivalry dissolved slightly.

Not because anyone won.

Because the category itself became unnecessary.

---

Later, Mira joined Arjun outside.

"You talked to Ishaan."

"Yes."

"How did that go?"

"He admitted he used to want you back."

Mira looked surprised.

"I knew."

"You knew?"

"Mostly."

They stood quietly.

"Did you ever want him back?" Arjun asked.

Mira took a breath.

"Sometimes."

He nodded.

The old version of him would have treated the answer as a threat.

The new version treated it as information.

That distinction was still fragile.

"And now?"

"Now I think I wanted the version of myself I was around him."

Arjun looked at her.

"Important?"

"Unmissable."

The word surprised him.

She continued.

"I thought being missed proved I mattered."

"And does it?"

"I don't know."

The two of them laughed.

They had become fluent in uncertainty.

Perhaps that was progress.

---

At midnight, after the showcase closed, everyone gathered on the steps.

Kabir raised a paper cup.

"To all the terrible decisions that made us interesting."

Rhea smiled.

"And to learning before making the same one twice."

Sameer added, "That is optimistic."

"Let me have the moment," Kabir said.

Everyone laughed.

Dr. Rao arrived carrying the blue notebook.

She placed it on the step.

"One final question for this semester."

She opened to a blank page.

**How do you know you have changed?**

Nobody spoke.

Finally Arjun wrote:

**When the same situation appears and you want to make the old choice, but you don't.**

Mira added:

**When another person's freedom stops feeling like evidence against your worth.**

Kabir wrote:

**When a new person stops feeling like a new version of yourself.**

Rhea looked at him.

"That's better than your chemistry theory."

"I'm evolving."

"Unfortunately."

They laughed again.

But Arjun noticed something.

The word change had appeared.

Everyone liked it.

Everyone wanted to believe it.

The danger was obvious only later:

saying you had changed was much easier than discovering whether reality agreed.

And the next episode would give them a situation in which intentions alone would not be enough.
