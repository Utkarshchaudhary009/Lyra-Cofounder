# Episode 5 — Collision

A secret is rarely one thing.

Usually it is a collection of small decisions that would look harmless separately.

A message not shown.

A message shown to the wrong person.

A sentence remembered without its context.

A promise interpreted more strongly than it was spoken.

By March, the group had accumulated enough of these to make a crisis almost inevitable.

---

The showcase planning meeting began at nine and ended at twelve-thirty because nobody agreed on the opening sequence.

Mira wanted poetry first.

Arjun wanted the interactive installation first.

Ishaan wanted a speaker to open.

Naina wanted the audience to move through the space in a specific order.

Kabir wanted food.

"The audience doesn't care about our philosophical differences," Kabir said.

"They care about what they see," Mira replied.

"Exactly. Food."

Rhea laughed.

The room relaxed.

Then Arjun said, "We need structure."

Mira said, "We need breathing room."

The disagreement was ordinary.

The history underneath it was not.

They were no longer arguing only about an event.

They were arguing through months of accumulated assumptions.

---

Later that afternoon, Mira asked Arjun to walk outside.

"Did you change the speaker list?"

"I moved two names."

"Without telling me?"

"Because the timings were wrong."

"You keep doing that."

"Doing what?"

"Deciding that your reason automatically gives you the right."

He frowned.

"That's not what I think."

"Then why does it keep happening?"

He had no immediate answer.

She continued.

"You always say you're trying to help."

"I am."

"I know. That's what makes it difficult."

He looked away.

A good intention can still erase another person's agency.

He understood the sentence abstractly.

Now it had his name on it.

---

At the same time, Naina was trying to help Kabir and Rhea.

They had been arguing for a week.

Naina believed the solution was obvious.

Rhea wanted space.

Kabir wanted reassurance.

Naina arranged a dinner without asking either of them.

She invited everyone.

"You'll talk, you'll sort it out, and then it'll be normal," she announced.

Sameer looked at her.

"Did either of them agree to this?"

"They'll thank me later."

He sighed.

"That's usually where control begins."

Naina rolled her eyes.

"I'm helping."

"Maybe."

"What's with everyone saying maybe?"

"Because helping and controlling can look almost identical from the helper's side."

Naina disliked him for saying it.

She ignored him anyway.

---

At dinner, everything went wrong.

Kabir arrived expecting Rhea to be alone with him.

Instead the whole group was there.

He became defensive.

Rhea felt ambushed.

Naina insisted everyone stay.

Mira tried to mediate.

Arjun tried to structure the conversation.

Ishaan left.

Then someone found the screenshot.

It was a private message from Arjun to Mira from weeks earlier.

**I don't think Ishaan is the problem. I think you don't know what you want, and I keep becoming the place where you store the uncertainty.**

Mira had never shown it to anyone.

But Kabir had seen it accidentally when Arjun handed him his phone.

He had mentioned it once to Rhea.

Rhea had told Naina.

Naina had forgotten she knew.

Now the message was on the table.

Mira looked at Arjun.

"Why did you let them see this?"

"I didn't."

"Then who did?"

Nobody answered.

The chain was too embarrassing to admit.

---

Kabir finally spoke.

"I saw it. I didn't mean for everyone to see it."

Rhea looked at him.

"And you told me?"

"Yes."

Naina said, "I only knew because—"

"Stop," Mira said.

The room went silent.

She picked up the phone.

"This is exactly the problem. Everyone keeps passing around pieces of other people's lives because they think they understand the situation better than the person inside it."

Arjun looked at her.

He knew she was talking about the screenshot.

He also knew she was talking about something larger.

He had done the same thing in another form.

He had watched her behavior, interpreted it, and built a theory about what she felt.

He had not needed a screenshot to become an author of a story she had not agreed to.

---

The dinner ended early.

People left in silence.

Arjun remained with Mira.

"I'm sorry," he said.

"For what?"

"For the message."

"Why? It was honest."

"For making you a theory."

She looked at him.

"What?"

"I keep trying to explain you to myself."

She said nothing.

"When I don't understand you, I invent reasons. Then I react to those reasons as if they were facts."

Mira looked tired.

"I do that too."

"I know."

"No, you don't."

He waited.

"I also invented a theory about you."

"What theory?"

"That you wanted me but were too scared to admit it."

He felt his chest tighten.

"And?"

"Sometimes I used your uncertainty to keep hope alive."

He nodded slowly.

They had both been using interpretation as emotional shelter.

Reality was colder.

Reality simply said: we care about each other, we do not agree about what that should become.

---

The next day, Kabir apologized to Rhea.

"I told Naina about the message," he admitted.

"Why?"

"Because I wanted someone to tell me I wasn't overreacting."

Rhea sat quietly.

"That's what I keep doing."

"What?"

"I outsource my certainty."

She looked at him.

"I tell someone my story until they agree with it. Then I feel justified."

He laughed bitterly.

"It's embarrassing."

"It's human."

"Not exactly helpful."

"No. But useful if you stop."

They agreed to take a week apart.

Kabir wanted to object.

He did not.

For once, respecting another person's decision mattered more than obtaining immediate relief.

---

Naina had the hardest reaction.

She went to Sameer.

"Do you think I was wrong?"

"About what?"

"Everything."

"No."

She looked relieved.

Then he said, "But being partly right can still make the method wrong."

She frowned.

"I really hate you sometimes."

"That's okay."

"I thought I was helping."

"I believe you."

"Then why did it become a disaster?"

"Because you decided the outcome was more important than the agency of the people you were helping."

Naina stared at the floor.

"So should I stop caring?"

"No. Learn to care without taking over."

She nodded.

It was not an answer she liked.

It was an answer she could use.

---

Mira and Arjun sat in the empty auditorium that evening.

The event decorations were being removed.

The stage looked smaller without lights.

"Do you remember the first time we met?" Mira asked.

"The broken lights?"

"And the terrible banner."

"You insulted my usefulness."

"You were not useful."

He laughed.

For a moment they returned to the beginning.

Then Mira said, "I think I made you into a story too."

"What kind?"

"The person who would understand me without me having to explain."

He smiled sadly.

"That's impossible."

"I know now."

"And I made you into the person who would eventually choose me if I was patient enough."

She nodded.

They were not confessing romantic truths anymore.

They were confessing narrative errors.

Both had taken another human being and filled the unknown spaces with wishes.

---

Before leaving, Mira opened the blue notebook.

She wrote:

**A person cannot consent to a story you invented about them.**

Arjun added:

**And you cannot call the consequences of your invention proof that your invention was true.**

They closed the notebook.

Outside, the corridor lights switched off one row at a time.

Neither spoke.

For the first time, the danger between them was no longer jealousy.

It was interpretation.

And interpretation was harder to fight because it often felt like intelligence.
