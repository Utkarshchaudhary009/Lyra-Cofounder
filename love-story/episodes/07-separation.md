# Episode 7 — Separation

Separation is often described as an event.

It is usually a process.

First the messages become shorter.

Then the shared references become less current.

Then a joke has to be explained because the person who used to understand it is no longer there.

Then one morning you realize you have not checked whether they are online.

The absence has become ordinary.

That is when it becomes real.

---

Summer made the university almost empty.

Mira remained in Delhi for her internship.

Arjun moved to Bengaluru for three months.

Kabir and Rhea stayed in Lucknow.

Naina took a research opportunity elsewhere.

Sameer graduated first.

The group chat changed from a constant stream to occasional sparks.

Photos of food.

A joke at midnight.

A question about someone's exam.

Then silence.

Arjun expected to miss Mira intensely.

Instead he missed her in strange intervals.

While waiting for a cab.

When something funny happened in a meeting.

When he walked past a bookstore.

When he saw a blue notebook in a shop window.

The missing was no longer an emergency.

That surprised him.

---

One Sunday, he called Mira.

"How are you?"

"Tired."

"Good tired?"

"Mostly."

They spoke about work.

Then about the city.

Then about nothing.

Finally Mira said, "I think we're becoming different people."

Arjun smiled.

"We always were."

"I mean it feels more visible now."

"Yeah."

A pause.

"Do you want to stay close?" she asked.

He took his time.

"Yes. But I don't want to measure closeness by how often we talk."

"Good."

"Why?"

"Because I don't want our care to become an obligation."

He understood.

They ended the call with no promise to speak again tomorrow.

The absence after the call felt strangely peaceful.

---

Kabir was having a different experience.

Rhea's graduate applications were taking up most of her time.

She was busy.

Kabir began noticing the gaps.

One reply instead of five.

One call instead of three.

One evening together instead of four.

His old fear returned.

He told himself the relationship was fading.

Then he caught himself.

Perhaps the relationship was changing.

The difference was enormous.

He did not immediately demand proof.

He asked Rhea what she needed.

"More space," she said.

His stomach tightened.

"And do you still want us?"

"Yes."

"Then I can handle space."

He meant it.

Mostly.

That was enough.

Growth did not require the absence of fear.

It required the ability to act without allowing fear to write the script.

---

Naina returned to Lucknow for a week.

She met Sameer at a café.

"You disappeared," she said.

"I graduated."

"Same thing."

He laughed.

"How is everyone?"

"Different."

"Good."

She looked at him.

"Why do you always say good?"

"Because different is the only direction time moves."

She smiled.

They talked about the group.

Then Naina admitted something.

"I miss fixing things."

Sameer laughed.

"That's the first honest statement you've made all year."

She frowned.

"I mean it. It made me feel useful."

"There you are."

"What?"

"You were calling it love, but part of it was identity."

She leaned back.

She knew he was right.

Being needed had made her feel valuable.

When nobody needed her intervention, she sometimes felt unnecessary.

The solution was not to find new people to rescue.

It was to learn that usefulness and worth were not the same category.

---

In Bengaluru, Arjun received an offer to extend his internship.

It would mean another year away from Lucknow.

He was excited.

Then anxious.

Then excited again.

He told Mira.

"That's huge," she said.

"I think so."

"Will you take it?"

"Probably."

She paused.

"Then take it."

He laughed.

"You always make decisions sound easy."

"Only when they're yours."

The sentence stayed with him.

Love could support a decision without being the reason for it.

That distinction mattered.

---

Three weeks later, Mira received her own offer.

Delhi.

Longer term.

Better role.

She accepted.

Arjun congratulated her.

Neither asked what this meant for them.

Not because they did not care.

Because they knew the question had changed.

It was no longer:

**Will we end up together?**

It was:

**Can two people remain meaningful to each other while choosing lives that do not converge?**

There was no obvious answer.

---

Mira came back to Lucknow for a weekend.

She met Arjun in the old café.

He had just returned from Bengaluru for a few days.

They sat opposite each other.

For five minutes, the conversation was awkward.

They had once known each other's routines.

Now each had new references.

New colleagues.

New neighborhoods.

New ambitions.

The awkwardness was honest.

It proved that memory was not the same thing as continuity.

"You feel different," Mira said.

"You too."

"Is that bad?"

"No."

"Then why are you sad?"

He looked down.

"Because I wanted the version of us from last year to remain available."

Mira smiled sadly.

"So did I."

They sat silently.

Then she said, "Maybe relationships don't always end because they fail. Maybe sometimes they end because the shape that once fit both people no longer fits either."

Arjun nodded.

He wanted to argue.

He couldn't.

---

That night they walked near the river.

The city was humid.

Streetlights reflected on the water.

Mira asked, "Do you regret us?"

Arjun thought carefully.

"No."

"Not even the difficult parts?"

"I regret some choices. I don't regret knowing you."

She smiled.

"That's probably the most adult sentence you've ever said."

"Don't tell anyone."

They laughed.

At the end of the road, a bus arrived.

Mira looked at the route number.

"That's mine."

She turned toward him.

For a second, both felt the old instinct to turn the departure into a promise.

Say something important.

Create a final scene.

Make the moment permanent.

Instead Arjun said:

"Text me when you reach."

She nodded.

"I will."

She got on the bus.

He watched it leave.

No chase.

No dramatic speech.

The bus disappeared into traffic.

And for the first time, he understood that accepting a departure was not the opposite of love.

Sometimes it was one of its forms.
