# Episode 7 — Separation, continued

The next morning, Arjun woke before his alarm.

For a moment he reached for his phone.

Then stopped.

He had always imagined that maturity would feel like becoming calm.

Instead, it felt more like noticing the impulse before obeying it.

---

Mira reached Delhi at 7:12 a.m.

She sent one message.

**Reached.**

Arjun replied:

**Good. Sleep.**

She sent a laughing emoji.

The conversation ended.

It was a tiny thing.

And yet both of them noticed that neither tried to manufacture a second conversation.

The relationship was no longer measured by constant contact.

It was becoming something less visible and possibly more real.

---

Months later, Arjun attended a product conference in Mumbai.

There he met someone new, Anika, who was working on educational software.

They talked for three hours.

He enjoyed it.

On the train back to his hotel, he caught himself constructing a familiar story.

Interesting person.

Easy conversation.

Shared ambitions.

Maybe this means something.

He laughed.

The old machinery was still there.

The difference was that he could see the gears.

He did not reject the attraction.

He simply refused to give it a verdict before evidence existed.

That was new.

---

Kabir called him that night.

"Rhea got her university offer."

"Where?"

"Pune."

"That's good."

"Yes."

A pause.

"I'm scared," Kabir said.

Arjun smiled.

"Good."

"Why does everyone say good to feelings that are obviously terrible?"

"Because being scared doesn't automatically mean something is wrong."

Kabir laughed.

"I hate the mature version of you."

"He's less entertaining."

"Exactly."

They talked for another hour.

Kabir did not ask Arjun how to keep Rhea from leaving.

He asked how to build a relationship that could survive both people having lives.

That was a better question.

---

Rhea left for Pune.

Kabir did not promise to follow.

He did not promise to wait forever.

He promised to be honest about what he could sustain.

They tried long distance.

Sometimes it worked.

Sometimes it didn't.

There were missed calls.

There were ordinary evenings.

There were moments of doubt.

But Kabir stopped treating every doubt as an emergency.

When he felt insecure, he said so.

When Rhea needed space, she said so.

The relationship became less exciting and more trustworthy.

Kabir sometimes missed the excitement.

He did not manufacture drama to recover it.

That was perhaps his first real change.

---

Naina began volunteering at a campus mentoring program.

At first she tried to solve every student's problem.

Then she remembered the notebook.

She began asking:

"What do you want?"

"What have you tried?"

"Do you want advice or listening?"

Her role changed.

She was not rescuing.

She was helping people become more capable of handling their own choices.

She discovered that empowerment was less satisfying than rescue.

It was also more useful.

The difference mattered.

---

Sameer sent one message to the group after graduation.

**Don't mistake the end of a chapter for proof that the chapter was worthless.**

Kabir replied with a joke.

Naina sent a heart.

Mira said, **Too sentimental.**

Arjun wrote nothing.

He saved the message.

Not because it was profound.

Because it was true.

---

One year after the festival, Arjun returned to Lucknow.

He walked through the old campus.

The auditorium had been repainted.

The café had changed its menu.

The library steps were the same.

He sat there alone.

A student he did not know sat nearby, writing in a blue notebook.

Arjun smiled at the coincidence.

He did not approach.

He opened his own phone instead.

There was a message from Mira.

**In Lucknow for two days. Coffee?**

He looked at it for a long time.

The old version of him would have read destiny into the timing.

The new version simply thought:

I would like that.

He replied:

**Yes. Tomorrow?**

The answer came:

**Tomorrow.**

That was all.

The story had not restarted.

It had merely continued.
