# Episode 8 — Recognition, continued

Recognition changed the way they interpreted old memories.

A year earlier, Arjun would remember Mira laughing with Ishaan and feel threatened.

Now he could remember the same moment and ask what, exactly, had been threatened.

His answer changed depending on the day.

Sometimes his pride.

Sometimes his imagined future.

Sometimes the simple desire not to feel replaceable.

The memory had not changed.

The model had.

---

Mira experienced the same thing with her past.

She found an old photograph from the first festival.

Arjun was in the background, holding a coil of cable.

She remembered how important that day had felt.

For years she had interpreted the intensity as evidence that the relationship itself was extraordinary.

Now she could separate the experience from the interpretation.

She had been excited.

She had been curious.

She had been attracted.

She had been afraid of losing something before she knew what it was.

All of those could be true simultaneously.

None alone proved a permanent relationship.

---

Kabir started keeping a list of his triggers.

Delayed replies.

Canceled plans.

A new person entering Rhea's life.

Comparisons with other couples.

Social media photographs.

When one appeared, he wrote three questions:

**What happened?**

**What did I tell myself it meant?**

**What do I actually know?**

The method did not remove anxiety.

It made anxiety answerable.

One evening he showed the list to Rhea.

She smiled.

"You're turning into a scientist."

"No. Scientists have better stationery."

They laughed.

Then she asked, "What did you learn?"

"That my first interpretation is usually designed to protect my ego."

"That's a painful discovery."

"Extremely."

"Useful, though."

"Unfortunately."

---

Naina had perhaps the most practical transformation.

She stopped trying to be the person with the answer.

Students came to her with relationship problems.

Instead of immediately saying what they should do, she asked them to name the decision.

Not the emotion.

Not the fear.

The decision.

"Do you want to stay?"

"Do you want to leave?"

"Do you want to ask for something?"

"Do you want to set a boundary?"

Once the decision was named, the rest became easier.

The mind often becomes confused because it is trying to solve several problems at once.

A person can be sad and still know what to do.

A person can be uncertain and still know what they need to ask.

A person can love and still leave.

This was not coldness.

It was separation of categories.

---

Arjun and Mira met one final time before graduation.

They opened the old notebook.

The first question remained visible under layers of handwriting:

**What do you call love when you remove the story?**

Mira read it aloud.

"What would you answer now?"

Arjun thought.

"I don't know."

She smiled.

"Good."

"But I know what I wouldn't call it."

"What?"

"A feeling that gives me rights over another person."

Mira nodded.

"I wouldn't call jealousy proof."

"I wouldn't call sacrifice proof."

"I wouldn't call permanence proof."

"And I wouldn't call a breakup proof that the love was fake."

They looked at the page.

The definitions had not produced a final answer.

They had produced boundaries around bad answers.

Perhaps that was what knowledge often looked like in real life: not a perfect formula, but fewer ways to fool yourself.

---

The slow campus clock struck six.

Mira closed the notebook.

"Ready?"

Arjun nodded.

They left the room.

Neither knew exactly what the future would do.

But they knew how they wanted to behave when it arrived.

That was enough for the next episode.
