# Recurring Motifs

## The blue notebook
A physical object that travels through the ensemble. Early entries contain simplistic definitions. Later entries contain contradictions, questions, crossed-out answers, and eventually choices. It is never a magical wisdom device; different characters interpret it differently.

## Ordinary days
A recurring phrase: someone makes an ordinary day less ordinary. Early meaning: romantic intensity. Later meaning: gratitude for finite moments. Final meaning: permanence is not required for significance.

## Doors and thresholds
Auditorium doors, hostel gates, train doors, apartment entrances. Characters repeatedly stand at literal thresholds when deciding whether to enter, leave, wait, or follow.

## Shared objects
Books, tea cups, notes, borrowed jackets, chargers, tickets, photographs. Objects move between characters and carry memory without becoming sentimental shortcuts.

## The unanswered message
Early: a source of anxiety and interpretation. Middle: a tool characters use to create ambiguity. Late: a reminder that another person's silence is information but not an instruction to invent a story.

## Maps and routes
Bus routes, train platforms, future cities, career paths. Physical geography mirrors the growing problem of whether two people can choose the same direction without either surrendering autonomy.

## Repeated questions
Questions should recur with altered answers:
- What are we?
- What do you want?
- Are you sure?
- What does love mean?
- What are you responsible for?
- What happens if we choose differently?

The power comes from the answer changing because the character has changed, not because the wording changed.
