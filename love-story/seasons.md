# Ten-Season Architecture

## Season 1 — Attraction
**Question:** What does attraction feel like, and what do we mistake it for?

Episodes should establish ordinary life before romance dominates it. Arjun and Mira meet through shared work. Their attraction develops through repeated low-stakes contact. The audience experiences the pleasure and novelty of being understood. Early contradictions remain small enough to dismiss.

**End-state:** They believe the relationship is becoming special, but they have not yet defined what that means.

## Season 2 — Possession
**Question:** When does care become ownership or control?

Jealousy emerges naturally. Both characters discover the other can affect their emotional state without intending to. Ambiguity becomes a source of leverage. Friends begin seeing a pattern before the couple does.

**End-state:** A boundary is crossed. They learn that feeling threatened does not automatically grant authority over another person's choices.

## Season 3 — Rejection
**Question:** What does another person's no actually mean?

A clear rejection occurs. The story explores wounded pride, interpretation, dignity, friendship after rejection, and the temptation to turn another person's decision into a judgment of one's own worth.

**End-state:** At least one character realizes that rejection is information, not an argument to defeat.

## Season 4 — Substitution
**Question:** Can changing the person change the pattern?

Characters enter new relationships or pursue new attractions. A second relationship appears healthier at first. Slowly, familiar dynamics return. The reader starts seeing the invariant behavioral pattern.

**End-state:** The characters begin suspecting that they are carrying their previous relationship into the next one.

## Season 5 — Collision
**Question:** What happens when incompatible models of love meet?

Old and new relationships overlap socially. Different philosophies are forced into the same decisions. Friendship, loyalty, romantic attachment, and ambition become mutually constraining.

**End-state:** A major choice makes clear that every model contains truth and every model is incomplete.

## Season 6 — Consequence
**Question:** What do beliefs cost when they become behavior?

The season focuses on aftermath rather than ideology. Characters must live with decisions they thought were loving. Secrets, omissions, overprotection, avoidance, or manipulation create concrete costs.

**End-state:** The characters can no longer evaluate their beliefs only by intention; they must account for consequences.

## Season 7 — Separation
**Question:** Who are we without the relationship?

The central romantic structure breaks or pauses long enough to remove its stabilizing and destabilizing effects. Careers, friendships, family, health of routines, and identity become the foreground.

**End-state:** Each major character discovers which parts of themselves existed independently of being wanted.

## Season 8 — Recognition
**Question:** Can we see the patterns we keep repeating?

The audience should now be ahead of the characters. Old phrases, reactions, and decisions recur in altered circumstances. Characters finally name their own patterns without someone else forcing the diagnosis.

**End-state:** Insight becomes specific enough to predict behavior.

## Season 9 — Responsibility
**Question:** What does love actually require of us, and what does it not?

Commitment is tested against freedom, compatibility, truth, sacrifice, and personal direction. The question is no longer simply whether people love each other, but what action is justified by that love.

**End-state:** Characters develop principles that survive disagreement and uncertainty.

## Season 10 — Choice
**Question:** What do we choose when every honest choice has a cost?

The final season tests whether the characters' philosophical growth changes behavior. No perfect solution appears. The resolution comes from choices that are owned, understood, and accepted rather than from fate or reward.

**End-state:** The reader sees a more mature relationship between feeling, freedom, choice, and responsibility. The ending should leave the model open enough for the reader to continue thinking.
