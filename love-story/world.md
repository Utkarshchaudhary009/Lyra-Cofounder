# World

## Setting

A contemporary Indian university ecosystem spanning campus life, nearby neighborhoods, cafés, hostels, family homes, internships, travel, and early professional life.

The setting should feel lived-in rather than cinematic: ordinary corridors, group projects, festivals, exams, family expectations, commutes, weddings, internships, late-night calls, rented rooms, and changing cities.

## Why this setting matters

The characters are at an age where identity, friendship, career, family, freedom, attraction, and commitment are all changing at once. This creates natural pressure on relationships without requiring melodramatic villains.

## Social system

The characters exist inside overlapping networks:

- close friends who know different versions of the same person
- classmates and colleagues who create repeated encounters
- family members whose expectations shape long-term choices
- former partners who remain socially adjacent
- new people who expose old patterns

## Story reality

There is no narrator who tells the audience what is objectively true about love. Reality is revealed through behavior, contradictions, consequences, and time.

Nobody exists solely to teach a lesson. Every major character must have a life that would continue even if the romantic plot disappeared.

## Recurring physical spaces

The library steps, a crowded café, campus auditorium, hostel terrace, city bus routes, train platforms, a family dining table, a quiet park, and later workplaces or apartments.

These spaces should recur so that the audience experiences how meaning changes when the people using the same place have changed.
