# Chapter 8: The Black-Market Gala

The Shanghai underground had its own gravity, pulling in the world's worst secrets and dressing them in silk. The Black-Market Gala was held beneath the Bund, in a subterranean ballroom that pre-dated the wars. It was an auction house where the currency was biological warfare and the patrons drank champagne while bidding on localized plagues.

Lyra Sterling stood near a cascading fountain of champagne, a glass flute held loosely in her hand. She wore a gown of midnight blue silk that draped like liquid shadow, the neckline plunging with architectural precision, the back entirely bare. It was armor masquerading as vulnerability. Her hair was pinned sharply back, leaving her razor-sharp features unobstructed. She looked precisely like what she used to be: a CEO who owned the room and everyone in it.

Beside her, Anderson looked like a weapon forced into formalwear.

His suit was tailored to charcoal perfection, cut to hide the bulk of a man built for demolition, but the fit couldn’t hide the quiet, deadly economy of his movements. He didn't scan the room like a typical bodyguard; he absorbed it. Every exit, every concealed weapon, every blind spot. He stood close enough to her that she could feel the heat radiating from his chest.

“Three o'clock,” Anderson murmured, his voice a low hum vibrating over the string quartet. “Russian syndicate. They’re bidding on the respiratory agent.”

“Let them bid,” Lyra replied, her eyes scanning the crowd. “We’re not here for the anthrax derivatives. We’re here for the Chimera.”

Somewhere above them, on the catwalk overlooking the ballroom, Kenji stood completely still in a black trench coat, his mind reaching through the noise. The Japanese telepath was fishing in a sea of greed, searching for the one mind that carried Julian’s instructions.

“Kenji, status,” Lyra sub-vocalized into her ear-piece.

*‘So much noise,’* Kenji’s voice echoed directly in her mind, a psychic projection smooth and melancholic. *‘Greed is loud, Lyra. But I am sorting through it. The Genesis operatives are here. The ones Vance sent.’*

“Keep looking,” she instructed.

A waiter passed by with a tray of hors d'oeuvres. Anderson stepped imperceptibly closer to Lyra, shifting his weight to block a drunken arms dealer from brushing against her shoulder. It was a fluid, protective gesture, completely automatic.

The string quartet transitioned into a slower, mournful waltz.

“We need to move,” Anderson said, his eyes fixed on a Genesis operative near the VIP lounge. “He’s looking for you. If we stand still, we're targets. If we blend, we're scenery.”

“Then we blend,” Lyra said. She turned to face him, setting her empty flute on a passing tray.

Anderson looked at her, his jaw tightening. “Sterling—”

“It’s Lyra,” she corrected, stepping into his space. She placed her hand on his shoulder. Under the bespoke wool, his muscles were rigid as iron. “Dance with me.”

He didn't want to. She could see the tactical calculus running behind his eyes, the refusal forming on his lips. But she also saw the moment it broke. He slid one hand around her waist, his palm flat against the bare skin of her back. The touch was electric, jarringly warm. Her breath hitched.

He led her onto the floor.

They moved together with a synchronization that terrified her. He wasn't a man who danced; he was a man who adapted. He anticipated her steps the same way he anticipated a combatant’s strike. The space between them narrowed until there was no space at all.

Her hand rested against his chest. She could feel his heartbeat—slow, steady, unnervingly calm. But his breathing was shallow.

His hand on her back shifted. His thumb moved against the silk of her dress, just where it met her skin—one centimeter, back and forth. It wasn't a tactical maneuver. It was an involuntary betrayal of the eighty years of solitude he carried.

She looked up at him. The ambient light of the ballroom caught the exhaustion in his eyes.

This was not falling in love, she told herself. Falling implied accident. This was a calculated descent into known hazardous terrain with full awareness of catastrophic failure. It was also the most honest decision she'd ever made.

She opened her mouth to speak. To tell him—something. To acknowledge the fracture in the armor.

His jaw tightened. He pulled her closer, his mouth dropping to hover right beside her ear. The heat of his breath made her shiver.

“Don’t,” he whispered.

The word was a plea and a warning. It meant *don't break this, don't name this, don't make me want this.*

*‘Lyra,’* Kenji’s voice slammed into her mind, shattering the moment. *‘I have it.’*

Anderson stepped back instantly, the space between them immediately turning cold. Lyra composed herself, her face returning to the mask of the Queen.

“What do you see, Kenji?” she whispered.

*‘The Genesis operatives. They are not here to buy. They are here to distribute. Julian’s plan... it’s not an attack. It’s an infection. He’s using Genesis’s global vaccine distribution network. A pathogen designed to rewrite the genome. To undo us.’*

Lyra’s blood ran cold. Genesis supplied sixty percent of the world’s immunizations. If Julian hijacked the supply chain...

*‘There is a problem, though,’* Kenji continued, his mental voice laced with confusion. *‘The pathogen is incomplete. The operative’s mind... he knows Julian is waiting. Julian needs the blueprint. He needs the DNA of the original subjects to finalize the sequence.’*

Lyra frowned, turning away from the dance floor. She walked toward the edge of the ballroom, where a large, frosted glass partition separated the VIP lounge from the main floor. Anderson flanked her, his hand resting near the concealed holster beneath his jacket.

“Sarah,” Lyra sub-vocalized, calling the healer at the safehouse. “Did you finish the blood work on the team?”

“I did,” Sarah’s voice crackled over the comms, sounding utterly exhausted. “Lyra, it’s strange. I was running the sequences. Mei, Dmitri, Kenji, Leo... myself. We all share an identical anomaly.”

Lyra touched the glass partition. It was dark, highly reflective. She could see her own ghost-like reflection staring back at her. The sharp features. The impeccable dress. The granddaughter of Alistair Sterling.

“What kind of anomaly?” Lyra asked, her voice dropping.

“A forty-seven-base-pair sequence,” Sarah said. “It’s engineered. But it’s... it’s incomplete. Like a fragment of a larger code. It doesn't do anything on its own. But if you take mine, and Mei’s, and Leo’s... if you put all the fragments together...” Sarah trailed off. The silence on the line was deafening.

Through the glass partition, Lyra saw her reflection. But behind it, faintly visible through the frosted surface, she saw the Genesis operatives packing their silver briefcases. Moving out. Moving on.

*The trap is not ahead of you. It is behind you.* Kenji’s words echoed in her mind.

Lyra closed her eyes. The air in her lungs turned to ash.

“The journal,” she whispered.

“What?” Anderson asked, stepping closer to her, sensing the sudden shift in her vital signs.

Lyra reached into the small clutch purse she carried, pulling out her grandfather’s weathered leather journal. She flipped to the back page. The page she had read a dozen times since they pulled it from the ashes of Site Zero.

*1. 47-A. 2. 47-B. 3. 47-C. 4. 47-D. 5. 47-E. 6. 47-F. 7. 47-G.*

*Disposability quotient.*

The seven numbers weren’t subject IDs. They were DNA fragment indices.

Her grandfather hadn’t just experimented on those children. He had DESIGNED the ultimate pathogen, and then, terrified of what he had created, he had split the blueprint across the seven subjects. He had hidden the weapon inside the victims.

*Subject 7 is beyond our control. Seal the facility. Let history forget.*

Julian didn't have the full code. He couldn't create the pathogen on his own. He needed the others. He needed the fragments to be brought together.

Lyra opened her eyes, staring at her reflection in the glass partition. The elegant, commanding woman looking back at her was a pawn. She saw the Assembly Trap clearly now, the horrifying perfection of Julian's design.

Julian hadn't let them escape the bunker because they were lucky. He had let them live so they could do his work for him. By traveling the globe, by recruiting Mei, Dmitri, Kenji, Leo, and Sarah, Lyra hadn't been building an army to stop the Chimera.

She had been assembling the blueprint.

She had gathered the weapon for him.

“Lyra?” Anderson’s hand closed gently over her arm. “What is it?”

She looked at him, the weight of the revelation crushing the breath from her throat. Her grandfather's failsafe was broken. And she was the one who broke it.

"He used us," she whispered, the glass cold against her fingertips. "We didn't form a resistance, James. We built his bomb."
