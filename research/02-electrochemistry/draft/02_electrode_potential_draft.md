# Chapter 2: Electrode Potential & Standard Hydrogen Electrode - Research Draft

## 1. Concept Details & Minute Details


### The Core Idea
When a metal is placed in a solution of its own ions, a potential difference develops between the metal and the solution. This is called electrode potential. If the concentration is 1M, pressure is 1 atm, and temperature is 298K, it is the Standard Electrode Potential ($E^\circ$). By IUPAC convention, standard electrode potential is always standard *reduction* potential.

### Measurement of Electrode Potential
- Absolute electrode potential cannot be measured. We can only measure the potential difference between two electrodes.
- The Standard Hydrogen Electrode (SHE) is used as a reference electrode, assigned an arbitrary potential of exactly 0.00 V at all temperatures.

### Minute but Important Details (Often Ignored)
1. **Electrochemical Series:** A series of elements arranged in increasing order of their standard reduction potentials.
   - Low (negative) $E^\circ_{red}$: Strong reducing agents (e.g., Li, K, Ca, Na, Mg). Easily oxidized.
   - High (positive) $E^\circ_{red}$: Strong oxidizing agents (e.g., $F_2$, $Au^{3+}$, $Cl_2$, $Ag^+$). Easily reduced.
2. **SHE Construction:**
   - Platinum black coated Pt foil is used because finely divided platinum increases the surface area for the $H_2$ gas to adsorb and rapidly attain equilibrium between $H_2$ gas and $H^+$ ions.
   - Pressure of $H_2$ must be exactly 1 bar (or 1 atm).
   - Concentration of $H^+$ (from HCl or H2SO4) must be exactly 1 M.
3. **Calculation of Cell Potential ($E^\circ_{cell}$):**
   - $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode}$ (where both are reduction potentials).
   - Alternatively: $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{OP}(Anode) + E^\circ_{RP}(Cathode)$.
4. **Feasibility of Reaction:** A redox reaction is spontaneous only if the overall $E^\circ_{cell}$ is positive.
5. **Storage of Solutions:** A solution of a salt (like $CuSO_4$) cannot be stored in a vessel of a metal (like Zn) if the metal has a lower reduction potential than the metal of the salt (because it will displace it).

## 2. Question Types & Strategy

**Type 1: Calculating $E^\circ_{cell}$**
*Strategy:* Identify Anode (lower reduction potential) and Cathode (higher reduction potential). Apply $E^\circ_{cell} = E^\circ_{cathode} - E^\circ_{anode}$.

**Type 2: Predicting Feasibility of a Reaction**
*Strategy:* If $E^\circ_{cell}$ > 0, feasible. If < 0, non-feasible.

**Type 3: Identifying Strongest Oxidizing/Reducing Agent**
*Strategy:* Highest +ve $E^\circ$ = Strongest Oxidizing Agent (gets reduced easily). Lowest -ve $E^\circ$ = Strongest Reducing Agent (gets oxidized easily).

**Type 4: Can we store X in vessel Y?<br>**
*Strategy:* The vessel is the solid metal (potential anode). The solution contains ions (potential cathode). Calculate $E^\circ_{cell}$. If positive, reaction occurs, so DO NOT store.

**Type 5: Displacement Reactions**
*Strategy:* A metal can displace any metal below it (higher $E^\circ$) in the electrochemical series from its salt solution.
