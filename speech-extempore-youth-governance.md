# The Role of Youth in Local Governance and Policy-Making

Good afternoon everyone,

Today, I want to talk about something that affects every single one of us in this room — the role of youth in local governance and policy-making.

Let me start with a question. When was the last time you attended a ward meeting?<br> When was the last time you knew who your local councilor was, let alone what they were voting on?<br> If the answer makes you uncomfortable, you're not alone. But here's the thing — if we're not at the table, we're on the menu.

Local governance is where democracy breathes. It's not the distant parliament or the grand presidential address — it's the pothole on your street, the garbage that isn't collected, the local school that lacks resources, the youth center that got shut down. These are the decisions that shape our daily lives. And yet, young people are conspicuously absent from these rooms.

Why does this matter?<br> Because policies made without youth input are policies built for yesterday. We are the generation that will live with the consequences of every decision made today — climate policy, urban planning, education reform, digital infrastructure. If we're not part of the conversation, we become passive recipients of decisions made by people who won't be around to face the consequences.

The late Wangari Maathai said, "The environment and human beings are interlinked. When we degrade the environment, we degrade ourselves." The same applies to governance. When we disengage from governance, we degrade our own future.

Now, I'm not saying it's easy. Young people face real barriers — the perception that we're inexperienced, that we're idealistic, that we don't understand how things "really work." But let me tell you what we do bring. We bring energy. We bring digital fluency. We bring fresh perspectives unburdened by "that's how it's always been done." We bring a stake in the future that no other demographic can match.

## What We Can Do

1. **Show up.** Attend your local council meetings. Most of them are public. Sit in the back. Listen. Then speak.

2. **Organize.** A single voice gets lost. A hundred voices demand to be heard. Use the tools at our disposal — social media, community groups, college networks — to build coalitions around the issues that matter to us.

3. **Run.** Not for Prime Minister tomorrow. Run for the student council. Run for the youth advisory board. Run for the local ward committee. The experience you gain today is the credibility you'll have tomorrow.

4. **Use data.** We are the most informed generation in history. Use research, use case studies, use successful models from other cities and countries. Nobody can argue with results.

5. **Partner, don't protest.** Storming town halls gets attention. Sitting across the table from decision-makers gets results. Learn the language of policy, budgets, and bylaws. Speak in terms of impact and cost-benefit. That's how you win.

## Closing

Democracy is not a spectator sport. It is not something that happens to us — it is something we build, every single day. The youth of today are not just leaders of tomorrow. We are citizens of today. And citizenship without participation is just a piece of paper.

Go to that meeting. Ask that question. File that petition. Run for that seat. The future doesn't belong to the old or the young. It belongs to those who show up to build it.

Thank you.
